Italy through the seasons, month by month

Blossom and Easter processions, beach summers and Ferragosto, harvest and truffle autumns, ski slopes and Carnival: how to pick the right time for your trip.

There is no bad time to visit Italy, only the wrong time for a particular trip. Rome in August is hot and half-shuttered; the Dolomites in August are perfect. Venice in November can flood, but it is also hauntingly quiet. Sicily in February is mild enough for lunch outdoors while the Alps are deep in snow. And because Italy runs on its food calendar, the season also decides what is on your plate: artichokes in spring, tomatoes in summer, truffles and new oil in autumn.

For most first trips, the sweet spots are mid-April to early June and September to mid-October: warm days, long evenings and fewer queues. Below we explain each season, then go month by month through the weather, what is on and how busy it gets, plus a few regional climate notes and the public holidays that close shops and fill trains.

The four seasons

Spring

March to May

Best for: cities, hiking, gardens, Easter traditions

The country turns green and the south warms up first: almond blossom in Sicily in February and March, wisteria and orange blossom in April, wild flowers across the Apennine plains by late May and June. Easter brings moving Holy Week processions, especially in Sicily, Puglia and Procida, but also Italian crowds. Days are often 18 to 24°C by May, perfect for cities and walking. Pack a layer.

Summer and Ferragosto

June to August

Best for: beaches, islands, the Alps and Dolomites, festivals

Long, bright days, warm seas and outdoor everything: opera in Verona's Roman arena, jazz in Perugia, village food festivals (sagre) every weekend. It is also hot, often 32 to 38°C in the cities and the south, and busy. Around Ferragosto on 15 August, Italians take their own holidays: cities empty, many family restaurants close, and beaches and mountains are packed. Book coast and island stays months ahead.

Autumn

September to November

Best for: food and wine, cities, walking, quieter coasts

Many people's favourite season. September seas are at their warmest and the grape harvest begins; October brings chestnuts, porcini, golden larches in the Alps and the white truffle fair in Alba. By November it is olive harvest time, and the new oil, peppery and bright green, is poured over toasted bread in every Tuscan and Umbrian trattoria. Rain increases from late October, and coastal resorts close.

Winter

December to February

Best for: skiing, Christmas markets, Carnival, crowd-free museums

The Alps and Dolomites become ski country from December to early April, and the Christmas markets of South Tyrol and Trentino glow from late November. Cities are quiet and cheaper outside the holidays, with mild sunshine in Rome, Naples and Sicily. Then comes Carnevale in February: masks in Venice, satirical floats in Viareggio and the orange battle in Ivrea. Expect fog in the Po valley and short days.

Month by month

Temperatures are typical daytime highs for Rome; subtract a few degrees for the north and add a few for Sicily.

January

January

Weather: cold and often crisp, 12 to 14°C in Rome, snow in the mountains, fog in the Po valley, mild in Sicily. What's on: the Befana on 6 January, winter sales from early January, peak ski season. Crowds: the quietest month in the cities, with the lowest prices outside ski resorts.

February

February

Weather: still cold, but the south has almond blossom. What's on: Carnevale, with Venice's masks, Viareggio's papier-mâché floats and Ivrea's orange battle; in 2027 the last day, Martedì Grasso, is 9 February. The almond blossom festival in Agrigento. Crowds: low, except Venice at Carnival and the ski slopes in half-term weeks.

March

March

Weather: changeable, 15 to 17°C, sunny spells and showers. What's on: early spring in the south, mimosa for International Women's Day on 8 March, and in 2027 Easter falls on 28 March, with Holy Week processions. Crowds: building slowly; Easter weekend is busy.

April

April

Weather: pleasant, 18 to 20°C, some rain. What's on: Milan Design Week (Salone del Mobile), Vinitaly in Verona, Liberation Day on 25 April, wisteria and gardens. Crowds: busy around holiday weekends and with school groups; book city hotels ahead.

May

May

Weather: warm and settled, 22 to 25°C, the sea warming up in the south. What's on: 1 May holiday, the Corsa dei Ceri in Gubbio on 15 May, the flower carpets of Noto's Infiorata, open-cellar weekend Cantine Aperte, the Giro d'Italia bike race. Crowds: moderate; one of the best months overall.

June

June

Weather: hot, 27 to 30°C, longest days, swimming season begins. What's on: Republic Day on 2 June, Florence's Calcio Storico, flower carpets for Corpus Domini, the Spoleto and Ravenna festivals, opera season opens at the Arena di Verona, mountain huts open mid-month. Crowds: rising steadily, still easier than July.

July

July

Weather: very hot, 31 to 35°C, heatwaves possible. What's on: the Palio di Siena on 2 July, Umbria Jazz in Perugia, Palermo's Santa Rosalia festival, Venice's Redentore fireworks on the third weekend. Crowds: high at the coast and in the cities; the mountains are glorious.

August

August

Weather: hottest month, 32 to 38°C, occasional thunderstorms. What's on: Ferragosto on 15 August, the second Palio di Siena on 16 August, La Notte della Taranta in Salento, the Rossini Opera Festival in Pesaro, the Venice Film Festival from late month. Crowds: peak at beaches and islands, while cities are oddly empty and many shops close.

September

September

Weather: warm, 26 to 28°C, the sea at its warmest. What's on: the grape harvest begins, Venice's Regata Storica on the first Sunday, Arezzo's Saracen Joust, food festivals everywhere. Crowds: busy in the first half, much calmer after mid-month. A favourite month.

October

October

Weather: mild, 21 to 23°C, more rain later in the month. What's on: the Alba White Truffle Fair (to early December), Eurochocolate in Perugia, Trieste's Barcolana regatta, chestnut and mushroom festivals, golden larches in the Alps, and since 2026 a national holiday for St Francis on 4 October. Crowds: moderate; coastal resorts start to close.

November

November

Weather: the wettest month in much of Italy, 15 to 17°C; acqua alta flooding is possible in Venice. What's on: olive harvest and new oil, novello wine, truffles, All Saints on 1 November, Venice's Festa della Salute on 21 November. Crowds: low, with bargain city breaks but many seaside hotels closed.

December

December

Weather: cool, 12 to 14°C in Rome, snow in the Alps. What's on: Christmas markets in Bolzano, Merano, Bressanone and Trento, nativity workshops on Naples' Via San Gregorio Armeno, La Scala's season opening on 7 December, ski season starts. Crowds: quiet until mid-month, then busy and pricey from Christmas to 6 January.

Regional climate notes

Alps and Dolomites

Cool summers, perfect for hiking from mid-June to late September, when most lifts and huts are open. Ski season runs from December to early April. Mid-October to November and May are quiet shoulder months, when many hotels close.

Trentino-Alto Adige guide

The northern lakes

Como, Garda, Maggiore and Orta have a mild microclimate with lemons and palms, but ferries reduce and many hotels close from November to March. April to June and September are ideal.

Lombardy guide

Po valley cities

Milan, Turin, Bologna, Verona and Venice have hot, humid summers with mosquitoes, and cold, foggy winters. Spring and autumn are best; Venice can flood between October and January.

Emilia-Romagna guide

Liguria

Sheltered by mountains, the Riviera is mild in winter and good for walking from March. Summer is busy; heavy autumn rain can close the Cinque Terre paths.

Liguria guide

Central Italy

Tuscany, Umbria, Marche and Lazio have hot, dry summers and mild, damp winters. The hill towns are cooler than Rome and Florence in summer. April to June and September to October are perfect.

Tuscany guide

Apennine interior

Abruzzo, Molise and inland Basilicata have real winters with snow and ski resorts, and fresh summers when the coast is sweltering.

Abruzzo guide

The south

Naples, Puglia, Calabria and the Amalfi Coast are swimmable from late May to mid-October. July and August are very hot; spring and autumn are lovely, and winters are mild but can be wet.

Campania guide

Sicily and Sardinia

The longest summers in Italy, often 35°C or more inland, with sea swimming from June to October. Winters are mild on the coasts, though Etna and Sardinia's Gennargentu mountains get snow.

Sicily guide

Public holidays

On national holidays, offices, many shops and some museums close, and trains and roads fill up. Each city also has a patron saint's day when it shuts down to celebrate.

New Year's Day (Capodanno)

1 January

Late mornings after big New Year's Eve parties and fireworks.

Epiphany (Epifania)

6 January

The Befana, a kindly witch, brings children sweets. Ends the Christmas season.

Easter Sunday and Monday (Pasqua, Pasquetta)

28-29 March 2027

Easter Monday is traditionally a day for picnics and countryside trips, so roads are busy.

Liberation Day (Festa della Liberazione)

25 April

Marks the end of fascism and German occupation in 1945. Also St Mark's Day in Venice.

Labour Day (Festa dei Lavoratori)

1 May

Big free concert in Rome; many museums are closed.

Republic Day (Festa della Repubblica)

2 June

Military parade in Rome and the Frecce Tricolori air display.

Ferragosto (Assumption)

15 August

The height of the summer holidays: cities empty, beaches overflow, fireworks on the coast.

St Francis of Assisi (San Francesco)

4 October

Restored as a national holiday from 2026 for Italy's patron saint. In 2027 it falls on a Monday.

All Saints (Ognissanti)

1 November

Families visit cemeteries; a long weekend for many.

Immaculate Conception (Immacolata)

8 December

The unofficial start of Christmas, when trees go up and markets get busy.

Christmas Day and St Stephen's Day (Natale, Santo Stefano)

25-26 December

Family days. Almost everything closes on the 25th; book restaurants for lunch well ahead.

Patron saints' days

Local

For example St John on 24 June in Florence, Genoa and Turin; Sts Peter and Paul on 29 June in Rome; Santa Rosalia on 15 July in Palermo; San Gennaro on 19 September in Naples; Sant'Ambrogio on 7 December in Milan.

Good to know

  • Book July and August coast and island stays, and the Dolomites in August, several months ahead.
  • Avoid Italian holiday weekends (ponti) for driving and last-minute train bookings.
  • Many museums close on Mondays and on 1 January, 1 May and 25 December.
  • In August, check that the restaurants and shops you are counting on are not closed per ferie (for holidays).
  • Bring a light scarf or layer in any season: churches require covered shoulders, and evenings cool quickly away from the coast.
