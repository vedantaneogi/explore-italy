Twenty regions, one long table

Plan a trip through Italy's art, food, music and landscapes, region by region, the way Italians travel it themselves.

Manarola's painted houses stacked on a cliff above its tiny harbour, Cinque TerrePhoto: Timothy A. Gonsalves, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Italy isn't one country to tick off. It's twenty, each with its own dialect, its own bread and its own idea of the perfect evening.

Start with a region and you'll find the towns worth your days, what to order, the family tables that have been open for a century and the festivals worth timing a trip around. Follow an itinerary if you want the route worked out, or wander through the experiences, from opera houses to cheese dairies.

Everything here is information, not a sales pitch. Where you need to book a train, a museum slot or a farm stay, we link straight to the official source.

Hidden gems

Villages, islands and quiet cities that most visitors drive past.

See all hidden gems

Civita di Bagnoregio

Lazio, between Orvieto and Lake Bolsena

Why: a town on a vanishing rock · When: early morning, or misty October days

A medieval village on a pinnacle of crumbling tufa, reached only by a long, steep footbridge across the badlands of the Valle dei Calanchi. A handful of people still live here. There is a small entry fee; pay it, then linger in the lanes, the cellar wine bars and the belvedere over the clay gullies.

Lazio guide

Santo Stefano di Sessanio

Abruzzo, Gran Sasso National Park

Why: mountain silence and wool-trade stone · When: June to October

A fortified shepherds' village at about 1,250 metres, built by the wool trade and rescued from abandonment by careful restoration. Eat the tiny local lentils, walk up to the hilltop castle of Rocca Calascio, and drive across the vast Campo Imperatore plateau, nicknamed Italy's little Tibet.

Abruzzo guide

Craco

Basilicata, an hour from Matera

Why: Italy's most striking ghost town · When: spring or autumn, late afternoon light

A hilltop town emptied by landslides in the 1960s and left exactly as it was, its towers and palazzi slowly settling into the clay. You can visit only on a guided tour with a helmet, which makes it feel even more haunting. Film crews love it; you will see why.

Basilicata guide

Bosa

Sardinia, west coast

Why: colour, river life and sweet Malvasia · When: May to June, September

A sweep of pastel houses climbing from the River Temo to the Malaspina castle, with old tanneries along the water and a beach at Bosa Marina. Taste the amber Malvasia di Bosa, then drive the cliff road north towards Alghero, one of the island's loveliest.

Sardinia guide

Castelmezzano

Basilicata, Lucanian Dolomites

Why: village-on-the-rocks drama · When: May to September (zip line is seasonal)

Houses wedged between jagged sandstone spires, facing its twin village Pietrapertosa across the valley. Walk the Path of the Seven Stones between them, climb the via ferrata, or, if you dare, fly across on the Volo dell'Angelo, a steel-cable zip line high above the gorge.

Basilicata guideVolo dell'Angelo

Procida

Campania, Bay of Naples

Why: real island life near Naples · When: May to June, September to October

The smallest island in the bay and the least polished, which is the point. Marina Corricella's ice-cream-coloured fishermen's houses, the fortified old town of Terra Murata, lemon gardens and a few small beaches. Ferries and hydrofoils from Naples take 40 to 60 minutes.

The year in festivals

Palios, processions, harvests and music nights. Time your trip around one.

Full festival calendar

Sant'Antonio Abate and the Mamuthones

Mamoiada, Sardinia

16-17 January

Bonfires burn across inland Sardinia, and in Mamoiada the Mamuthones make their first outing of the year: men in black sheepskins and dark wooden masks, shaking heavy cowbells in a slow, eerie march.

Sardinia guide

Festa di Sant'Agata

Catania, Sicily

3-5 February

One of the largest religious festivals in the world. Devotees in white robes haul the saint's silver carriage through the city for two days and nights, with fireworks, giant candles and torrone stalls.

Sicily guide

Carnevale di Venezia

Venice, Veneto

Late January to Shrove Tuesday

Masks, costumed balls and the Flight of the Angel from the Campanile in St Mark's Square. The best moments are early morning, when costumed Venetians pose in empty campi. In 2027 it ends on 9 February.

Veneto guideOfficial programme

Carnevale di Viareggio

Viareggio, Tuscany

February weekends

Giant papier-mache floats, some as tall as a building, satirise politicians and celebrities as they roll along the seafront promenade over several weekends.

Tuscany guide

Battle of the Oranges

Ivrea, Piedmont

Carnival Sunday to Shrove Tuesday

For three days, teams on foot pelt armoured carts with oranges in a mock revolt against a medieval tyrant. Spectators who wear the red Phrygian cap are off-limits; everyone else is fair game.

Piedmont guideStorico Carnevale di Ivrea

Processione dei Misteri

Trapani, Sicily

Good Friday

Twenty groups of carved wooden statues of the Passion are carried through the old town, swaying to brass bands, for around 24 hours from Good Friday afternoon.

Sicily guide
