Itineraries  /  7 days

Tortellini, Parmigiano, balsamic and Barolo

A week eating your way along the Via Emilia, then into Piedmont's Langhe hills for Barolo and white truffles.

The endless arcade of the San Luca portico climbing out of BolognaPhoto: Serenapaliria, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Italy's food valley

Some of Italy's best-known foods come from a single stretch of flat land along the old Roman Via Emilia: mortadella and tortellini in Bologna, aged balsamic vinegar in Modena, Parmigiano Reggiano and Parma ham around Parma. This trip spends five days there, visiting a cheese dairy at dawn, an attic of balsamic barrels and the markets and trattorie where locals eat. It then crosses to Piedmont's Langhe, a UNESCO wine landscape of Nebbiolo vineyards, hilltop castles and, from October, white truffles. Trains link the Emilian cities in under an hour; hire a car for the countryside days.

  1. Bologna
  2. Modena
  3. Parma
  4. Alba
  5. Barolo
  6. La Morra
  7. Bra
Length
7 days
Best season
September to November: grape harvest, porcini, and Alba's white truffle fair (10 October to 6 December in 2026). Spring is also good, with Parmigiano dairies working at full tilt and green vineyards. August is hot and many family restaurants close.
Getting around
Regional and fast trains link Bologna to Modena (about 25 minutes) and Parma (about 1h). Dairy and acetaia visits are outside the towns, so book a taxi or hire a car for those mornings. Parma to Alba is about 2h45 by car, or 3h30-4h by train via Milan or Turin. In the Langhe you need a car or a driver, especially if you plan to taste wine.
Budget
Allow €160-250 per person per day mid-range; tastings, a truffle dinner or a dairy tour each add €20-100.

Day by day

Swap days around freely; the order follows the most efficient route.

Day1

Bologna's Quadrilatero

Base: Bologna

Start in Piazza Maggiore and walk into the Quadrilatero, the medieval market streets behind it, where delis hang with mortadella and prosciutto. Buy salumi, cheese and bread, and carry them into Osteria del Sole, a wine bar trading since 1465 where you bring your own food and buy only the drinks. In the afternoon see the Archiginnasio's anatomical theatre and walk under the city's UNESCO-listed porticoes, which run for roughly 40 kilometres in the old centre alone.

  • The Quadrilatero market streets
  • Osteria del Sole
  • Archiginnasio anatomical theatre
Eat
Trattoria Anna Maria on Via Belle Arti for tortellini in brodo, the benchmark version in a city of tortellini. Book a few days ahead.
Stay
Within the old city walls, near Piazza Maggiore or the university quarter.

Day2

Pasta and the San Luca portico

Base: Bologna

Book a morning class with a sfoglina, one of Bologna's professional pasta makers, and learn to roll a sheet of egg pasta by hand and fold tortellini. In the afternoon walk the 666 arches of the San Luca portico, nearly 4 kilometres and uphill all the way, to the sanctuary overlooking the Apennines. Return for an aperitivo of Pignoletto sparkling wine and crescentine (fried dough puffs) with salumi.

  • Hand-rolled pasta class
  • Portico di San Luca walk
  • Crescentine and Pignoletto
Eat
Tagliatelle al ragù, the real thing that inspired spaghetti bolognese, at any old-town trattoria.
Stay
Same base in Bologna.

Day3

Modena: balsamic vinegar

Base: Bologna or Modena

Take the train to Modena (about 25 minutes). Taxi to Acetaia Giusti, a family producer of balsamic vinegar since 1605, for a free guided tour of its attic barrels and a tasting of vinegars aged up to decades (book ahead). Back in town, browse the Mercato Albinelli covered market and see the Romanesque Duomo and Ghirlandina tower, a UNESCO site. If you are a car fan, the Ferrari museum in Maranello is 30 minutes away.

  • Acetaia Giusti tour and tasting
  • Mercato Albinelli
  • Modena's Duomo and Ghirlandina
Eat
Trattoria Aldina, up a staircase opposite the Mercato Albinelli: a lunch-only, no-bookings dining room for tortellini, bollito and home cooking since 1973.
Stay
Bologna, or Modena's centre if you want a quieter night.

Day4

Parma: Parmigiano at dawn

Base: Parma

Take an early train to Parma (about 1h from Bologna) and a taxi to a Parmigiano Reggiano dairy for 8am, when cheesemakers lift curds from the copper vats with linen cloths. Book through the consortium's dairy list or directly with a caseificio. In the afternoon see Correggio's dome fresco in the Duomo and the pink marble Baptistery, then walk across the river to the Oltretorrente district for dinner.

  • Parmigiano Reggiano dairy visit
  • Correggio's Assumption in the Duomo
  • Parma Baptistery
Eat
Anolini in brodo (small stuffed pasta in broth), torta fritta with Prosciutto di Parma, and a glass of Lambrusco.
Stay
Parma's centro storico.

Day5

Prosciutto hills, then on to the Langhe

Base: Alba

Pick up a hire car and drive 30 minutes south to Langhirano for a tour of a Prosciutto di Parma curing house, where hams hang in tall rooms with windows opened to the valley breeze. Then drive about 2h45 west to Alba, a market town of red-brick towers. In season, walk through the truffle market and smell the white truffles in the Cortile della Maddalena before dinner.

  • Prosciutto di Parma curing house
  • Alba's towers and old town
  • White truffle market (October to early December)
Eat
Osteria dell'Arco on Piazza Michele Ferrero in Alba, run by the team behind Bra's Boccondivino, for tajarin (fine egg pasta) with butter and, in season, shaved white truffle.
Stay
Alba's old town, or a vineyard agriturismo just outside.

Day6

Barolo country

Base: Alba

Drive the loop of Barolo villages, each on its own hill. Start at the belvedere in La Morra for the view over the vineyards, visit the WiMu wine museum in Barolo castle, and see the fortress of Serralunga d'Alba. Book two winery visits, one large and one small family cellar, and appoint a driver or share driving between tastings. Hazelnuts from these hills go into the region's gianduja chocolate; look for torta di nocciole.

  • La Morra belvedere
  • WiMu wine museum in Barolo
  • Serralunga d'Alba castle
Eat
Vitello tonnato, carne cruda all'albese (hand-chopped raw veal) and a plate of agnolotti del plin.
Stay
Same base in Alba.

Day7

Bra, Pollenzo and home

Base: Bra

Drive 20 minutes to Bra, where the Slow Food movement began in the 1980s. Visit the Banca del Vino wine library at the University of Gastronomic Sciences in nearby Pollenzo, then have a long final lunch in Bra. Afterwards, drop the car in Turin (about 1h) or at an airport. Bra's old centre has baroque churches and a lively Friday market, and the town's butchers still make its raw veal sausage to a recipe once granted by royal permission. Buy hazelnuts, cheese and a bottle of Barolo to take home.

  • Bra, home of Slow Food
  • Pollenzo's Banca del Vino
  • Salsiccia di Bra
Eat
Osteria del Boccondivino in Bra, the original Slow Food osteria, for raw salsiccia di Bra, tajarin and bonet (chocolate and amaretti pudding). Book; it fills even at lunch.
Stay
Depart via Turin or Milan.

Before you go

  • Parmigiano dairies work from early morning; visits usually start at 8am and production is over by late morning.
  • Many Emilian trattorie close on Sunday evening and Monday; plan your big meals for Tuesday to Saturday.
  • In the Langhe, book cellar visits a week or more ahead in autumn, and do not drive after tasting.
  • Alba's truffle prices are set daily and posted at the market; buy from licensed sellers inside the fair.
  • Balsamic labelled 'Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale DOP' is aged at least 12 years and comes in a standard small bottle; anything else is the everyday version.
