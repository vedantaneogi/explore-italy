Where Italy has eaten for generations

Grand cafés, century-old trattorie, Neapolitan pizzerias, pastry shops and wine bars that have fed Italians for generations, and still open their doors every morning.

A mirrored and gilded room inside Caffè Florian in VenicePhoto: Jorge Franganillo, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

Some of the best meals in Italy come with a history lesson on the side. Casanova drank in a Venetian wine cellar that still pours from the same counter; Cavour plotted unification over hot chocolate in Turin; the margherita was named in a Neapolitan pizzeria that still fires the same kind of oven. These places have outlived kingdoms, wars and fashions because they did one thing well and kept doing it.

Every address here is still open and serving, verified for 2026, with the year its story began. Many belong to the Locali Storici d'Italia association, which has championed historic cafés and restaurants since the 1970s. Go for breakfast at the counter rather than an expensive table, order the house speciality, and look up at the ceilings: some of Italy's finest interiors come with a cappuccino.

The grand cafés

Mirrors, marble and two centuries of conversation. These are the most famous, and they're worth the table price at least once.

Caffè Florian

Venice, Veneto

Since 1720

Opened under the arcades of Piazza San Marco in 1720 as 'Alla Venezia Trionfante', Florian is Italy's oldest café still in business. Casanova, Goethe, Byron and Dickens all took a table in its little mirrored and frescoed rooms. Order a hot chocolate in winter, and in summer listen to the orchestra playing on the square.

Veneto guideCaffè Florian

Caffè Al Bicerin

Turin, Piedmont

Since 1763

A tiny room of wood panelling, mirrors and eight marble tables, facing the Consolata sanctuary since 1763. Its namesake drink layers chocolate, coffee and cream in a small glass. Cavour came here while waiting for the royal family to finish Mass; you'll wait for a table on Sunday mornings.

Piedmont guideCaffè Al Bicerin

Caffè Pedrocchi

Padua, Veneto

Since 1831

A neoclassical temple to coffee opened by Antonio Pedrocchi in 1831, with red, white and green salons. It stayed open day and night for decades, earning the name 'the café without doors', and Padua's students rose against Austrian rule here in 1848. Order the minty caffè Pedrocchi and don't stir it.

Veneto guideCaffè Pedrocchi

Gran Caffè Gambrinus

Naples, Campania

Since 1860

Naples' grand salon on Piazza del Plebiscito, opened in 1860 and dressed in Belle Époque stucco and paintings. D'Annunzio and Oscar Wilde were regulars. Stand at the bar for a scalding espresso and a sfogliatella, and leave a caffè sospeso, a prepaid coffee for the next person who can't afford one.

Campania guide

Caffè degli Specchi

Trieste, Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Since 1839

Opened in 1839 on Piazza Unità d'Italia, the vast square that opens straight onto the Adriatic. The 'café of mirrors' has watched Trieste pass from Habsburg port to Italian city. Order a 'capo in b' in local coffee code and sit outside facing the sea.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia guideCaffè degli Specchi

More historic cafés

Caffè Gilli

Florence, Tuscany

Since 1733

Started by a Swiss family in 1733 as a sweet bakery near the Duomo, Gilli moved to Piazza della Repubblica in the early twentieth century. Mirrors, chocolates in glass cabinets and the city's classic terrace for an aperitivo.

Tuscany guideOfficial site

Antico Caffè San Marco

Trieste, Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Since 1914

A Viennese-style coffee house of 1914, with dark wood, mask-painted walls and a bookshop inside. Writers still work at its marble tables; Claudio Magris made it famous in his essays.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia guideOfficial site

Baratti & Milano

Turin, Piedmont

Since 1858

Confectioners since 1858 and in the glass-roofed Galleria Subalpina since 1875. Buy gianduiotti from the carved wooden counters, then take a vermouth in the gilded café.

Piedmont guideOfficial site

Caffè Mulassano

Turin, Piedmont

Since 1907

A jewel box of marble, mirrors and coffered ceiling on Piazza Castello, open since 1907. A plaque inside says the tramezzino, Italy's crustless sandwich, was invented here in 1926.

Piedmont guideOfficial site

Caffè Rivoire

Florence, Tuscany

Since 1872

Founded in 1872 by a chocolatier to the House of Savoy, facing the Palazzo Vecchio on Piazza della Signoria. Its thick hot chocolate, with or without cream, is the reason to come.

Tuscany guideOfficial site

Sant'Eustachio Il Caffè

Rome, Lazio

Since 1938

Since 1938 a few steps from the Pantheon, still roasting its beans over wood. The famous 'gran caffè' comes pre-sweetened with a dense crema made behind a screen: ask for it 'amaro' if you don't take sugar.

Lazio guide

Caffè Meletti

Ascoli Piceno, Marche

Since 1907

An Art Nouveau café on one of Italy's loveliest squares, Piazza del Popolo, open since 1907. Order the house anisetta 'con la mosca', with a few coffee beans floating in it.

Marche guideOfficial site

Antico Caffè Spinnato

Palermo, Sicily

Since 1860

On pedestrian Via Principe di Belmonte since 1860, Spinnato is where Palermitans go for granita and brioche in summer, and for cassata and cannoli all year round.

Sicily guideOfficial site

Antico Caffè

Cagliari, Sardinia

Since 1855

Cagliari's historic café on Piazza Costituzione, below the Bastione di Saint Remy. Its story begins in 1838 and its name in 1855, when it became the Caffè Genovese; the panelled rooms still keep that era's charm.

Sardinia guideOfficial site

Trattorie, osterie and restaurants

Tablecloths, old waiters and the dishes the house has been making for a century or more.

Del Cambio

Turin, Piedmont

Since 1757

Turin's grandest dining room, opposite the Palazzo Carignano where Italy's first parliament met. Red velvet, crystal and a plaque at Cavour's table. Today it's a Michelin-starred restaurant with a more casual bistro and bar alongside.

Piedmont guideOfficial site

Antica Trattoria della Pesa

Milan, Lombardy

Since 1880

A wood-panelled trattoria near Porta Garibaldi serving Milan's classics since 1880: saffron risotto, ossobuco with gremolata and a cotoletta alla milanese pounded thin and fried in butter.

Lombardy guideOfficial site

Hosteria Giusti

Modena, Emilia-Romagna

Since 1605

The Giusti deli has sold salumi on the same street since 1605, often called the oldest in the world. In 1989 the old curing room behind it became a tiny hosteria of a few tables; book weeks ahead for tortellini, cotechino and aged balsamic.

Emilia-Romagna guideOfficial site

Trattoria Sostanza

Florence, Tuscany

Since 1869

A narrow room of shared marble tables, white tiles and old photographs, cooking since 1869. Order the butter chicken, sizzling in its own pan, and the artichoke tortino. Book ahead and bring cash.

Tuscany guide

Buca Lapi

Florence, Tuscany

Since 1880

In the vaulted cellars of Palazzo Antinori since 1880, under walls papered with old travel posters. Come hungry for a bistecca alla fiorentina sold by weight.

Tuscany guideOfficial site

La Campana

Rome, Lazio

Since c. 1518

Often called Rome's oldest restaurant, on the lane near Piazza Navona that took its name, with records going back to the early sixteenth century. Waiters in white jackets bring carciofi alla romana, vignarola in spring and coda alla vaccinara.

Lazio guideTurismo Roma

Checchino dal 1887

Rome, Lazio

Since 1887

Facing Testaccio's old slaughterhouse, the Mariani family have cooked the quinto quarto, Rome's offal tradition, since 1887. The wine cellar is dug into Monte Testaccio, an ancient hill of broken Roman amphorae.

Lazio guideOfficial site

Antica Focacceria San Francesco

Palermo, Sicily

Since 1834

Facing the church of San Francesco d'Assisi since 1834, with marble tables and a cast-iron stove. Eat pani ca' meusa, arancine and panelle; the owners famously refused to pay mafia protection money.

Sicily guideOfficial site

Pizzerias of Naples

The four addresses that tell pizza's story. Expect queues, paper tickets and the best fifteen minutes of your trip.

Antica Pizzeria Port'Alba

Naples, Campania

Since 1738

Under the old city gate of Port'Alba, among the booksellers, this began in 1738 as a stand supplying street vendors with marinara pizzas to sell around town. Often called the world's oldest pizzeria.

Campania guideOfficial site

Pizzeria Brandi

Naples, Campania

Since 1760

Founded in 1760, this is where pizzaiolo Raffaele Esposito made a pizza in the colours of the flag, tomato, mozzarella and basil, for Queen Margherita in 1889. A plaque by the door marks the birth of the margherita.

Campania guideOfficial site

L'Antica Pizzeria da Michele

Naples, Campania

Since 1870

Since 1870 the Condurro family have served just two pizzas, marinara and margherita, on marble tables near Forcella. Take a number at the door; the wait is part of the ritual.

Campania guideOfficial site

Pizzeria Starita a Materdei

Naples, Campania

Since 1901

A neighbourhood institution since 1901, where Sophia Loren sold fried pizza in the 1954 film 'L'oro di Napoli'. Try the pizza fritta, then a classic from the wood oven.

Campania guideOfficial site

Pastry shops and confectioners

Sweet shops that have kept their recipes, and often their counters, for generations.

Pasticceria Cova

Milan, Lombardy

Since 1817

Founded near La Scala in 1817 and now on Via Montenapoleone. Elegant cakes, panettone at Christmas and a stand-up coffee among Milan's best-dressed shoppers.

Lombardy guideOfficial site

Pietro Romanengo fu Stefano

Genoa, Liguria

Since 1780

Genoa's confectioners since 1780, making candied fruit, sugared almonds, rose syrup and violet sweets by hand, sold in a nineteenth-century shop in Via Soziglia.

Liguria guideOfficial site

Klainguti

Genoa, Liguria

Since 1828

Opened by Swiss pastry makers in 1828 on Piazza Soziglia. Try the 'Falstaff' brioche, which Giuseppe Verdi is said to have praised as better than his own opera.

Liguria guideOfficial site

Pasticceria Sandri

Perugia, Umbria

Since 1860

On Perugia's main street, Corso Vannucci, since 1860, with a frescoed vaulted ceiling, walnut cabinets and waiters in bow ties. Buy torcolo or seasonal sweets.

Umbria guideOfficial site

Confetti Pelino

Sulmona, Abruzzo

Since 1783

Sulmona is Italy's capital of confetti, the sugared almonds given at weddings, and the Pelino family has made them since 1783. Visit the factory museum, then buy them shaped into flowers.

Abruzzo guideOfficial site

Pasticceria Regoli

Rome, Lazio

Since 1916

A family pastry shop near Piazza Vittorio since 1916, the benchmark for maritozzi, soft buns split and filled with whipped cream to order. On weekends they're gone by late morning.

Lazio guide

Pintauro

Naples, Campania

Since 1785

The name most linked with the sfogliatella, on Via Toledo since 1785. After a long closure it reopened in spring 2026 with its historic interior restored; order a riccia, still warm.

Campania guide

Gay-Odin

Naples, Campania

Since 1894

Naples' chocolate makers since 1894. The Via Toledo shop keeps its Liberty-style furnishings; buy the 'Foresta', chocolate shaped like a gnarled tree branch.

Campania guideOfficial site

Pasticceria Ascalone

Galatina, Puglia

Since 1745 (by tradition)

Family tradition says the pasticciotto, Salento's custard-filled shortcrust pastry, was born in this shop in 1745. Whatever the date, eat one warm for breakfast.

Puglia guide

Caffè Sicilia

Noto, Sicily

Since 1892

On Noto's Baroque main street since 1892, famous for almond granita, cassata and honey-and-citrus creations by pastry chef Corrado Assenza.

Sicily guideOfficial site

Café Konditorei König

Merano, Trentino-Alto Adige

Since 1893

Founded in 1893 by a pastry chef trained at Vienna's court confectioners, and run by the König family ever since. Viennese cakes and house-made pralines in a spa town that still feels Habsburg.

Trentino-Alto Adige guide

Wine bars, bacari and buffets

The oldest places of all are the ones that mostly served wine.

Al Brindisi

Ferrara, Emilia-Romagna

Since 1435

Behind Ferrara cathedral, documented as a tavern in 1435 and often called the oldest osteria in the world. Legend says Copernicus lodged here as a student. Sit under the hams with a glass of local wine and cappellacci di zucca.

Emilia-Romagna guideOfficial site

Cantina Do Mori

Venice, Veneto

Since 1462

Venice's oldest bacaro, in business near the Rialto since 1462, with copper pots hanging from dark beams. Stand at the counter for an ombra of wine and 'francobolli', stamp-sized sandwiches. Cash only.

Veneto guide

Osteria del Sole

Bologna, Emilia-Romagna

Since 1465

A wine-only osteria in the Quadrilatero since 1465. It sells nothing but wine: bring your own mortadella, cheese and bread from the market stalls outside, as Bolognese have done for centuries.

Emilia-Romagna guideOfficial site

Buffet da Pepi

Trieste, Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Since 1897

Trieste's classic 'buffet' since 1897, where pork of every cut simmers in a steaming caldaia. Point at what you want and have it carved with mustard, freshly grated horseradish and sauerkraut, with a beer.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia guide

Good to know

  • Many historic cafés charge much more at a table than at the counter; for the experience without the bill, drink standing at the bar.
  • The famous pizzerias of Naples don't take bookings: go at opening time, around noon or 7pm, or expect to queue.
  • Old trattorie often close one or two days a week and for part of August; check before you travel across town.
  • Small historic places often prefer cash, so carry some euros.
  • Look for the Locali Storici d'Italia plaque by the door: it's a reliable sign of a place with real history.
