The best day trips from Italy's big cities

Ruins, lakes, hill towns and islands you can reach by train, bus or boat and be back in time for dinner.

Italy's rail network makes it easy to base yourself in one city and see a lot more. Regional trains are cheap, frequent and need no booking, and many of the country's most memorable places, from Pompeii to Lake Como, are an hour or less from a major station. A day trip also lets you see somewhere small at its best: arrive after the early rush, eat a long lunch, and leave before the evening light is gone.

We have grouped the trips below by base city, with honest journey times and the simplest way to get there. A few need advance tickets, notably Pompeii, Padua's Scrovegni Chapel and Pisa's Leaning Tower, so check the links before you go. Everything else you can decide on the morning, which is half the fun.

From Rome

Rome's suburban trains and Cotral buses reach Renaissance gardens, an ancient port and two cliff-top towns. Leave from Termini, Tiburtina or Piramide.

Tivoli

Villa d'Este and Hadrian's Villa

About 1h by regional train from Tiburtina or Cotral bus from Ponte Mammolo

Two UNESCO sites in one day: the terraced Renaissance gardens of Villa d'Este, with hundreds of fountains, and the sprawling ruins of Emperor Hadrian's country palace on the plain below. See the villa in the morning and the fountains in the afternoon light.

Lazio guide

Ostia Antica

Rome's ancient harbour city

About 30 minutes by the Roma-Lido train from Porta San Paolo (Piramide)

A whole Roman town of streets, apartment blocks, taverns, baths and a theatre, often quieter than Pompeii and set among umbrella pines. Allow three hours and bring a picnic; the mosaic floors of the Piazzale delle Corporazioni are the highlight.

Lazio guide

Orvieto

A cathedral on a volcanic cliff

About 1h15 by regional train from Termini, then the funicular

Orvieto sits on a plug of volcanic tuff above the Umbrian countryside. Ride the funicular up from the station to see the cathedral's glittering facade and Signorelli's Last Judgement frescoes, then tour the caves beneath the town and drink a glass of Orvieto Classico.

Umbria guideCathedral tickets

Civita di Bagnoregio

The village on a crumbling hill

About 2h: train to Orvieto, then a Cotral bus to Bagnoregio

A tiny medieval village on an eroding pinnacle of clay, reached only by a long, steep footbridge. Go late in the afternoon when the day groups have gone. Combine it with Orvieto: a local bus links the two towns on weekdays.

Lazio guideVisitor information

From Florence

Tuscany's best-known towns are within easy reach of Santa Maria Novella station. For Siena and San Gimignano, the bus is often quicker than the train.

Siena

Gothic city of the Palio

About 1h15 by Autolinee Toscane rapid bus, or 1h30 by train

Florence's old rival is a red-brick city built around the shell-shaped Piazza del Campo. Climb the Torre del Mangia, see the striped cathedral and its inlaid marble floor, and get lost in the lanes of the seventeen contrade, each with its own flag and fountain.

Tuscany guideCathedral tickets

Lucca

A city you walk around on its walls

About 1h20 by regional train

Lucca's Renaissance walls are intact and topped with a tree-lined promenade four kilometres long; rent a bike and ride the full circuit. Inside are oval Piazza dell'Anfiteatro, built over a Roman arena, the Guinigi Tower with oak trees on its roof, and Puccini's birthplace.

Tuscany guideLucca tourism

Pisa

More than the Leaning Tower

About 1h by regional train

The Piazza dei Miracoli is genuinely astonishing: cathedral, baptistery and tower in white marble on a green lawn. Book a timed slot to climb the tower, then walk to the Arno, where the university city has good-value trattorie and a lively evening scene.

Tuscany guideTower tickets

San Gimignano

Medieval skyline of towers

About 1h45 by train or bus to Poggibonsi, then a local bus

Fourteen stone towers survive from the 72 that rich families once built to show off. Arrive early or stay late to see it without the crowds, climb the Torre Grossa, and taste Vernaccia, the local white wine, in the vineyards just outside the walls.

Tuscany guideVisitor information

From Venice

Vaporetti reach the outer lagoon islands, and the mainland cities of the Veneto are a short train ride from Santa Lucia station.

Burano and Torcello

Painted houses and Byzantine mosaics

About 45 minutes by vaporetto line 12 from Fondamente Nove

Burano's fishermen's houses are painted every colour imaginable along quiet canals; come for the lace, the risotto di gò and the photographs. Five minutes away, nearly abandoned Torcello has a cathedral with a vast golden Last Judgement mosaic.

Veneto guideVaporetto timetables

Padua

Giotto's masterpiece and a great market

About 30 minutes by fast train, 45 by regional

Padua's Scrovegni Chapel is covered in Giotto's frescoes, a turning point in Western art; visits are timed and short, so book ahead. Then see the Basilica of St Anthony and the market squares of Piazza delle Erbe and Piazza della Frutta.

Veneto guideBook the Scrovegni Chapel

Verona

Roman arena and medieval squares

About 1h10 by fast train

A Roman amphitheatre still used for summer opera, Piazza delle Erbe's market, the Scaliger tombs and a hill-top view from Castel San Pietro. Verona is compact enough to see in a day, though an evening at the opera is worth staying for.

Veneto guideOpera tickets

From Milan

Milan sits at the foot of the lakes and the Alps. Trenord regional trains leave from Centrale, Garibaldi and Cadorna stations.

Lake Como

Varenna and Bellagio by ferry

About 1h by Trenord train to Varenna-Esino, then a 15-minute ferry

Take the train to Varenna, then hop on the short ferry to Bellagio, at the point where the lake's two arms meet. Walk the gardens of Villa Melzi, or carry on by boat to Villa del Balbianello on its wooded headland, and come back on the evening ferry with the mountains turning pink.

Lombardy guideFerry timetables

Bergamo

A hill town above a city

About 50 minutes by regional train, then the funicular

Ride the funicular from the modern lower town to Città Alta, the walled upper town with Venetian walls on the UNESCO list. See the Cappella Colleoni and Santa Maria Maggiore, then eat casoncelli (stuffed pasta with butter and sage) and polenta.

Lombardy guideVisit Bergamo

Lake Maggiore

Stresa and the Borromean Islands

About 1h by train from Milano Centrale to Stresa

From Stresa's lakefront, boats run to Isola Bella, an island turned into a baroque palace with terraced gardens and white peacocks, and Isola dei Pescatori, a fishing village with lakeside restaurants. Spring, when the azaleas bloom, is the best time.

Piedmont guide

Turin

Royal palaces and cafés

About 1h by Frecciarossa or Italo

Italy's first capital has arcaded avenues, the world's oldest Egyptian museum outside Cairo, the Mole Antonelliana's cinema museum, and grand cafés where you drink bicerin, a layered coffee, chocolate and cream drink invented here.

Piedmont guideEgyptian Museum tickets

From Naples

The Circumvesuviana train runs to the buried Roman towns, and hydrofoils leave Molo Beverello for the islands all day.

Pompeii

The city Vesuvius buried

About 35-40 minutes by Circumvesuviana to Pompei Scavi

Walk Roman streets with ruts worn by cart wheels, peer into bakeries, bars and frescoed villas, and see the plaster casts of victims. Tickets are named and checked against ID, with a daily cap; go in the morning window and give it at least four hours.

Campania guidePompeii official site

Herculaneum

Smaller, better preserved

About 20 minutes by Circumvesuviana to Ercolano Scavi

Buried deeper than Pompeii, Herculaneum kept its upper floors, wooden beams, mosaics and even food. It takes two to three hours and is never as crowded. Combine it with Pompeii only if you start early.

Campania guideHerculaneum official site

Procida

The bay's most colourful island

About 40 minutes by hydrofoil from Molo Beverello

Smaller and calmer than Capri, Procida is all pastel houses, lemon groves and fishing boats. Walk up to Terra Murata for the view over Marina di Corricella, swim at Chiaiolella and eat lemon salad and fresh fish by the harbour.

Campania guideHydrofoil tickets

Caserta Royal Palace

The Bourbon answer to Versailles

About 35-50 minutes by regional train from Napoli Centrale

The Reggia di Caserta has 1,200 rooms and a park that runs three kilometres in a straight line of fountains and cascades to the English Garden. Rent a bike or take the shuttle up the park and walk back down.

Campania guidePalace tickets

From Bologna

Bologna is the rail hub of the north, and the cities of Emilia-Romagna are mostly under an hour away on cheap regional trains.

Ravenna

Byzantine mosaics

About 1h10 by regional train

Eight early Christian monuments here are UNESCO-listed for their mosaics, which glow in gold, blue and green. See San Vitale with its portraits of Justinian and Theodora, the starry ceiling of the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, and Dante's tomb.

Emilia-Romagna guideMosaic monument tickets

Ferrara

A Renaissance city by bicycle

About 30 minutes by regional or fast train

The Este dukes built a moated castle and an ideal Renaissance city, and Ferrara is still flat, quiet and full of bicycles. Ride the nine kilometres of tree-lined walls, see the Castello Estense and the Palazzo dei Diamanti, and eat cappellacci di zucca (pumpkin pasta).

Emilia-Romagna guideCastello Estense

Modena

Balsamic vinegar and a Romanesque cathedral

About 25 minutes by train

Modena's cathedral and Ghirlandina tower are UNESCO-listed, the Mercato Albinelli is one of the best food markets in the region, and the balsamic acetaie on the edge of town offer tours and tastings. Car fans can add the Ferrari museum in Maranello.

Emilia-Romagna guideCathedral information

From Palermo and Catania

Sicily's trains are slow but scenic, and buses fill the gaps. Palermo has the best city trips; Catania is the gateway to Etna and the southeast.

Monreale

A cathedral lined with gold

About 40 minutes by AMAT bus 389 from Piazza Indipendenza, Palermo

In the hills above Palermo, Monreale's 12th-century cathedral is covered with more than 6,000 square metres of gold mosaics, telling the Bible from Creation onwards. Do not miss the cloister, with 228 carved twin columns, or the view over the Conca d'Oro.

Sicily guide

Cefalù

Beach town with a Norman cathedral

About 1h by regional train from Palermo Centrale

A fishing town under a huge rock, with a sandy beach right next to the old centre. See the cathedral's Christ Pantocrator mosaic, climb La Rocca for the view, and swim before the train back.

Sicily guideTrain times

Mount Etna

Europe's highest active volcano

About 1h by car from Catania, or 2h by the daily AST bus

From Rifugio Sapienza, walk the Silvestri craters, take the cable car to 2,500 metres, and go higher only with a licensed guide. Access shifts with volcanic activity, so check conditions on the day and bring warm layers even in summer.

Sicily guideCable car and excursions

Syracuse

Greek theatre and island old town

About 1h15 by train or Interbus from Catania

Once the rival of Athens, Syracuse has a vast Greek theatre still used for classical drama each summer, the Ear of Dionysius cave, and Ortigia, an island old town of baroque piazzas, a morning market and swimming platforms off the rocks.

Sicily guideGreek theatre season

Good to know

  • Regional train tickets are not tied to a specific train; buy them on the Trenitalia app and check in digitally, or validate paper tickets before boarding.
  • Start early: most day-trip towns are calmest before 10am and after 5pm.
  • Check return times before you set out, especially for buses and ferries, which can stop early in the evening and out of season.
  • Sunday and holiday timetables are thinner, particularly on regional buses in the south.
  • Book timed tickets for Pompeii, the Scrovegni Chapel and Pisa's tower before you travel; the rest can usually be bought on the day.
