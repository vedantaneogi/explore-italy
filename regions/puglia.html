The South

Puglia

Whitewashed towns, trulli and two seas, all under a huge sky.

A lane of whitewashed trulli with grey conical stone roofs and painted symbols in Alberobello, under a blue skyPhoto: Berthold Werner, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Puglia

Puglia is the heel of Italy's boot, a long, flat-ish peninsula with the Adriatic on one side and the Ionian on the other. Its light is white and hard, bouncing off limestone walls, dry-stone field boundaries and the conical roofs of trulli. Inland, sixty million olive trees grow in red earth, some of them older than the masserie, the fortified farmhouses, that tend them.

This is a place to slow down and eat. Breakfast is a pasticciotto warm from the oven; lunch might be orecchiette with turnip tops, raw sea urchins on a rocky beach or focaccia from a Bari bakery. Evenings belong to the passeggiata in a baroque square in Lecce or a hilltop town like Ostuni. And on summer nights the whole Salento seems to dance to the tambourine beat of the pizzica.

Capital
Bari
Best time to go
May, June and September to mid-October are ideal: sea-warm, sunny and calmer than August, when Italian holidaymakers fill the Salento beaches. Autumn brings the olive harvest from October.
Getting there and around
Fly into Bari or Brindisi; both airports have good European links. Trains run down the Adriatic coast from Bari to Brindisi and Lecce, and Ferrovie del Sud Est serves the Valle d'Itria and Salento towns, but a car makes the masserie, beaches and small villages far easier to reach.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Puglia, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Lecce

The 'Florence of the south', a whole city carved in soft, honey-coloured stone that ages to gold. Gaze up at the riot of cherubs and beasts on the façade of Santa Croce, step into the enclosed Piazza del Duomo at night, and see the half-excavated Roman amphitheatre in Piazza Sant'Oronzo. Look out for papier-mâché workshops in the side streets.

Alberobello

A UNESCO-listed town of more than a thousand trulli, the dry-stone houses with cone roofs often painted with white symbols. The Rione Monti quarter is the postcard; the quieter Aia Piccola is still mostly lived in. Climb up to the Trullo Sovrano, the only two-storey trullo, and come early or late to dodge the tour buses.

Ostuni

The 'white city' rises on a hill above olive groves that run to the sea. Lose yourself in the lime-washed lanes of the old town, climb to the Gothic cathedral with its great rose window, and have an aperitivo on the ramparts as the walls turn pink at sunset.

Polignano a Mare

A town built on the very edge of limestone cliffs riddled with sea caves. The pebble cove of Lama Monachile, under a Bourbon bridge, is where everyone swims; follow the balconies of the old town for views straight down to the water. Try a caffè speciale where it was invented.

Otranto

Italy's easternmost town, a walled port on clear, turquoise water. Inside the cathedral is a 12th-century floor mosaic of the Tree of Life, full of elephants, kings and monsters. Swim off the town beach, walk the Aragonese castle walls, then drive south along the cliffs past Torre Sant'Emiliano.

Gargano and Vieste

The spur of the boot is a mountainous promontory of beech forest, the Foresta Umbra, ending in white cliffs and sea caves. Vieste sits on a headland beside the giant Pizzomunno sea stack. Take a boat along the coast to the Grotta Campana and the arch at Architiello di San Felice.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Watch orecchiette being made in Bari Vecchia

On Strada Arco Basso, behind the Norman castle, women sit at tables in their doorways shaping orecchiette with a flick of the knife. Buy a bag to take home, then walk to the Basilica di San Nicola.

Eat at a fornello pronto in Cisternino

Butchers in the Valle d'Itria grill your choice of meat on the spot. Order bombette, little rolls of pork stuffed with caciocavallo, and eat at tables in the lane with a jug of red wine.

Swim at the Grotta della Poesia

A natural pool in the limestone at Roca Vecchia, north of Otranto, linked to the sea by an underwater tunnel. Locals jump from the rim; go early on a summer morning to have room to float.

Cycle the Valle d'Itria

Quiet lanes wind between dry-stone walls, trulli and masserie from Locorotondo to Cisternino and Martina Franca. Stop at a dairy for fresh caciocavallo and a farm for olive oil.

Visit Castel del Monte

Frederick II's mysterious octagonal castle crowns a hill in the Murgia, every tower and room built around the number eight. It is on the 1-cent euro coin.

Go down into the Grotte di Castellana

A huge cave system near Alberobello, discovered in 1938 and explored on guided walks. The long tour, about three kilometres, ends at the Grotta Bianca, glittering with white alabaster formations.

Watch dawn at Punta Palascia

The easternmost point of Italy, a lighthouse on a wild cliff south of Otranto, is where the first sunrise in the country is seen. On clear days you can make out the mountains of Albania across the strait.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Puglia, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Orecchiette con cime di rapaLittle ear-shaped pasta with bitter turnip tops, anchovy, garlic, chilli and toasted breadcrumbs. The signature dish of Bari.
Focaccia bareseThick, oily and crisp at the edges, studded with cherry tomatoes and olives. Bought by the slice from bakery ovens all over Bari.
Pane di Altamura DOPA durum-wheat sourdough loaf with a thick crust and yellow crumb that stays fresh for days, still baked in wood-fired ovens in the Murgia.
Fave e cicorieA smooth purée of dried broad beans served with boiled wild chicory and a stream of new olive oil. Peasant food at its best.

Cheese

Burrata di AndriaA pouch of fresh mozzarella filled with stracciatella, shreds of curd soaked in cream. Born in Andria in the early 20th century and best eaten within a day.

Wine

Primitivo di ManduriaA big, ripe, dark-fruited red from the plains near Taranto, the grape that is a genetic twin of California's Zinfandel.
NegroamaroThe Salento's own red grape, earthy and slightly bitter, behind Salice Salentino and many of Italy's best rosati.

Coffee & spirits

Caffè lecceseEspresso poured over ice with sweet almond milk syrup, stirred until frothy: Lecce's essential summer drink, best at a bar on Piazza Sant'Oronzo.

Oil

Extra-virgin olive oilPuglia makes more olive oil than any other Italian region. Look for peppery Coratina from the north and gentler Ogliarola in the Salento.

Sweets

PasticciottoAn oval shortcrust pastry made with lard and filled with custard, eaten warm for breakfast across the Salento.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

More legacy tables across Italy

Pasticceria Ascalone

Galatina

Since 1745

The family pastry shop where, by local tradition, the pasticciotto was invented from leftover pastry and custard. Still on Via Vittorio Emanuele II, still turning out trays of them warm from the oven.

Il Super Mago del Gelo Mario Campanella

Polignano a Mare

Since 1935

A gelateria on Piazza Garibaldi credited with inventing the caffè speciale: espresso with lemon zest, sugar, amaretto and cream. Also excellent almond granita.

Antonella Ricci (formerly Al Fornello da Ricci)

Ceglie Messapica

Since 1966

The Ricci family's country restaurant, long one of Puglia's most celebrated, now run by Antonella Ricci and Vinod Sookar. Expect refined Pugliese dishes built on local vegetables and herbs, plus the wood-fired oven.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

Sleep in a masseria

Masseria Il Frantoio, outside Ostuni, is a whitewashed farmhouse built over an ancient underground olive press, surrounded by organic olive groves. Long dinners use the farm's own produce and oil.

See options

Rent a trullo in the countryside

Rather than stay in Alberobello itself, rent a restored trullo among the vineyards around Locorotondo or Martina Franca. Thick stone walls keep them cool in summer, and you'll wake to birdsong and dry-stone walls.

A design masseria near the sea

Masseria Moroseta, on a hill near Ostuni, is a small guesthouse of six rooms around a courtyard, built in local stone among old olive trees, with communal breakfasts of home-grown produce.

A palazzo B&B in Lecce

Many baroque townhouses in Lecce's centro storico have become family-run B&Bs with vaulted ceilings of local stone and roof terraces looking over the church domes. You'll be a short stroll from the evening passeggiata.

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Cisternino

A tiny white hill town in the Valle d'Itria with arches, outdoor staircases and potted geraniums. Come in the evening, when the smoke from the fornelli pronti drifts across Via Duca d'Aosta and everyone sits down to grilled meat.

Tremiti Islands

A tiny archipelago off the Gargano, with pine-covered San Domino, the fortified abbey on San Nicola and some of the clearest water in the Adriatic. Ferries and hydrofoils run from Termoli, Vieste and Rodi Garganico in summer.

Santa Maria di Leuca

The very tip of the heel, called 'de finibus terrae', the end of the earth, where the Adriatic and Ionian are said to meet. Climb to the basilica and lighthouse, admire the 19th-century seaside villas, and take a boat into the sea caves along the cliffs.

Galatina

A Salento town with a jewel: the Basilica of Santa Caterina d'Alessandria, every inch covered in 15th-century frescoes. Galatina is also the home of tarantism, where those 'bitten' by the tarantula came to be healed at the chapel of San Paolo.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

La Notte della Taranta

Melpignano and towns across the Salento

Late August

A touring festival of pizzica and Salento folk music that ends with a huge free concert in Melpignano, where thousands dance until dawn.

Event info

Festa di San Nicola

Bari

7-9 May

A costumed parade re-enacts the arrival of the saint's relics in 1087, and his statue is carried out to sea on a fishing boat, followed by a flotilla.

Carnevale di Putignano

Putignano

26 December to Shrove Tuesday

One of Europe's oldest carnivals, dating back to 1394, with giant papier-mâché floats satirising politicians and celebrities paraded through the town on the carnival Sundays.

Fòcara di Novoli

Novoli, near Lecce

16-17 January

For Sant'Antonio Abate, the town builds a towering bonfire from vine cuttings and sets it alight with fireworks, followed by music and wine.

Music, craft and custom

The Salento's heartbeat is the pizzica, a fast, whirling dance driven by frame drums, violins and accordions. It grew out of tarantismo, the belief that the bite of the tarantula could only be cured by days of frenzied dancing, documented by anthropologist Ernesto de Martino in Galatina in 1959. Today it fills the Notte della Taranta. In the Grecìa Salentina villages such as Calimera and Martano, some elders still speak Griko, a Greek dialect. Craft traditions are lively: papier-mâché saints in Lecce, ceramics in the Quartiere delle Ceramiche at Grottaglie, and the trullo-builders' skill in dry stone.
