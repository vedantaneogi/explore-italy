The North

Emilia-Romagna

Italy's food valley: porticoed cities, Parmigiano, Byzantine gold and Adriatic beaches.

Bologna's sea of terracotta roofs, domes and medieval towers seen from San PetronioPhoto: Ввласенко, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Emilia-Romagna

Draw a line along the old Roman Via Emilia from Piacenza to Rimini and you have threaded together Italy's greatest food cities: Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena and Bologna. This is the land of Parmigiano Reggiano, Prosciutto di Parma, traditional balsamic vinegar and tortellini folded by hand, and of Ferrari, Lamborghini and Ducati too. The cities are prosperous, walkable and arcaded, ideal for a slow trip by train.

Romagna, the eastern half, has a different temperament: warmer, louder and nearer the sea. Ravenna's Byzantine mosaics glow in dim churches, Rimini and the Riviera fill up with beach umbrellas and nightlife in summer, and the hills behind are dotted with castles, spa towns and Sangiovese vineyards. Between the two lie the Po delta lagoons around Comacchio, flat and luminous, full of flamingos and eel fishermen's huts.

Capital
Bologna
Best time to go
April to June and September to October are best for city visits and food tours, when cheese dairies and acetaie are busy. Summer suits the Riviera and Ravenna Festival, though the cities get very hot. In autumn there are truffle and chestnut fairs in the hills, and November fog wraps the plain in a lovely melancholy.
Getting there and around
Bologna's Marconi airport is the main gateway, with a monorail to the central station, and Parma and Rimini have smaller airports. Bologna Centrale is Italy's rail crossroads, under 40 minutes from Florence and about an hour from Milan by high-speed train. Regional trains along the Via Emilia link every city, so you don't need a car except in the hills.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Emilia-Romagna, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Bologna

Walk under 62 km of UNESCO-listed porticoes from Piazza Maggiore to the two leaning towers. Shop in the Quadrilatero's market lanes, climb the Asinelli tower or walk up to San Luca, and eat tortellini in brodo and tagliatelle al ragù in an osteria.

Modena

The Romanesque cathedral and its Ghirlandina tower are a UNESCO site, and the Albinelli market is a feast of local produce. Visit an attic acetaia to taste aged balsamic, tour the Enzo Ferrari museum, and book well ahead if you dream of Osteria Francescana.

Parma

An elegant ducal city of pink and ochre: Correggio's swirling dome in the cathedral, the octagonal Baptistery, and the wooden Teatro Farnese in the Pilotta palace. Visit a Parmigiano dairy at dawn and a prosciutto house in Langhirano, and catch Verdi at the Teatro Regio.

Ravenna

The last capital of the Western Roman Empire has eight UNESCO-listed monuments covered in gold, blue and green mosaics. Don't miss San Vitale's Justinian and Theodora, the starry vault of Galla Placidia's mausoleum, and Sant'Apollinare in Classe out among the pines. Dante's tomb is here too.

Ferrara

The Este dukes' Renaissance city is flat, quiet and best seen by bicycle. Ride the nine kilometres of walls, explore the moated Castello Estense, and walk the medieval Via delle Volte. Order cappellacci di zucca and a slice of salama da sugo, the city's slow-cooked sausage.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

See Parmigiano Reggiano being made

Arrive at a caseificio around 8am to watch the curd lifted from copper vats in linen cloths, then walk through cathedral-like aging rooms. Many dairies in the Parma and Reggio hills offer tours.

Taste traditional balsamic in an acetaia

In family attics around Modena and Spilamberto, vinegar ages for at least twelve years in barrel batteries of different woods. Taste drops on Parmigiano, and even on gelato.

Take a pasta class in Bologna

Learn to roll sfoglia with a long rolling pin and fold tortellini as small as a navel, the way the sfogline do. Classes run in home kitchens and in the city's food schools.

Walk the porticoes to San Luca

The world's longest portico climbs nearly four kilometres and 666 arches from Porta Saragozza to the hilltop Sanctuary of the Madonna di San Luca. It's a local Sunday ritual.

Visit the Motor Valley

Visit the Ferrari museums in Maranello and Modena, the Lamborghini museum at Sant'Agata Bolognese and the Ducati factory in Borgo Panigale. Some tours drive past the Fiorano test track.

Explore the Po Delta by boat

From Comacchio, flat-bottomed boats glide through the Valli di Comacchio lagoons past flamingos, herons and the old eel-fishing stations. Go at sunset, when the water turns pink.

Eat piadina by the sea

Romagna's flatbread is cooked on a terracotta or iron griddle and stuffed with squacquerone cheese, rocket and prosciutto. Eat it at a seaside kiosk, a chiosco, on the Riviera.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Emilia-Romagna, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Tortellini in brodoTiny rings of egg pasta filled with pork loin, prosciutto, mortadella and Parmigiano, served in clear capon broth. It's Bologna's Christmas dish.
Tagliatelle al ragùGolden egg ribbons with a slow-simmered meat sauce made with very little tomato. Never call it 'spaghetti bolognese' here.
Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOPCooked grape must aged for at least twelve years in wooden barrels. It's thick, glossy, sweet-sour and used drop by drop.
Piadina Romagnola IGPThin griddled flatbread made with lard, folded around squacquerone and rocket, or ham and cheese.

Cheese

Parmigiano Reggiano DOPAged for at least twelve months and often thirty or more, crystalline and nutty. Eat chunks broken off with a short almond-shaped knife.

Cured meats

Prosciutto di Parma DOPSweet, delicate ham cured only with sea salt and the dry air of the Parma hills, for at least a year.
Culatello di Zibello DOPThe prized heart of the ham, aged in damp cellars by the Po. Fog and humidity give it its depth.
Mortadella Bologna IGPA huge, silky pork sausage studded with cubes of fat, and sometimes pistachios. Eat it in thin slices or cubed with an aperitivo.

Wine

LambruscoA dry, fizzy red served cool, made to cut through rich Emilian food. Look for Sorbara or Grasparossa.
Sangiovese di RomagnaA fresh, cherry-scented red from the Romagna hills. Drink it with grilled meat, cappelletti or a plate of piadina and salumi.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

Osteria del Sole

Bologna

Since 1465

Bologna's oldest osteria, in the Quadrilatero, serves only wine. Bring your own mortadella and bread from the market stalls next door, and share long tables with students and pensioners.

Website

Al Brindisi

Ferrara

Since 1435

Often called the oldest osteria in the world, behind the cathedral. Copernicus is said to have drunk here as a student. Have a glass of local Bosco Eliceo wine with a plate of cured meats.

Website

Hosteria Giusti

Modena

Since 1605

A tiny dining room with only a few tables, behind one of Italy's oldest delicatessens, the Salumeria Giusti. Book for tortellini, cotechino with zabaglione, and aged balsamic.

Website

Paolo Atti & Figli

Bologna

Since 1868

A Bolognese bakery and pasta shop on Via Caprarie, famous for handmade tortellini, bread and certosino spice cake. Buy a tray of fresh pasta to cook at your apartment.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

Antica Corte Pallavicina, Polesine Parmense

The Spigaroli family's sixteenth-century castle farm on the Po has a handful of rooms, heritage pigs, cellars full of culatello, and their own farm table. It's a true food lover's stay.

See options

An agriturismo in the Modena hills

Family farms around Vignola and Castelvetro rent rooms among cherry orchards and Lambrusco vines. Many keep their own balsamic attic, and the hosts will happily walk you through the barrels before dinner.

A farm stay in the Romagna hills

Behind Faenza and Brisighella, farmhouses among olive groves and Sangiovese vineyards make a peaceful base. Pick up fresh piadina and squacquerone in the villages, and spend warm evenings on a terrace above the valley.

Day trips

Out in the morning, back for dinner.

Day trips from every major city

Bologna to Modena

Visit the cathedral and the Albinelli market in the morning, eat tortellini for lunch, and tour an acetaia in the afternoon.

About 25 min by train

Bologna to Ravenna

Spend a day among the mosaics of San Vitale, Galla Placidia and the Neonian Baptistery, and eat a piadina in the old town before the train home.

About 1h20 by train

Bologna to Dozza and Brisighella

See Dozza's painted walls and the regional enoteca in its castle, then drive on to Brisighella for its covered donkey lane and three hilltop pinnacles.

About 40 min to 1h by car

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Dozza

A tiny hilltop village whose house walls are covered in murals left by artists at the biennial Muro Dipinto festival. The Rocca hosts the Enoteca Regionale, a cellar of the region's wines.

Brisighella

A medieval village below three chalk pinnacles crowned with a clock tower, a castle and a sanctuary. Walk the covered Via degli Asini, the donkey lane, and taste the green, peppery Brisighella DOP olive oil.

Comacchio

A little Venice of canals and bridges in the Po Delta, built around the triple Trepponti bridge. Eat marinated eel, visit the old Manifattura dei Marinati, and take a boat out into the lagoon to see the flamingos.

Castell'Arquato

A golden-stone village in the hills near Piacenza, with a Romanesque collegiate church, a turreted fortress and views over the Arda valley. Drink a glass of local Gutturnio with thin slices of coppa piacentina in the piazza.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Palio di Ferrara

Piazza Ariostea, Ferrara

Last Sunday of May

Said to be the world's oldest palio, first run in 1259. It's preceded by Renaissance parades and flag-throwing through May, followed by horse, donkey and foot races.

Event info

Ravenna Festival

Ravenna

June to July

Opera, orchestral music, jazz and dance in basilicas, theatres and the Rocca Brancaleone, with famous conductors and dawn concerts among the mosaics.

Event info

Festival Verdi

Parma and Busseto

Late September to October

Verdi's operas in his home territory, at the Teatro Regio di Parma and the tiny theatre in Busseto, with talks and concerts in the town squares.

Event info

La Notte Rosa

Romagna Riviera

June

The Riviera's 'pink night': concerts, fireworks and parties along 110 km of coast from Comacchio to Cattolica, with the whole Riviera lit up in pink until dawn.

Music, craft and custom

This is a region of music and conviviality. Verdi was born near Busseto, Pavarotti in Modena, and Bologna is a UNESCO City of Music with a strong rock and cantautori scene. Romagna dances liscio, the ballroom polkas and mazurkas of Secondo Casadei, in seaside halls, and Fellini's Rimini still feels like a film set. Bologna's university, founded in 1088, is the oldest in the Western world. Traditional crafts include hand-rolled sfoglia, Faenza majolica and rust-printed hemp cloths in Romagna. The local dialects are strong and widely spoken.
