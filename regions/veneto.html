The North

Veneto

From lagoon to Dolomites: Venice, Palladio's villas and the Prosecco hills.

Boats on the Grand Canal before the Rialto Bridge in warm evening light, VenicePhoto: kallerna, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Veneto

The Veneto runs from the lagoon to the Dolomites, and for centuries it was the mainland of the Most Serene Republic of Venice. That inheritance is everywhere: winged lions over city gates, Palladio's villas among the maize fields, and arcaded squares in Padua, Vicenza and Treviso where the spritz is the excuse to sit down. Venice itself needs no introduction, but it rewards anyone who stays the night, after the day-trippers leave.

Beyond Venice, Verona has its Roman arena and Shakespearean balconies, Padua has Giotto's blue chapel, and the steep Prosecco hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene are a UNESCO landscape. To the north rise Cortina and some of the most famous peaks in the Dolomites. The cooking moves from cicchetti and creamy baccalà in the lagoon to radicchio from Treviso, risotto, polenta and grappa in the foothills.

Capital
Venice
Best time to go
April to June and September to October are best, with mild weather, the Biennale on and fewer crowds. Carnival in February and the Regata Storica in September are peak times in Venice. Come to Cortina from December to March for skiing or July to September for hiking, and to the Prosecco hills for the September harvest.
Getting there and around
Venice Marco Polo and Treviso airports serve the lagoon, and Verona Villafranca serves the west. Fast trains link Venezia Santa Lucia, Padua, Vicenza and Verona in under 90 minutes, and regional trains reach Treviso, Conegliano and Bassano. In Venice you walk or take the vaporetto. Cortina is reached by direct coaches from Venice and its airport, or you can drive into the mountains.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Veneto, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Venice

Stay at least two nights. Cross the Rialto at dawn when the fish market opens, see Tintoretto at the Scuola Grande di San Rocco, and get lost in Cannaregio and Castello, where washing still hangs over the canals. Eat cicchetti standing up, and ride vaporetto line 1 down the Grand Canal at sunset.

Verona

Pink-marble Piazza delle Erbe and the Roman Arena, where opera plays under the stars in summer. Skip Juliet's balcony crowds, then climb to Castel San Pietro for the river view, explore San Zeno's bronze doors, and drink Valpolicella or a glass of Soave in an old osteria.

Padua

Giotto covered the Scrovegni Chapel in deep-blue frescoes around 1305, and timed slots keep visits magical. Padua is also home to one of Europe's oldest universities, with its anatomical theatre, and to the Basilica of Sant'Antonio. Have a spritz under the arcades of Piazza delle Erbe and Piazza della Frutta.

Vicenza

Palladio's city: the Basilica Palladiana's loggias on the main square, the Teatro Olimpico with its trompe-l'oeil streets, and, just outside town, the domed Villa La Rotonda. Climb to Monte Berico's sanctuary for the city view, and eat baccalà alla vicentina with soft polenta.

Cortina d'Ampezzo

The elegant capital of the Dolomites, ringed by the Tofane, Cristallo and Cinque Torri. Ski the Olympia slopes in winter. In summer, take the cable car up Lagazuoi to the First World War tunnels, or drive to the Tre Cime di Lavaredo for the classic loop walk around the three peaks.

Bassano del Grappa

A handsome town on the Brenta river, crossed by Palladio's covered wooden bridge, the Ponte degli Alpini. Taste grappa at Nardini's riverside bar, which has been pouring since 1779, eat white asparagus in spring, and see the Monte Grappa memorials from the First World War.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Go on a bacaro crawl in Venice

Hop between wine bars around Rialto and Cannaregio for an ombra, a small glass of wine, and cicchetti: baccalà mantecato on polenta, sarde in saor and fried meatballs. Most close by early evening.

Row a gondola yourself

Take a voga alla veneta lesson with Row Venice in Cannaregio, standing to row a traditional batellina like a Venetian. It's quieter, cheaper and more fun than a gondola ride.

See opera in the Arena di Verona

Unreserved stone-step seats are cheap. Bring a cushion, and buy a candle to light as the overture starts. The summer season runs from June to early September.

Visit the outer lagoon islands

Take the vaporetto to Burano's painted houses and lace shops, then cross to Torcello's Byzantine mosaics and its almost empty meadows. Mazzorbo has a walled vineyard, where you can have lunch.

Drive the Prosecco road

Follow the Strada del Prosecco between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene. Stop at the Cartizze hill, eat at an osteria senza oste, and taste at family cantine.

Cycle the Brenta Riviera

Follow the Brenta canal from Padua towards the lagoon past the Venetian villas, including the Villa Pisani with its hedge maze and Palladio's Villa Foscari 'La Malcontenta'.

Walk the Tre Cime loop

From Rifugio Auronzo, an easy 10-km circuit goes around the three great walls of the Tre Cime via Rifugio Locatelli. Start early in summer, as the toll road and car park fill fast.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Veneto, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Baccalà mantecatoDried cod whipped with olive oil into a fluffy cream and spread on grilled polenta. It is the king of Venetian cicchetti.
Sarde in saorFried sardines marinated with sweet-sour onions, vinegar, pine nuts and raisins. It was a sailors' dish designed to keep.
Risotto al nero di seppiaInky-black risotto made with cuttlefish and its ink, a classic of the lagoon. Order it in spring, when the cuttlefish are in season.
Bigoli in salsaThick wholewheat spaghetti in a slow-cooked sauce of onions and anchovies, traditionally eaten on fast days.
Radicchio Rosso di Treviso IGPTight, crimson-and-white winter radicchio, grilled, folded into risotto or baked. The late tardivo variety looks like a flower.

Cheese

Asiago DOPA cheese from the Asiago plateau, sold fresh and mild or aged and crumbly. Taste it with honey and a glass of Valpolicella.

Wine

Amarone della ValpolicellaA rich, dry red made from grapes dried for months before pressing. Drink it with braised meat or a hard cheese.
Prosecco Superiore DOCGFrom the steep hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene, finer and drier than the everyday stuff. Try a cloudy col fondo version.

Coffee & spirits

SpritzProsecco, a bitter liqueur such as Aperol, Select or Campari, and a splash of soda, with an olive or orange slice. Venetians drink it from late afternoon.

Sweets

TiramisùTreviso claims the original: savoiardi soaked in coffee, mascarpone, eggs and cocoa. There's no alcohol in the classic recipe.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

More legacy tables across Italy

Caffè Florian

Venice

Since 1720

Italy's oldest café, with mirrored and frescoed salons under the Procuratie of Piazza San Marco. Casanova and Dickens took coffee here. Pay the music surcharge and enjoy the orchestra.

Website

Cantina Do Mori

Venice

Since 1462

Said to be Venice's oldest bacaro, a dark wooden bar near Rialto hung with copper pots. Stand at the counter for an ombra and francobolli, tiny 'stamp' sandwiches. Closed on Sundays.

Caffè Pedrocchi

Padua

Since 1831

The neoclassical 'café without doors' was once open day and night, and students and patriots met here in 1848. Order the caffè Pedrocchi with mint cream, and don't stir it.

Website

Antica Bottega del Vino

Verona

Since 1890

A dark-panelled osteria off Via Mazzini with a legendary cellar of Amarone and Valpolicella. Order the risotto all'Amarone, or a Veronese classic such as pastissada de caval, a slow horse stew.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

A Prosecco hills agriturismo

Family wineries around Valdobbiadene, Follina and Refrontolo rent rooms among the steep vineyard 'hogbacks'. Breakfast includes soppressa salami and local cheese, and at harvest time you can lend a hand among the vines.

A rifugio in the Dolomites

Sleep at Rifugio Lagazuoi at 2,752 metres or Rifugio Locatelli below the Tre Cime, with a dinner of canederli and a dawn view across a sea of peaks. Book early for July and August.

An apartment in residential Venice

Stay in Cannaregio, Castello or on the Giudecca, where Venetians still live. You'll shop at the Rialto market, drink at the local bacaro, and have quiet canals to yourself at night.

Day trips

Out in the morning, back for dinner.

Day trips from every major city

Venice to Padua

Book a slot at the Scrovegni Chapel, walk to the Basilica of Sant'Antonio, and have a spritz in the market squares before heading back for dinner in Venice.

About 30 min by train

Venice to Burano and Torcello

Visit colourful Burano for lace and fish risotto, then quiet Torcello for the golden Last Judgement mosaic in its cathedral.

About 45 min by vaporetto

Verona to Lake Garda and Valpolicella

Taste Amarone in a Valpolicella cantina, then swim and have dinner at Lazise or Garda on the lake's eastern shore.

About 40 min by car or bus

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Asolo

The 'city of a hundred horizons' is a hilltop town of arcades and frescoed façades above the Prosecco and olive country. Explorer Freya Stark and actress Eleonora Duse lived here. Walk up to the Rocca at sunset.

Arquà Petrarca

A medieval village in the volcanic Euganean Hills, where the poet Petrarch spent his last years. Visit his house, taste brodo di giuggiole, a jujube liqueur, and walk the olive-covered slopes.

The Prosecco hills

The steep 'hogback' hills between Conegliano and Valdobbiadene are patterned with vineyards, little churches and osterie. Visit the Molinetto della Croda, a seventeenth-century mill built against a waterfall at Refrontolo, or stand on the slopes of Cartizze at harvest time.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Carnevale di Venezia

Venice

About two weeks before Lent, February

Masks and costumes fill the calli. Highlights include the Flight of the Angel from the Campanile and the water parade in Cannaregio. Book rooms early.

Event info

Festa del Redentore

Venice

Third weekend of July

A pontoon bridge spans the Giudecca Canal to Palladio's Redentore church, and on Saturday night Venetians eat dinner on boats before a huge fireworks display over the basin.

Regata Storica

Grand Canal, Venice

First Sunday of September

A parade of Renaissance-style boats with costumed rowers, followed by fiercely contested races between Venetian rowing clubs down the Grand Canal.

Arena di Verona Opera Festival

Verona

June to early September

Aida, Carmen and Turandot are staged on a grand scale in the Roman amphitheatre. Bring a cushion and a light jacket.

Music, craft and custom

The Venetian Republic left behind a language as much as a style. Venetian dialect is still spoken with pride, from Goldoni's comedies to the cries of the market. Murano glassblowing, Burano lace, the squeri where gondolas are built and Venice's Carnival masks all remain living crafts. Palladio's buildings defined Western architecture, and Vivaldi, Venetian by birth, taught at the Pietà. In the hills, Marostica's living chess game and the Alpini mountain troops' choirs show off the mainland's own traditions.
