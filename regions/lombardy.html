The North  /  Lombardia

Lombardy

Milan's hustle, Lake Como's villas and Renaissance courts across the rice-flat plain.

Lake Como's deep blue arms and the Bellagio promontory seen from the Sentiero del ViandantePhoto: Luca Casartelli, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Lombardy

Lombardy is Italy's engine room, and Milan sets the pace with fashion, design, finance and the best aperitivo in the country. Look a little further, though, and the region slows down beautifully. The great lakes, Como, Maggiore, Iseo and Garda, push fingers of deep blue water into the Alps, lined with villa gardens and ferry piers. On the plain, rice fields and poplar rows lead to Renaissance court cities like Mantua and Cremona.

In the north, the Valtellina and Valcamonica climb into real mountains, with terraced vineyards, buckwheat pasta and ten thousand years of rock carvings. The cooking is rich and butter-based: saffron risotto, ossobuco, polenta with every stew, a different stuffed pasta in every town, and cheeses from creamy Gorgonzola to mountain Bitto. Start in Milan, then take the train. Almost everything is within ninety minutes.

Capital
Milan
Best time to go
April to June and September to October suit the lakes and cities best, when the gardens are in bloom or the harvest is on. Milan is hot and half-empty in August. December is festive, with Sant'Ambrogio, the opening of La Scala and panettone everywhere.
Getting there and around
Milan has three airports: Malpensa, Linate and Bergamo Orio al Serio. Milano Centrale is the high-speed rail hub for the whole north. Trenord regional trains reach Como, Varenna, Bergamo, Brescia, Cremona and Mantua, and ferries criss-cross Lake Como, Iseo and Garda. You only need a car for the valleys.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Lombardy, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Milan

Climb onto the Duomo's marble roof among the spires, walk through the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, and book weeks ahead for Leonardo's Last Supper. Then do as the Milanese do: browse Brera, see the Pinacoteca, and finish with an aperitivo along the Navigli canals as the sky turns orange.

Lake Como

A deep, inverted-Y lake ringed by mountains, villas and terraced gardens. Base yourself in Bellagio at the junction of its arms, or in quieter Varenna, and hop between them by ferry. Visit Villa del Balbianello and Villa Carlotta, then swim from a stone lido as the boats go by.

Bergamo

Ride the funicular up to the Città Alta, a walled hilltop town encircled by UNESCO-listed Venetian walls. Piazza Vecchia, the Colleoni Chapel and the lane of Via Gombito are best at dusk. Eat casoncelli, butter-and-sage ravioli, and walk the walls at sunset for views over the plain.

Mantua

The Gonzaga court city sits on three artificial lakes. Inside the Palazzo Ducale, Mantegna's Camera degli Sposi looks back at you from a painted oculus. In Palazzo Te, Giulio Romano's giants crash down from the ceiling. Eat tortelli di zucca in Piazza delle Erbe, and buy sbrisolona to take home.

Monte Isola, Lake Iseo

Europe's largest lake island is car-free, so you walk or ride the little bus between fishing villages where nets dry in the sun. Climb to the Madonna della Ceriola sanctuary at the top, eat sardines dried on wooden racks, and ferry back to Franciacorta for sparkling wine.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

See the Last Supper

Leonardo's mural in the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie allows small groups for fifteen minutes. Tickets go on sale in batches months ahead, so set a reminder, or book a guided tour.

Catch an opera at La Scala

Gallery seats can be surprisingly affordable, and on performance days a few last-minute tickets are released. The season opens every 7 December, the feast of Milan's patron saint.

Ferry-hop Lake Como

Buy a day pass for the central lake and hop between Bellagio, Varenna, Menaggio and Tremezzo. Leave time for a lakeside lunch of missoltini, sun-dried shad, with polenta.

Tour the Franciacorta cellars

South of Lake Iseo, rolling vineyards make Italy's finest bottle-fermented sparkling wine. Cycle between cantine around Erbusco and Adro, then stop for a glass in a medieval borgo.

Walk among prehistoric carvings

At Capo di Ponte in the Valcamonica, open-air parks protect rocks carved over eight thousand years with deer, warriors, ploughs and the 'Camunian rose'. It was Italy's first UNESCO site.

Hear a violin in Cremona

The Museo del Violino holds instruments by Stradivari and Guarneri, and short concerts in its auditorium let you hear them played. Workshops around the town still build them by hand.

Ride the Bernina Express

From Tirano in the Valtellina, the red Rhaetian Railway train climbs over the Bernina Pass to St Moritz via the circular viaduct of Brusio. It is UNESCO-listed and spectacular.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Lombardy, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Risotto alla milaneseCarnaroli rice cooked with bone marrow, butter and saffron until golden and creamy. It is often paired with ossobuco.
Cotoletta alla milaneseA thick, bone-in veal chop, breaded and fried in clarified butter. Purists want the thick, pink-centred chop; the thin, plate-sized 'elephant's ear' is its flashier cousin.
Pizzoccheri della ValtellinaShort buckwheat ribbons layered with potatoes, Savoy cabbage, Casera cheese and garlic butter. Mountain fuel.
Tortelli di zuccaA Mantuan pasta filled with pumpkin, crushed amaretti and mostarda, dressed with butter and Grana. It's sweet, savoury and very Renaissance.

Cheese

Gorgonzola DOPBlue-veined cow's milk cheese, sold dolce and creamy or piccante and crumbly. Try it melted over polenta.
Bitto DOPA Valtellina mountain cheese made in summer pastures, sometimes with goat's milk, and aged for years by devotees.

Wine

Franciacorta DOCGBottle-fermented sparkling wine from Chardonnay and Pinot Nero grown near Lake Iseo. Try a Satèn for something softer.

Coffee & spirits

Campari and the Negroni sbagliatoThe bitter red aperitivo Gaspare Campari created in 1860. The 'mistaken' Negroni swaps gin for sparkling wine and was invented at Bar Basso.

Sweets

PanettoneMilan's tall Christmas bread, naturally leavened for days and studded with raisins and candied orange.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

Pasticceria Marchesi

Milan

Since 1824

A wood-panelled pastry shop on Via Santa Maria alla Porta, near the Last Supper. Stand at the counter for coffee and a cream-filled brioche, and buy panettone in its pale green box.

Camparino in Galleria

Milan

Since 1915

At the Duomo entrance to the Galleria, the Art Nouveau bar opened by Davide Campari still serves Campari seltz from a tap. Drink it standing at the mosaic bar, the way the regulars do.

Website

Antica Trattoria della Pesa

Milan

Since 1880

Named for the old customs weighbridge outside, this is one of the city's oldest trattorie. Order ossobuco with saffron risotto, or cotoletta, then the zabaglione.

Website

Caffè del Tasso

Bergamo

Since 1476

On Piazza Vecchia in the Città Alta, this began as an inn and later became a café. It's a member of the Locali Storici d'Italia. Have a coffee or a glass of Valcalepio looking onto the square.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

A Franciacorta wine-estate agriturismo

Many sparkling-wine estates between Erbusco and Lake Iseo rent rooms in restored farmhouses. Wake up among the vines, cycle to the lake, and taste cuvées in the cellar before dinner.

A mountain rifugio in the Valtellina

In Val Masino or Valmalenco, family-run rifugi reached on foot serve pizzoccheri and sciatt, buckwheat cheese fritters, after a day on the granite trails. Bunk rooms are simple and the stars are spectacular.

A lakeside B&B in a Como village

Skip the grand hotels and stay in a family house in Varenna, Nesso or Argegno. You'll have breakfast on a terrace above the water and a ferry stop at the bottom of the steps.

Day trips from every major city

Milan to Bergamo Città Alta

Spend a day on the Venetian walls, in Piazza Vecchia and the Colleoni Chapel, with casoncelli and polenta for lunch in the upper town.

About 50 min by train, then funicular

Milan to Varenna and Bellagio

Visit Villa Monastero's gardens, cross to Bellagio for its lanes and villa park, and catch the late-afternoon light on the water from the ferry deck.

About 1h by train to Varenna, then ferry

Mantua to Sabbioneta

Visit Vespasiano Gonzaga's ideal Renaissance town, where the star-shaped walls, the theatre and the long gallery were all built to perfect proportions.

About 40 min by car

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Sabbioneta

A Renaissance 'ideal city' built from scratch in the 1500s by Vespasiano Gonzaga, with star-shaped walls, a jewel-box theatre by Scamozzi and a 96-metre gallery. It's UNESCO-listed with Mantua, but you'll probably have it almost to yourself.

Valcamonica rock art

More than 140,000 figures carved over eight millennia cover the glacier-smoothed rocks of this alpine valley. Walk the Naquane park at Capo di Ponte in low afternoon light, when the carvings stand out best.

Crespi d'Adda

A perfectly preserved company town built in the late 1800s around a cotton mill on the Adda river. It has workers' cottages with gardens, a school, a church and the owners' mock-medieval castle, and is a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Oh Bej! Oh Bej!

Milan, around Castello Sforzesco

Around 7 December

Milan's market for Sant'Ambrogio, its patron saint: stalls of roasted chestnuts, mulled wine, antiques and crafts, and the unofficial start of Christmas in the city.

Festivaletteratura

Mantua

Early September

Five days of author talks in piazzas, courtyards and palazzi. It is one of Europe's most charming literary festivals, with readings at every corner.

Milan Design Week

Milan

April

The Salone del Mobile at the fair grounds, plus hundreds of Fuorisalone installations in courtyards, showrooms and palazzi across the city.

Sagra di San Giovanni

Isola Comacina, Lake Como

Weekend nearest 24 June

A procession of boats to Lake Como's only island, a mass among the ruins, and at night thousands of snail-shell oil lamps floating on the water before fireworks.

Music, craft and custom

Milan's culture is opera, design and work. La Scala's 7 December premiere is the event of the social year, and Milanese dialect still sounds in the old osterie. Cremona's traditional violin-making is on UNESCO's intangible heritage list, and luthiers still work there in dozens of workshops. Bergamo gave the commedia dell'arte two of its masked characters, Harlequin and Brighella. In the valleys above Bergamo, teams of bell-ringers still play their church bells by hand, and around Lake Como silk has been woven for centuries.
