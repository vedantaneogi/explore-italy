The Centre

Abruzzo

Wolves, shepherds' villages and the Apennines' wildest peaks, an hour from the Adriatic.

The octagonal church of Santa Maria della Pietà below Rocca Calascio with the snowy Gran Sasso behindPhoto: kallerna, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Abruzzo

About a third of Abruzzo is protected as national or regional park, and its mountains are serious: the Gran Sasso, with Corno Grande, the highest summit in the Apennines, and the vast rounded bulk of the Maiella. Marsican brown bears and wolves still roam the beech forests of the Abruzzo National Park, and for centuries shepherds walked their flocks hundreds of kilometres down the tratturi to the plains of Puglia every autumn.

The villages they left behind are stone-built and high: Santo Stefano di Sessanio, Castel del Monte, Scanno. Below them the coast is gentler, with long sandy beaches around Pescara and, further south, the Costa dei Trabocchi, where wooden fishing platforms on stilts now serve fish suppers over the sea. Food is honest and generous: arrosticini lamb skewers, saffron from the Navelli plain and Montepulciano d'Abruzzo poured by the jug.

Capital
L'Aquila
Best time to go
June to September for hiking, the high plains and the coast, with Sulmona's joust in late July and L'Aquila's Perdonanza at the end of August. Winter brings skiing at Roccaraso and Campo Imperatore and snowbound villages made for long lunches by the fire.
Getting there and around
Abruzzo Airport at Pescara has flights from around Europe, and Rome Fiumicino is roughly two hours' drive from L'Aquila. The A24 and A25 motorways cross the mountains from Rome to the coast, frequent buses link Rome with L'Aquila and Pescara, and trains run along the Adriatic and, slowly, inland to Sulmona. In the mountains you need a car.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Abruzzo, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

L'Aquila

The capital was badly damaged by the 2009 earthquake and has been patiently rebuilt; its centre is alive again. See the pink-and-white checkerboard façade of Santa Maria di Collemaggio, where Pope Celestine V was crowned, the medieval Fontana delle 99 Cannelle and the massive 16th-century Spanish fortress. Then buy soft chocolate torrone on Piazza Duomo.

Sulmona

Ovid's hometown sits in a green basin ringed by mountains and is famous for confetti, sugared almonds shaped into flowers, bouquets and ears of wheat. Walk under the 13th-century aqueduct on Piazza Garibaldi, visit the confetti museum at the Pelino factory and come in late July for the Giostra Cavalleresca.

Campo Imperatore

A high, treeless plateau under the Gran Sasso, nicknamed 'Little Tibet' for its wind-scoured emptiness, with wild horses and grazing sheep. Drive up from Castel del Monte or take the cable car from Fonte Cerreto, hike towards Corno Grande, then eat arrosticini grilled at a roadside ristoro.

Abruzzo National Park

One of Italy's oldest national parks, founded in the 1920s and home to the Marsican brown bear, Apennine wolves and Abruzzo chamois. Base yourself in Pescasseroli or Civitella Alfedena, walk the Val di Rose at dawn for chamois, and go out with a licensed guide for the best chance of seeing bears.

Costa dei Trabocchi

South of Ortona the coast turns into pebbly coves and green headlands dotted with trabocchi, spidery wooden fishing machines on stilts, several of them now restaurants. Cycle the Via Verde, a path along the old coastal railway towards Vasto, and book a trabocco dinner for sunset.

Scanno

A grey-stone village above a heart-shaped lake, made famous by the photographs Henri Cartier-Bresson and Mario Giacomelli took of its women in traditional dress. Walk the path above the lake to see the heart, and look for the presentosa, a filigree pendant crafted by local goldsmiths.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Spend a night on a trabocco

Book dinner on one of the converted trabocchi near San Vito Chietino or Fossacesia: raw fish, brodetto and fried calamari served on a wooden platform with the Adriatic under your feet.

Walk the Gole del Sagittario

The road and trails from Anversa degli Abruzzi to Scanno run through a narrow limestone gorge with a WWF reserve at its foot. Stop for cheese at La Porta dei Parchi, the shepherds' farm just outside Anversa.

Climb to Corno Grande

From the Campo Imperatore hotel, the normal route to the 2,912-metre summit is a long, rocky day hike; the easier walk to Rifugio Duca degli Abruzzi gives huge views over the plateau.

Eat arrosticini by the fire

Skewers of mutton or lamb cubes grilled over a long trough-like brazier called a fornacella, eaten by the dozen with bread drizzled with oil and a glass of Montepulciano.

See saffron flowering on the Navelli plain

In late October and early November the fields below Navelli turn purple with crocus flowers, picked at dawn and stripped by hand for the precious red stigmas of Zafferano dell'Aquila DOP.

Ski or snowshoe at Roccaraso

The Alto Sangro resorts around Roccaraso and Rivisondoli are the biggest ski area in southern-central Italy; outside the season, the same slopes are good for hiking and mountain biking.

Cycle the Via Verde dei Trabocchi

A flat cycle path along the old Adriatic railway line, through tunnels and past beaches and trabocchi between Ortona and Vasto. Rent bikes in San Vito Chietino or Fossacesia and stop for a swim.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Abruzzo, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

ArrosticiniThin skewers of mutton or lamb, grilled over embers and salted at the end; a shepherd's dish that has become the region's emblem.
Spaghetti alla chitarraSquare-edged egg pasta cut by pressing the dough through the steel strings of a wooden frame called a chitarra, served with a ragù of tiny meatballs, pallottine.
Zafferano dell'Aquila DOPSaffron grown on the Navelli plain since the 13th century, deep red and intensely aromatic. Try it in risotto or with the local pasta.
Brodetto alla vasteseVasto's fish stew cooked in a terracotta pot with tomato and sweet peppers, made with a mix of Adriatic fish and never stirred, only shaken.

Cheese

Pecorino di FarindolaA rare sheep's cheese from the slopes of the Gran Sasso, made with pig's rennet, a Slow Food presidium with a nutty, slightly spicy flavour.

Cured meats

Ventricina del VasteseA coarse pork salami seasoned with sweet and hot dried peppers and wild fennel, traditionally aged inside a pig's stomach; a Slow Food presidium.

Wine

Montepulciano d'AbruzzoThe region's everyday and best red, dark and plummy with soft tannins; the pale-red Cerasuolo d'Abruzzo is made from the same grape and is perfect chilled in summer.

Coffee & spirits

CenterbeA fierce green herbal liqueur from Tocco da Casauria, flavoured with mountain herbs and drunk as a digestivo; the strong version is around 70% alcohol.

Sweets

Confetti di SulmonaSugared almonds made in Sulmona since the 15th century, sold plain or wired into flowers, bouquets and rosaries for weddings and christenings.
ParrozzoPescara's dome-shaped almond cake covered in dark chocolate, created in 1920 in imitation of rustic corn bread; Gabriele D'Annunzio wrote verses in its praise.

Confetti Pelino

Sulmona

Since 1783

Sulmona's most famous confetti maker, still family-run, with a shop and a museum of confectionery art and machinery at its factory. Buy a few sugared-almond flowers and watch how they are made.

Bar Fratelli Nurzia

L'Aquila

Since 1835

The Nurzia family's soft chocolate-and-hazelnut torrone has been made since 1835; their Liberty-style bar on Piazza Duomo serves coffee with torrone, ferratelle wafers and hot chocolate in winter.

Pasticceria Emo Lullo

Guardiagrele

Since 1889

Registered in 1889 and still in the same family, this pastry shop is the home of the sise delle monache, the 'nuns' breasts': three soft domes of sponge filled with custard and dusted with sugar.

Villa Maiella

Guardiagrele

Since Late 1960s

Founded as a trattoria by nonna Ginetta and nonno Arcangelo, now a restaurant with rooms run by the third generation at the foot of the Maiella. Their tripe, snails and sagne e fagioli are a lesson in Abruzzese cooking.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

Albergo diffuso in Santo Stefano di Sessanio

Sextantio, the albergo diffuso that rescued this half-abandoned village, spreads its rooms through medieval houses with smoke-blackened walls, wool blankets from local looms and no TVs. It is the model that inspired similar projects across Italy.

Shepherds' farm stay in Anversa degli Abruzzi

La Porta dei Parchi is an organic sheep farm run by a cooperative at the mouth of the Sagittario gorge. Stay in the bioagriturismo, eat their raw-milk cheeses and lamb, and spend a day as a shepherd on transhumance.

See options

Mountain rifugio on the Gran Sasso

Hut-to-hut walking is part of Abruzzo life: book a bunk at a staffed rifugio like Duca degli Abruzzi, above Campo Imperatore, for a hearty dinner, a cold night and sunrise over the Adriatic.

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Santo Stefano di Sessanio

A stone village at 1,250 metres on the edge of Campo Imperatore, once a Medici wool outpost, saved from abandonment by a careful restoration that kept every old wall and doorway. Buy the tiny local lentils and walk the old mule track towards Rocca Calascio.

Rocca Calascio

One of the highest castles in Italy, a ruined stone fortress on a ridge at around 1,460 metres, with the octagonal church of Santa Maria della Pietà just below. It was a filming location for Ladyhawke; go at sunset, when the whole ridge glows gold.

Pacentro

A Maiella village crowned by the towers of the Cantelmo-Caldora castle, the ancestral village of Madonna's grandparents. On the first Sunday of September young men race barefoot down a stony slope and up to the church in the Corsa degli Zingari.

Atri

A hill town between the Gran Sasso and the sea with a Romanesque cathedral whose apse is covered in Andrea Delitio's 15th-century frescoes. Around it, the Calanchi di Atri reserve protects a strange landscape of eroded clay ridges.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Processione dei Serpari

Cocullo

1 May

The statue of Saint Dominic is draped with live, harmless snakes caught in the surrounding hills and carried through the village, an ancient rite blending pagan and Christian traditions.

Giostra Cavalleresca

Sulmona

Late July

Knights from the town's districts and nearby villages joust in a figure-eight course in Piazza Garibaldi, after a grand parade in Renaissance costume.

Perdonanza Celestiniana

L'Aquila

28-29 August

Since 1294 the Holy Door of Collemaggio has opened for a plenary indulgence granted by Pope Celestine V, with a historic procession and concerts. UNESCO intangible heritage.

Le Farchie

Fara Filiorum Petri

16 January

On the eve of Saint Anthony's day, the village's districts raise and burn huge bundles of reeds up to ten metres tall in front of the saint's church.

Music, craft and custom

Abruzzo's culture grew out of transhumance, and the tratturi, wool and shepherds' songs still shape it. Zampogna bagpipes and the organetto drive the saltarello, and 'Vola vola vola', a 1922 song, is the unofficial anthem at every feast. Craft traditions are alive: maiolica in Castelli, bobbin lace in Pescocostanzo, wrought iron and copper in Guardiagrele, filigree in Scanno and Sulmona's confetti flowers. Gabriele D'Annunzio was born in Pescara, and his house there is a museum.
