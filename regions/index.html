Twenty regions

Each with its own dialect, kitchen, saints, festivals and landscapes. Choose one, or string a few together.

The three sheer peaks of the Tre Cime di Lavaredo above a green alpine meadowPhoto: Daniele Bonaldo, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  2. Regions

The North

Alps, lakes, lagoon cities and the richest kitchens in the country.

Valle d'Aosta

Valle d'Aosta / Vallée d'Aoste

Italy's smallest region, where Roman gates sit beneath four-thousand-metre peaks.

Piedmont

Piemonte

Royal arcades, Barolo hills and white truffles under an Alpine horizon.

Liguria

A thin arc of coast where pastel villages cling between basil terraces and sea.

Lombardy

Lombardia

Milan's hustle, Lake Como's villas and Renaissance courts across the rice-flat plain.

Trentino-Alto Adige

Trentino-Alto Adige / Südtirol

Pale Dolomite spires, apple orchards and dumplings in three languages.

Veneto

From lagoon to Dolomites: Venice, Palladio's villas and the Prosecco hills.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Habsburg coffeehouses, Roman mosaics and white wines where Italy meets Central Europe.

Emilia-Romagna

Italy's food valley: porticoed cities, Parmigiano, Byzantine gold and Adriatic beaches.

The Centre

Hill towns, Renaissance cities, Rome, and the green Apennine spine.

Tuscany

Toscana

Renaissance cities, cypress-lined crests and Sunday lunches that run until dusk.

Umbria

Italy's green heart: saints, truffles and hill towns floating above the morning mist.

Marche

Renaissance Urbino, white Conero cliffs and hill towns facing the Adriatic.

Lazio

Rome, of course, plus volcanic lakes, Etruscan tombs and a coast of grottoes.

Abruzzo

Wolves, shepherds' villages and the Apennines' wildest peaks, an hour from the Adriatic.

Molise

The region Italians joke doesn't exist: sheep tracks, bell-founders and empty ruins.

The South

Volcanoes, baroque towns, two seas and the deepest roots of Italian food.

Campania

Pizza, volcanoes, lemon terraces and a city that never lowers its voice.

Puglia

Whitewashed towns, trulli and two seas, all under a huge sky.

Basilicata

Cave cities, Lucanian Dolomites and a slice of coast few people find.

Calabria

The toe of Italy: two seas, wild mountains and plenty of chilli.

The Islands

Sicily and Sardinia: whole worlds of their own, each with its own sea.

Sicily

Sicilia

Greek temples, Arab-Norman gold, a smoking volcano and the best pastry in Italy.

Sardinia

Sardegna

Turquoise coves, Bronze Age towers and a mountain heart that keeps its own ways.
