The South

Campania

Pizza, volcanoes, lemon terraces and a city that never lowers its voice.

The Amalfi Coast seen from the heights above Ravello, with green terraced slopes dropping to a deep blue seaPhoto: Bruno Rijsman, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Campania

Campania runs from the loud, glorious tangle of Naples to cliffs stacked with pastel houses and lemon pergolas. Vesuvius sits over everything, the volcano that buried Pompeii and still frames every view across the bay. Within an hour you can go from a wood-fired margherita on Via dei Tribunali to a Roman street rutted by cart wheels, then to a ferry deck with Capri rising out of the haze.

It is a region of extremes and appetites. Mornings start with a short, sweet espresso and a sfogliatella still crackling from the oven; afternoons drift by on a pebble beach under Positano's dome of green and yellow majolica. Go south and it quietens: Greek temples stand in buffalo pasture at Paestum, and the Cilento coast is all wild headlands and fishing villages where the anchovies come in at dawn.

Capital
Naples
Best time to go
April to early June and September to October bring warm sea, open ferries and fewer crowds; July and August are hot and busy on the coast, while Naples itself is lively all year.
Getting there and around
Naples Capodichino airport has flights across Europe, and Napoli Centrale is about 70 minutes from Rome by high-speed train. The Circumvesuviana railway runs to Pompeii, Herculaneum and Sorrento, SITA buses and summer ferries link the Amalfi Coast towns, and hydrofoils leave Naples' Molo Beverello for Capri, Ischia and Procida. A car is useful only in the Cilento.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Campania, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Naples

Italy's most theatrical city. Walk Spaccanapoli and Via dei Tribunali through the UNESCO-listed historic centre, see the marble Veiled Christ in Cappella Sansevero, and give a full morning to the Farnese sculptures and Pompeii mosaics at the National Archaeological Museum. Ride the funicular up to Vomero at dusk and watch the lights come on below Castel Sant'Elmo.

Pompeii and Herculaneum

Two Roman towns stopped mid-sentence by the eruption of 79 AD. Pompeii is vast: arrive at opening, walk the rutted Via dell'Abbondanza and find the painted Villa of the Mysteries. Smaller Herculaneum, buried deeper, keeps carbonised wooden beams, upper floors and mosaics so fresh they look recently swept.

Positano

The vertical town of a thousand postcards, poured down a ravine to the sea. Climb its stairways past linen shops and bougainvillea, have a granita by the majolica-domed church of Santa Maria Assunta, then swim off Spiaggia Grande or take a boat to quieter Fornillo. Evenings, when the day-trippers leave, are when it feels like a village again.

Ravello

Perched 350 metres above Amalfi, Ravello trades beaches for gardens and silence. Walk the long avenue of Villa Cimbrone to the Terrace of Infinity, where marble busts look out over the whole coast, then visit the Moorish cloister and terraces of Villa Rufolo, which inspired a scene in Wagner's Parsifal. Summer concerts there are unforgettable.

Capri

Glamorous, yes, but also a limestone island made for walking. Skip the funicular queue and follow Via Krupp's hairpins or the path to the Arco Naturale, look down on the Faraglioni sea stacks from the Giardini di Augusto, and ride the chairlift up Monte Solaro from Anacapri. Stay overnight to have the lanes almost to yourself.

Paestum

Three Doric temples, among the best-preserved Greek temples anywhere, standing in open meadows south of Salerno. Walk among the honey-coloured columns of the Temple of Neptune, then see the painted Tomb of the Diver in the museum. Lunch at a nearby buffalo farm on mozzarella made that morning.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Hike the Path of the Gods

Start at Bomerano above Agerola and follow the Sentiero degli Dei along the cliffs to Nocelle, with Positano and the Li Galli islets far below. Allow three hours, then descend the 1,700-odd steps or catch the bus.

Eat pizza on Via dei Tribunali

Join the queue at one of the historic pizzerias in the old centre and order a margherita or marinara, folded 'a portafoglio' if you take it to go. The art of the Neapolitan pizzaiuolo is on UNESCO's intangible heritage list.

Go underground in Naples

Descend 40 metres into Napoli Sotterranea, a maze of Greek quarries, Roman cisterns and wartime shelters beneath the historic centre, or tour the skull-lined Fontanelle Cemetery in the Rione Sanità.

Walk the rim of Vesuvius

A bus or shuttle climbs to about 1,000 metres; from there a 30-minute gravel path leads to the crater's edge, with the whole Bay of Naples spread below. Tickets must be booked online in advance.

Swim in the Fiordo di Furore

Climb down the steps from the coast road to a tiny beach wedged in a gorge under a high arched bridge. Come in the morning before the sun leaves the cove.

Visit a buffalo mozzarella dairy

Farms around Paestum and Battipaglia let you watch the curd being stretched by hand and taste mozzarella still warm. Many also make buffalo-milk yoghurt and gelato.

Soak in Ischia's thermal gardens

Ischia is a volcanic island full of hot springs. Spend a day moving between the terraced pools of a thermal park above Citara bay, then watch the sunset from Sant'Angelo.

Buy ceramics in Vietri sul Mare

At the eastern end of the Amalfi Coast, Vietri has made bright hand-painted majolica for centuries. Look for the tiled dome of San Giovanni Battista and the small workshops along Via Madonna degli Angeli.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Campania, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Pizza napoletanaSoft, blistered, thin in the middle with a puffy cornicione, cooked in about 90 seconds in a wood-fired oven. Margherita and marinara are the classics.
Colatura di alici di CetaraAn amber anchovy extract descended from Roman garum, made in the fishing village of Cetara. A few drops on spaghetti with garlic and parsley is a Christmas Eve tradition.
Ragù napoletanoA Sunday sauce simmered for hours with whole cuts of beef and pork until dark and glossy, served over ziti or candele.

Cheese

Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOPMade from water-buffalo milk in the plains around Caserta and Paestum. Eat it the day it is made, at room temperature, ideally with nothing at all.

Wine

Taurasi DOCGA powerful, long-lived red from Aglianico grapes grown on the hills of Irpinia, often called the Barolo of the south.
Fiano di Avellino and Greco di TufoTwo of Italy's finest native whites from Irpinia: Fiano nutty and honeyed, Greco mineral and taut. Both are DOCG.

Coffee & spirits

LimoncelloA bright lemon liqueur made from the thick-skinned Sfusato Amalfitano and Sorrento lemons, served ice-cold after dinner.
Neapolitan espressoShort, dense and usually pre-sweetened, served with a glass of water first. Leave a 'caffè sospeso', a paid-forward coffee, for the next person.

Sweets

SfogliatellaA shell of crisp, many-layered pastry filled with semolina, ricotta and candied citrus. The softer 'frolla' version uses shortcrust instead.
BabàA mushroom-shaped yeast cake soaked in rum syrup, sold in every Neapolitan pastry shop and sometimes split and filled with cream.

Gran Caffè Gambrinus

Naples

Since 1860

The grand café on the corner of Piazza del Plebiscito, with Liberty-style mirrored salons once frequented by Oscar Wilde and Gabriele D'Annunzio. Stand at the bar for an espresso and a sfogliatella.

Since 1870

The Condurro family's pizzeria in Forcella, famous for keeping things simple: marble tables, a fast queue and essentially just margherita and marinara, both superb.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

A buffalo-farm agriturismo near Paestum

Tenuta Seliano, run by the Bellelli Baratta family, is a historic estate of stone barns and towers a short ride from the temples. Guests can walk out to the buffalo herd, eat dinners built on farm produce and book cooking lessons.

A hillside organic farm above Sorrento

Agriturismo Le Tore sits among olive and lemon groves at Sant'Agata sui Due Golfi, between the Bay of Naples and the Amalfi Coast. Rooms are in a 19th-century farmhouse and breakfast comes from the farm.

See options

A palazzo B&B in the centro storico

Many of the grand old palazzi around Spaccanapoli now hide family-run B&Bs up marble staircases. You'll hear the neighbourhood wake up below and be steps from the pastry shops and pizzerias.

Fishermen's rooms on Procida

Skip the bigger islands and rent rooms from a local family in Marina Corricella or Chiaiolella, where boats are still hauled up between the houses and the evening passeggiata is unhurried.

Day trips

Out in the morning, back for dinner.

Day trips from every major city

Naples to Pompeii

Trains from Napoli Garibaldi stop at Pompei Scavi, right by the entrance. Combine with Herculaneum (Ercolano Scavi) on the same line if you start early.

35 min by Circumvesuviana train

Naples to Royal Palace of Caserta

The Bourbon kings' answer to Versailles: 1,200 rooms and a three-kilometre park of fountains and cascades ending in an English garden. Rent a bike for the grounds.

40 min by regional train

Sorrento to Capri

Frequent boats cross from Marina Piccola. Take the first ferry, walk to the Faraglioni lookout before the crowds, and return at sunset.

20-25 min by fast ferry

Salerno to Paestum

Trains on the line to Sapri stop at Paestum station, a short walk from the temples through the ancient walls.

35 min by regional train

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Procida

The smallest island in the bay, and the most lived-in. Marina Corricella's ice-cream-coloured houses tumble to a harbour of fishing boats; climb to Terra Murata for the view, then eat lemon salad and fresh pasta with octopus at the water's edge. Il Postino was filmed here.

Furore

Called 'the town that doesn't exist' because its houses are scattered across the slopes instead of gathered in a centre. Its fjord is a narrow cleft in the cliffs crossed by a single high bridge, with old paper mills and a tiny beach at the bottom.

Certosa di Padula

A Carthusian monastery in the Vallo di Diano so big it has its own cloister of 84 columns, among the largest in Europe. Its kitchens, monks' cells and a spiral marble staircase are part of the Cilento UNESCO site, and you'll often have them nearly alone.

Sant'Agata de' Goti

A medieval town in the Sannio built right to the edge of a tuff cliff above two streams, its houses rising straight out of the rock. Wander between the Romanesque cathedral and the frescoed church of the Annunziata, then taste local Falanghina.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Festa di San Gennaro

Naples Cathedral

19 September (also early May and 16 December)

The city crowds into the Duomo to watch for the liquefaction of its patron saint's blood, taken as a good omen for the year ahead. Street stalls and processions follow.

Ravello Festival

Ravello

July to early September

Concerts on a stage cantilevered over the cliff in the gardens of Villa Rufolo, with the coast as a backdrop. The dawn concert is legendary.

Event info

Processione dei Misteri

Procida

Good Friday

Islanders carry elaborate floats depicting biblical scenes, many built by local families, from Terra Murata down to Marina Grande in a solemn morning procession.

Festa dei Gigli

Nola

Sunday after 22 June

Teams of men shoulder eight 25-metre wooden towers and a ship and dance them through the streets to brass bands. It is listed by UNESCO as intangible heritage.

Music, craft and custom

Naples sings. Neapolitan song, from 'O sole mio' to 'Funiculì, Funiculà', written in 1880 for the new Vesuvius funicular, is the city's lingua franca, still heard from street guitarists and at the Festival di Piedigrotta. The tarantella pulses at village feasts, and Pulcinella, the black-masked, white-smocked commedia dell'arte clown, is the city's spirit. Neapolitan is a language with its own literature. Crafts run deep too: nativity figures are carved year-round on Via San Gregorio Armeno, Torre del Greco carves coral cameos, and Vietri and Capodimonte make ceramics and porcelain.
