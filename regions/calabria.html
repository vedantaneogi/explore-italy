The South

Calabria

The toe of Italy: two seas, wild mountains and plenty of chilli.

The sanctuary of Santa Maria dell'Isola on its rocky promontory above the turquoise sea at Tropea, in morning lightPhoto: Benjamin Smith, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Calabria

Calabria is the toe of the boot, almost all mountain and almost all coastline: some 800 kilometres of it, Tyrrhenian on one side and Ionian on the other. Towns like Tropea sit on sandstone cliffs above beaches of white sand and clear water, while inland the Sila and Aspromonte ranges hide forests of giant larch pines, lakes and villages where Albanian and Greek are still spoken.

This was Magna Graecia, where Pythagoras taught at Kroton and the Riace Bronzes were pulled from the sea in 1972. It is also Italy's spice cupboard: 'nduja spread on bread, red onions sweet enough to eat raw, bergamot, licorice and chilli in nearly everything. Tourism is still low-key, prices are kind, and hospitality is generous to the point of overwhelming.

Capital
Catanzaro
Best time to go
June and September are perfect for the coast, with warm sea and fewer crowds than August. Spring brings wildflowers in the Aspromonte and Sila, and the mountains stay cool in high summer.
Getting there and around
Lamezia Terme is the main airport, with smaller ones at Reggio Calabria and Crotone. High-speed trains run from Rome and Naples down the Tyrrhenian coast to Lamezia and Reggio, and a local line serves Pizzo and Tropea. Ferries cross from Villa San Giovanni to Messina in about 20 minutes. Rent a car for the interior and the Ionian coast.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Calabria, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Tropea

Calabria's prettiest town, its palazzi built right to the edge of a cliff above a long white beach. Climb the steps to the sanctuary of Santa Maria dell'Isola on its rock, eat red-onion everything along Corso Vittorio Emanuele, and watch the sunset with Stromboli smoking on the horizon.

Scilla

The home of Homer's sea monster, now a fishing town below a castle on a rocky spur. Its Chianalea quarter has houses rising straight out of the water, with boats moored between them. Eat swordfish caught by the tall passerelle boats in the Strait of Messina.

Reggio Calabria

The city at the tip of the toe, facing Sicily across the Strait. The National Archaeological Museum holds the Riace Bronzes, two 5th-century BC Greek warriors of breathtaking realism. Then walk the palm-lined Lungomare Falcomatà with an ice cream from Cesare.

Pizzo

A small town on a cliff overlooking the Gulf of Sant'Eufemia, famous for the tartufo, a hazelnut-and-chocolate gelato bomb. Have one in Piazza della Repubblica, see the castle where Joachim Murat was executed, and visit the Chiesetta di Piedigrotta, a church carved into a seaside cave.

Gerace

A medieval hill town above the Ionian coast, called the 'city of a hundred churches'. Its Norman cathedral, the largest in Calabria, is built with columns taken from ancient Locri. Walk the lanes to the ruined castle and Largo delle Tre Chiese.

Sila National Park

A high plateau of pine and beech forest, lakes and pastures, sometimes called the 'great forest of Italy'. See the 'Giants of the Sila', Calabrian black pines around 350 years old at Fallistro, and canoe on Lake Arvo. Camigliatello Silano is the base, with mushroom stalls in autumn.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

See the Riace Bronzes

In Reggio's National Archaeological Museum of Magna Graecia, the two Greek warriors stand in their own climate-controlled room. Allow time for the rest of the collection, from Locri's terracottas to the Philosopher's head.

Boat along the Costa degli Dei

From Tropea, small boats run to the coves around Capo Vaticano, including the Grotticelle beaches and Praia di Fuoco, with stops to snorkel in very clear water.

Taste 'nduja in Spilinga

The village above Tropea is the home of the soft, fiery spreadable pork salami. Visit a producer, and come in August for the 'nduja festival.

Visit the Amarelli licorice museum

In Rossano, the Amarelli family has made licorice since 1731. The free museum shows old equipment and documents, and the shop sells pure licorice in tins. In town, see the 6th-century Codex Purpureus Rossanensis.

Raft or hike the Raganello gorge

On the Pollino side, the river Raganello cuts a deep canyon below Civita. Cross the Devil's Bridge and walk into the gorge with a local guide.

Walk the Lungomare Falcomatà at sunset

Reggio's seafront promenade looks straight at Etna across the Strait. Join the evening passeggiata and look for the Fata Morgana mirage on hot, calm days.

See the Cattolica di Stilo

A tiny 10th-century Byzantine church with five little brick domes, perched on a slope below Monte Consolino above the town of Stilo. The frescoes inside are faded but beautiful.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Calabria, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Cipolla rossa di Tropea IGPSweet, mild red onions grown along the coast near Tropea, eaten raw in salads, in frittata, in jam and even in gelato.
PeperoncinoCalabrian chilli is in everything: dried, crushed, preserved in oil and in pasta sauces. Diamante holds a whole festival for it.
Fileja with 'ndujaHand-rolled pasta twisted around a knitting needle, from the Vibo Valentia area, usually served in a tomato sauce with 'nduja.

Cheese

Caciocavallo Silano DOPA pear-shaped stretched-curd cheese, hung in pairs to mature. Mild when young, sharp with age, and wonderful grilled.

Cured meats

'NdujaA soft, spicy pork salami from Spilinga, red with Calabrian chilli. Spread it on bread, melt it into pasta sauce or stir it into beans.
Soppressata di Calabria DOPA pressed salami of lean pork and lard, flavoured with chilli and black pepper. Some versions are mild, others very hot.

Wine

CiròThe region's best-known red, made from Gaglioppo grapes on the Ionian coast near Crotone. Light in colour but firm and spicy.

Sweets

Liquirizia di Calabria DOPLicorice root grows wild along the Ionian coast. The pure black extract is intense and slightly salty; try it in Rossano.
BergamotAlmost all the world's bergamot grows on the coast near Reggio. Its oil scents Earl Grey tea and perfume; locally it goes into marmalade, gelato and liqueur.
Tartufo di Pizzo IGPA ball of hazelnut and chocolate gelato with a liquid chocolate heart, rolled in cocoa. Invented in Pizzo in the 1950s.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

More legacy tables across Italy

Gran Caffè Renzelli

Cosenza

Since 1803

Run by the same family for seven generations and among the oldest cafés in Italy, on Corso Telesio near Cosenza's cathedral. It began as a sorbet maker; order coffee and one of the traditional cakes and sweets.

Bar Gelateria Dante

Pizzo

Since 1940

The bar in Piazza della Repubblica where, according to local tradition, the gelato maker Don Pippo shaped the first tartufo by hand in 1952 when he ran out of moulds. Eat one at a table in the square.

Gelato Cesare

Reggio Calabria

Since 1940

A kiosk on the Lungomare Falcomatà, run by the Destefano family for three generations and among Italy's most acclaimed gelaterie. Try bergamot, then walk the promenade.

Amarelli

Rossano

Since 1731

The family firm that has turned Calabrian licorice root into black pastilles for nearly three centuries. Its museum and shop sit in the family's 15th-century palazzo.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

An organic farm with a Michelin-starred kitchen

Dattilo, near Strongoli on the Ionian side, is the Ceraudo family's organic farm, with simple rooms, a pool under old olive trees and a restaurant run by chef Caterina Ceraudo using the farm's own produce.

See options

A bergamot farm in the Greek-speaking Aspromonte

Agriturismo Il Bergamotto, in a restored mill at Amendolea near Condofuri, grows bergamot and serves dishes made with it. It is a perfect base for walking in the Grecanica area.

A fisherman's house in Chianalea

In Scilla's waterfront quarter, small guesthouses and B&Bs occupy old fishermen's homes, some with balconies right over the sea. Fall asleep to the sound of the waves and eat swordfish a few steps away.

A mountain agriturismo in the Sila

Farm stays around Camigliatello Silano and Lorica serve wild mushrooms, potatoes of the Sila, caciocavallo and lamb, with forest walks and lake views from the door. In winter there is skiing nearby.

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Pentedattilo

An almost-abandoned village built into a rock formation shaped like a five-fingered hand, in the Aspromonte above Melito di Porto Salvo. A few artisans and a film festival bring it back to life in summer.

Stilo

A town on the slopes of Monte Consolino, birthplace of the philosopher Tommaso Campanella. Its jewel is the Cattolica, a perfect miniature Byzantine church with five domes, and the old town has Norman ruins and fine views of the Ionian.

Civita

An Arbëreshë village founded by Albanian refugees in the 15th century, with 'talking houses' whose chimneys and facades seem to have faces. Below it, the Devil's Bridge spans the Raganello gorge.

Le Castella

A 15th-century Aragonese fortress on a small island joined to the shore by a strip of land, near Isola di Capo Rizzuto. Swim and snorkel in the protected marine area around it, and eat fish in the village.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Peperoncino Festival

Diamante

Early to mid-September

Five days of chilli everything: tastings, a chilli-eating contest, cookery shows and music in the streets of a seaside town covered in murals.

Event info

Festa della Madonna della Consolazione

Reggio Calabria

Second Saturday of September

The city's patron is carried down from her hilltop basilica to the cathedral by porters at a run, cheered by crowds, followed by days of celebrations.

L'Affruntata

Vibo Valentia and nearby villages

Easter Sunday

Statues of the risen Christ, the Virgin and Saint John are carried at a run to 'meet' in the town square, as the Virgin's black veil is dropped.

Kaulonia Tarantella Festival

Caulonia

August

A week of tarantella, organetto, lira calabrese and bagpipe music in a hilltop town on the Ionian coast, with concerts, workshops and dancing in the streets until late.

Event info

Music, craft and custom

Calabria's folk music is the tarantella: fast, circular dances driven by organetto, tambourine, the zampogna bagpipe and the three-string lira calabrese, alive at village festivals and the Kaulonia Tarantella Festival. The region holds minorities with ancient roots: Arbëreshë villages like Civita and Lungro, founded by Albanian refugees in the 15th century, keep Byzantine-rite churches and their own language, while in the Aspromonte a handful of villages around Bova still speak Greko. Easter rites like the Affruntata are powerful theatre, and crafts include the woven blankets of Tiriolo and ceramics of Seminara.
