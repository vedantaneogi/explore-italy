The Centre

Marche

Renaissance Urbino, white Conero cliffs and hill towns facing the Adriatic.

White pebble beach and turquoise sea below the green cliffs of Monte Conero near SiroloPhoto: Luca Boldrini, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Marche

Le Marche, 'the Marches', is plural for good reason: it is a region of many small worlds packed between the Apennines and the Adriatic. Parallel valleys run down to the sea, each with hill towns lined up on its ridges: Urbino, the Renaissance court of Federico da Montefeltro; Recanati, where Leopardi gazed at his 'infinite' horizon; Ascoli Piceno, built of pale travertine. Behind them rise the Sibillini mountains, full of legends of sibyls and fairies.

The coast is a long string of family beaches, broken by the white limestone cliffs of Monte Conero, where coves like the Due Sorelle can only be reached by boat. Eating here is a joy: olive all'ascolana fried and served in paper cones, vincisgrassi baked pasta, a fish brodetto that changes recipe from port to port, and a cold glass of Verdicchio. Few foreign visitors make it here, which is part of the charm.

Capital
Ancona
Best time to go
June and September for the Conero beaches without the August crush; July and August for the opera festivals in Macerata and Pesaro and Ascoli's Quintana. Late spring is lovely in the Sibillini, and October and November bring truffle fairs around Acqualagna.
Getting there and around
Ancona Falconara airport has domestic and some European flights, with Bologna and Rome as bigger alternatives. Fast trains run along the Adriatic line through Pesaro, Ancona and San Benedetto del Tronto, and Ancona's port has ferries to Croatia and Greece. For Urbino, the hill towns and the Sibillini, a car or regional bus is needed.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Marche, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Urbino

A UNESCO-listed hill town that Federico da Montefeltro shaped into an ideal Renaissance city. His Palazzo Ducale, with its slender twin towers, houses the Galleria Nazionale delle Marche: Piero della Francesca's Flagellation, Raphael's La Muta. Visit Raphael's birthplace, then eat a crescia sfogliata among the students in Piazza della Repubblica.

Ascoli Piceno

A town built of creamy travertine, with a hundred towers once on its skyline. Piazza del Popolo is one of Italy's most elegant squares; have an anisetta at the Liberty-style Caffè Meletti, then eat olive all'ascolana, stuffed and fried, where they were invented. In summer come for the Quintana joust.

Riviera del Conero

Monte Conero's white limestone headland drops straight into a clear blue sea south of Ancona. Take a boat from Numana or Sirolo to the Due Sorelle beach, swim at Portonovo below its Romanesque church of Santa Maria, and eat moscioli, wild mussels harvested from the rocks, a Slow Food presidium.

Grotte di Frasassi

Discovered in 1971 in the gorge near Genga, this karst cave system has one of Europe's largest underground chambers, the Abisso Ancona, big enough to hold Milan Cathedral. Guided walks pass forests of stalagmites and petrified waterfalls. Nearby, the octagonal Tempio del Valadier sits in a cave mouth.

Recanati and Loreto

Recanati is the hometown of the poet Giacomo Leopardi: visit his family palazzo and library, and the hilltop that inspired L'Infinito. Just below, Loreto's basilica holds the Santa Casa, said to be the house of the Virgin Mary, carried here by angels, a pilgrimage site since the Middle Ages.

Monti Sibillini

A national park of high meadows and limestone peaks on the border with Umbria. Hike up to the Lago di Pilato, a glacial lake under Monte Vettore home to a tiny endemic red shrimp, or walk the Gola dell'Infernaccio. The villages around Montemonaco and Amandola are full of legends of the Sibyl's cave.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Take a boat to the Due Sorelle

Shuttle boats from Numana and Sirolo run in summer to the beach below the twin sea stacks of the Due Sorelle, reachable only by sea. Bring water and lunch; there are no facilities.

Hunt for truffles at Acqualagna

Acqualagna calls itself the truffle capital of Italy. Come for its national white truffle fair in October and November, or join a hunter and his dog in the woods any season.

Drive the Furlo Gorge

The old Via Flaminia runs through the gorge of the Candigliano, including a tunnel cut by Emperor Vespasian in 76 AD. Golden eagles nest in the cliffs; stop for a swim in the river pools.

Visit an accordion workshop in Castelfidardo

The world capital of the accordion since Paolo Soprani began making them in 1863. Visit the International Accordion Museum and a factory where they are still assembled by hand.

Watch paper being made in Fabriano

Fabriano has made paper since the 13th century and refined the watermark. At the Museo della Carta e della Filigrana, master papermakers still pull sheets by hand from a vat.

Eat brodetto in a different port

Ancona's brodetto has 13 kinds of fish, Porto Recanati's is coloured with wild saffron, San Benedetto's has green peppers and vinegar. Taste at least two and pick a side.

See Lorenzo Lotto's altarpieces

The Venetian painter spent years in the Marche, and his works fill churches and galleries in Recanati, Jesi, Loreto and Ancona. Jesi's Pinacoteca in Palazzo Pianetti has a fine group.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Marche, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Olive all'ascolanaLarge green Ascolana olives, pitted, stuffed with a mix of meats, coated in breadcrumbs and deep-fried; served hot in paper cones.
VincisgrassiThe Marche's rich baked pasta: layers of egg pasta with a ragù of chicken giblets, pork and veal, béchamel and Parmigiano, often with truffle.
BrodettoAdriatic fish stew made in every port with its own rule: Ancona, Porto Recanati, Fano and San Benedetto each guard a different recipe.
Moscioli di PortonovoWild mussels that grow on the rocks of the Conero coast, harvested by local fishermen from spring to autumn; a Slow Food presidium, best simply steamed.
Crescia sfogliataUrbino's flaky, lard-enriched flatbread, cooked on a griddle and filled with cured meats, cheese or wild greens.

Cheese

Casciotta d'Urbino DOPA small, soft cheese of sheep's and cow's milk from the Montefeltro hills, said to have been a favourite of Michelangelo.

Cured meats

Ciauscolo IGPA soft, spreadable pork salami from the Macerata hills, seasoned with garlic, pepper and fennel and lightly smoked; eaten on warm bread.

Wine

Verdicchio dei Castelli di JesiCrisp, almondy white from the hills around Jesi and Matelica, good young and surprisingly long-lived; the perfect match for brodetto.
Rosso ConeroA robust red made mostly from Montepulciano grapes on the slopes of Monte Conero, dark and spicy; drink with vincisgrassi or roast meats.

Coffee & spirits

Anisetta MelettiAscoli Piceno's anise liqueur, made since 1870; drink it 'con la mosca', with a coffee bean floating in the glass, at the Caffè Meletti.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

Agriturismo in the Sibillini foothills

Farm stays around Amandola, Sarnano and Montemonaco raise sheep, grow legumes and make pecorino; dinner is often the farm's own lamb, cheese and chickpeas, with the mountains outside the window.

Country house in the Montefeltro

Around Urbino and San Leo, old farmhouses have become family-run country houses with a pool and an orchard. They are a peaceful base for Renaissance towns, truffle woods and the Furlo gorge.

Fishermen's town by the Conero

Rent a room or apartment from a local family in Sirolo or Numana, where you can walk down to the beach in the morning, take the boat to the Due Sorelle, and eat moscioli in the piazza in the evening.

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Corinaldo

A hill town with intact 15th-century walls and a long, wide staircase, the Piaggia, climbing through the centre past the famous 'Pozzo della Polenta', the well at the heart of a local legend. The view from the walls takes in the whole Misa valley.

Offida

A small town of bobbin lace, with women working the tombolo on their doorsteps, and the Gothic church of Santa Maria della Rocca on the edge of a cliff. Taste Offida Pecorino and Passerina, two local white wines.

Gola del Furlo

A dramatic limestone gorge where the Via Flaminia passes through a Roman tunnel dug in 76 AD. Golden eagles nest on the cliffs; walk the trails up Monte Pietralata for a view down into the gorge.

Fermo

A handsome hill town above the coast with a vast Piazza del Popolo, Roman cisterns under the streets and a cathedral with a sea view. It is also a centre of the Marche shoe industry, with outlets in nearby towns.

Quintana di Ascoli

Ascoli Piceno

July and the first Sunday of August

Knights of the six districts charge at the Saracen-shaped target in a field outside town, after a parade of more than a thousand people in 15th-century costume.

Macerata Opera Festival

Macerata

July to August

Opera under the stars in the Sferisterio, a neoclassical arena built for a ball game, with remarkable acoustics and one of Italy's widest stages.

Rossini Opera Festival

Pesaro

August

Rossini's hometown stages his operas, including rarely performed ones, with top international singers, plus a production by the young voices of the Accademia Rossiniana.

Festa della Venuta

Loreto and across the Marche

Night of 9-10 December

Bonfires called focarazze light up the countryside to commemorate the angels carrying the Holy House to Loreto; pilgrims hold an all-night vigil at the basilica.

Music, craft and custom

The Marche gave Italy Rossini, Pergolesi and Spontini, the poet Leopardi and the painter Raphael, and it still loves opera, with summer festivals in Pesaro and Macerata. Castelfidardo has built accordions since 1863, Fabriano has made paper since the Middle Ages, and Offida and Ascoli keep the bobbin-lace tradition alive. Fermo and Macerata provinces make many of Italy's shoes. Folk music is the saltarello, danced to organetto and tambourine, and the dialect changes noticeably between the north, near Romagna, and the south, near Abruzzo.
