The South

Basilicata

Cave cities, Lucanian Dolomites and a slice of coast few people find.

Matera's Civita and cathedral bell tower glowing with lights at dusk above the cave houses of the SassiPhoto: Cristoforo64, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Basilicata

Basilicata, the old Lucania, is the instep of Italy's boot and one of the least visited regions in the country. That is its charm. Most of it is mountain and clay badland, with villages clinging to ridges, and it touches two seas: a short, dramatic stretch of Tyrrhenian cliffs at Maratea and a long Ionian beach of pine woods and Greek ruins at Metaponto.

Then there is Matera, a city carved into a ravine, whose cave dwellings went from national scandal in the 1950s to UNESCO site and European Capital of Culture in 2019. Beyond it, you'll find stone villages in the Lucanian Dolomites, the high wild beech woods of the Pollino and an extinct volcano covered in Aglianico vines. Food is frugal and fierce: crisp sweet peppers, chilli, sausage, hard bread and very good red wine.

Capital
Potenza
Best time to go
April to June and September to October are best for walking and for Matera, which can be very hot in August. July and August suit the Pollino's mountain trails and Maratea's coves.
Getting there and around
There is no airport: fly to Bari, about an hour from Matera, or to Naples for Potenza and Maratea. FAL trains link Bari with Matera, and Trenitalia serves Potenza and Maratea, but buses are sparse and a car is essential for the mountain villages.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Basilicata, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Matera

A city of two ravines, the Sasso Caveoso and Sasso Barisano, honeycombed with caves people lived in for thousands of years. Wander the stairways, visit a restored cave house and the frescoed rock church of Santa Maria de Idris, then cross the gorge to the Murgia park for the view back at sunset.

Maratea

Basilicata's only Tyrrhenian town, with 30 kilometres of cliffs, black-sand coves and caves. The old town has dozens of churches; above it, a 21-metre white statue of Christ the Redeemer looks out over the Gulf of Policastro. Swim at Cala Jannita or take a boat to the Grotta della Sciabica.

Castelmezzano and Pietrapertosa

Two stone villages tucked among the jagged sandstone peaks of the Lucanian Dolomites. Walk the Sentiero delle Sette Pietre between them, or fly across the valley at up to 120 km/h on the Volo dell'Angelo zip line. Pietrapertosa's Arab-Norman castle is carved into the rock.

Pollino National Park

Italy's largest national park, shared with Calabria, rises to more than 2,200 metres. Hike to Serra di Crispo to see the twisted pino loricato, the rare Bosnian pine, on the ridges, raft the Lao gorge, or visit the Arbëreshë villages of San Paolo Albanese and San Costantino Albanese.

Venosa

The birthplace of the Roman poet Horace, with an Aragonese castle, a Roman archaeological park, and the extraordinary unfinished abbey church of the Santissima Trinità, its roofless Norman nave built with stones from the Roman amphitheatre. Taste Aglianico in the local cellars.

Monte Vulture and Monticchio

An extinct volcano in the north, its slopes planted with Aglianico vines and chestnut woods. In the old crater lie the two Lakes of Monticchio, with the Abbey of San Michele clinging to the hillside above them. Nearby Melfi has Frederick II's castle and a fine museum.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Watch dawn over the Sassi

Walk to the Belvedere di Murgia Timone across the ravine before sunrise and watch the light hit Matera's caves and bell towers. On the way, explore the rock churches cut into the Murgia.

Fly the Volo dell'Angelo

Strapped into a harness, you zip on a steel cable between Pietrapertosa and Castelmezzano, high above the valley. Open from spring to autumn; book ahead.

Visit the Crypt of Original Sin

A cave church near Matera with 9th-century frescoes of the Creation and the Fall, so vivid it has been called the Sistine Chapel of rock art. Visits by reservation only.

Go down into the Palombaro Lungo

An enormous cistern under Matera's Piazza Vittorio Veneto, carved by hand and big enough to hold five million litres of water. Walkways lead along the waterline.

Taste Aglianico in a tuff cellar

In Barile and Rionero in Vulture, wine has long been aged in caves dug into the volcanic rock. Book a tasting at a family estate and pair it with local caciocavallo.

Cross the Tibetan bridge at Castelsaraceno

A footbridge of more than 580 metres strung high over a gorge in the Pollino-Lagonegrese area, among the longest of its kind in the world. Not for the faint-hearted.

Walk among Greek temples at Metaponto

On the Ionian coast, the fifteen Doric columns of the Tavole Palatine stand in farmland. Pair them with a swim on the long sandy beach backed by pine woods.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Basilicata, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Peperoni cruschiSun-dried sweet peppers from Senise, flash-fried until crisp and crumbled over pasta, eggs or baccalà. The region's signature crunch.
Pane di Matera IGPA big durum-wheat sourdough loaf, often shaped like a horn, with a thick crust and a yellow crumb that keeps for a week.
CrapiataA Matera soup of chickpeas, beans, lentils, broad beans and wheat, made together to celebrate the harvest, traditionally on 1 August.
Strascinati with mollicaHand-dragged pasta tossed with breadcrumbs fried in olive oil, peperoni cruschi and sometimes anchovies. Poor food at its best.
Melanzana rossa di Rotonda DOPA red-orange aubergine that looks like a tomato, grown in the Pollino and preserved under oil. Slightly bitter and fruity.

Cheese

Canestrato di Moliterno IGPA sharp sheep's and goat's milk cheese aged in wicker baskets in special cool rooms called fondaci.

Cured meats

LucanicaA spiced pork sausage so old that Roman writers named it after Lucania. Look for Lucanica di Picerno IGP, flavoured with fennel and chilli.

Wine

Aglianico del VultureA deep, tannic red from vines on the volcanic slopes of Monte Vulture. The Superiore is DOCG and ages beautifully.

Coffee & spirits

Amaro LucanoA bittersweet herbal liqueur created in Pisticci in 1894 and now one of Italy's best-known digestivi.

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

Tiri

Acerenza and Potenza

Since 1957

The Tiri family's bakery began in the hill town of Acerenza; grandson Vincenzo Tiri's panettone is now among Italy's most acclaimed. Visit the Potenza shop for leavened cakes all year round.

Website

Panificio Cifarelli

Matera

Since 1947

A third-generation bakery on Via Istria, among the few in Matera still with a wood-fired oven. Join the queue for a horn-shaped loaf of pane di Matera and some focaccia.

Website

Da Peppe

Rotonda

Since 1980

A family osteria in the Pollino, Slow Food-recommended, devoted to mountain cooking: red aubergines of Rotonda, local beans, mushrooms and lamb. The De Marco family also run a few guest rooms.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

Sleep in a cave in the Sassi

Sextantio Le Grotte della Civita has 18 cave rooms in Matera's oldest quarter, restored with bare stone, candlelight and simple antique furniture. Breakfast is served in a former rock church with views over the ravine.

See options

An albergo diffuso near Matera

Il Borgo Ritrovato in Montescaglioso spreads its rooms across restored historic houses in the old centre, a quiet base 18 km from the Sassi with a view over the Bradano valley.

A village base in the Pollino

Il Borgo Ospitale in Rotonda is an albergo diffuso set among the lanes of the old town, a good base for walks in the national park and dinners at the village osterie.

A farm stay in the Vulture

Family agriturismi on the slopes of Monte Vulture, around Rionero and Barile, offer rooms among the vines, Aglianico from their own cellars and dinners of handmade pasta, lamb and local caciocavallo.

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Craco

A medieval village on a clay ridge, abandoned after a landslide in 1963. Its Norman tower and empty palazzi have starred in films from The Passion of the Christ to Quantum of Solace. Visits are by guided tour, with hard hats.

Aliano

The village where the anti-fascist writer Carlo Levi was exiled in 1935-36, which he described in Christ Stopped at Eboli. See his house, the literary park and the 'calanchi', eerie white clay gullies all around.

Acerenza

A hill town known as the 'cathedral city', its great Romanesque cathedral rising high over the Bradano valley. Wander its walls for the views, then stop at the Tiri family's first bakery.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Festa della Bruna

Matera

2 July

Matera's patron, the Madonna della Bruna, rides through town on a papier-mâché float that the crowd tears to pieces at the end of the night, the 'strazzo', to take home as good-luck charms.

Event info

Maggio di Accettura

Accettura

Pentecost weekend (late May or June)

An ancient tree wedding: a huge oak from the Montepiano forest and a holly from the Gallipoli Cognato woods are hauled into town by oxen, joined, raised and climbed.

Carnevale di Tricarico

Tricarico

17 January to Shrove Tuesday

Men dressed as cows and bulls, with ribbons, masks and clanging cowbells, rush through the streets in a ritual 'transhumance' that opens on Sant'Antonio Abate's day.

Madonna Nera di Viggiano

Viggiano

First Sunday of May and first Sunday of September

Pilgrims carry the Black Madonna up to her mountain sanctuary in May and bring her back down in September, accompanied by harps and bagpipes.

Music, craft and custom

Basilicata's culture is rural and ritual. Spring 'tree feasts' like the Maggio of Accettura, the cow-and-bull masks of Tricarico's carnival and the procession of the Black Madonna of Viggiano echo far older beliefs. Viggiano was home to travelling harpists who played across Europe; the zampogna bagpipe still accompanies feast days. Carlo Levi's Christ Stopped at Eboli made the region's poverty famous, and Matera's caves have been a film set since Pasolini's Gospel According to Saint Matthew. Arbëreshë villages keep Albanian dress and language, and Matera's potters still make the rooster-shaped terracotta whistle, the cuccù.
