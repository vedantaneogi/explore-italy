The North

Liguria

A thin arc of coast where pastel villages cling between basil terraces and sea.

Vernazza's harbour and castle tower seen from the coastal trail to Corniglia, with prickly pears in the foregroundPhoto: Mathieu Chollet - MChollet, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Liguria

Liguria is a sliver of land squeezed between the Apennines and the sea, so steep that its villages seem stacked one on top of another. The Riviera di Levante, east of Genoa, has the postcard views: Portofino, Camogli and the five villages of the Cinque Terre, joined by cliff paths and dry-stone terraces. To the west, the Riviera di Ponente has wider beaches, olive groves and hilltop villages looking out to sea.

Genoa holds it all together. It's a proud old maritime republic with one of Europe's largest medieval centres, a dark maze of caruggi alleys running down to the port. Ligurians are famously frugal and wonderfully good cooks: pesto pounded from small-leaved basil, focaccia glistening with oil, farinata from a wood oven, and anchovies every way you can think of. Bring good shoes, because almost everything worth seeing involves stairs.

Capital
Genoa
Best time to go
Visit April to early June and mid-September to October for warm seas, quiet paths and open trattorias. July and August are hot and crowded, especially in the Cinque Terre. In winter the Riviera di Ponente stays mild and sunny, with mimosa in bloom by February.
Getting there and around
Genoa's Cristoforo Colombo airport is small. Pisa and Nice are good alternatives for either end of the coast. The coastal railway is the region's backbone: frequent regional trains link Genoa with Camogli, Santa Margherita, the Cinque Terre and La Spezia, and in the west Savona, Finale and Sanremo. Ferries cover the Gulf of Tigullio and the Cinque Terre in season, and having no car is an advantage here.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Liguria, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Genoa

Get lost in the caruggi, the narrow lanes that open onto marble churches and greengrocers. Walk Via Garibaldi, lined with the UNESCO-listed Palazzi dei Rolli, eat focaccia at nine in the morning like the locals, ride the Zecca–Righi funicular for a view over the port, and end the day in Boccadasse, the old fishermen's cove.

Cinque Terre

Five fishing villages, Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola and Riomaggiore, cling to cliffs above the sea, linked by train, boat and centuries-old paths. Hike the Sentiero Azzurro sections that are open, or climb to the Volastra and Corniglia vineyards for quieter views, and taste Sciacchetrà, the amber sweet wine made from dried grapes.

Portofino

A tiny harbour of ochre and rose houses, and expensive yachts. Arrive by boat from Santa Margherita, or on foot along the coastal path from Paraggi. Climb past the church of San Giorgio to Castello Brown and the lighthouse, then walk the wooded promontory paths to the abbey of San Fruttuoso, hidden in its own cove.

Camogli

Tall, painted houses crowd a pebble beach and a working fishing harbour. Camogli is lovelier and more lived-in than Portofino. Buy focaccia col formaggio, sit on the harbour wall at sunset and take the boat round the headland to San Fruttuoso or the Punta Chiappa rocks for a swim.

Porto Venere

At the tip of the Gulf of La Spezia, a wall of narrow tower-houses faces the harbour. A lane leads out to the striped church of San Pietro, perched on the rocks. Swim near Byron's Grotto, then take the short boat ride to the island of Palmaria for its trails and quiet coves.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Hike from Camogli to San Fruttuoso

A rugged two-and-a-half-hour trail over the Portofino promontory, with sections of chain-assisted rock, ends at a medieval abbey on a tiny beach. Take the boat back to Camogli.

Make pesto in a mortar

Book a class in Genoa where you pound DOP basil, Pra' basil if possible, with pine nuts, garlic and Fiore Sardo cheese in a marble mortar. Then eat it on trofie with potatoes and green beans.

Eat farinata from the wood oven

This chickpea pancake, crisp at the edges and creamy inside, comes out of copper pans at Genoa's sciamadde. Eat it hot, with plenty of black pepper and a glass of Vermentino.

Walk the Via dell'Amore and the high paths

The famous lovers' lane between Riomaggiore and Manarola has reopened after years of repairs. Combine it with the high-level Sentiero Verde Azzurro paths for vineyard views without the crowds.

Visit Genoa's aquarium and old port

Renzo Piano's redeveloped Porto Antico is home to one of Europe's largest aquariums and the Galata Museo del Mare. A harbour boat tour gives you a sea-level view of the city.

Swim at Baia del Silenzio

Sestri Levante's 'bay of silence' is a small curve of sand framed by painted houses and fishing boats. Go early in summer, or in late September when it lives up to its name.

Explore the Ponente hill villages

Drive inland from Bordighera and Ventimiglia to Dolceacqua, Apricale and Perinaldo. These stone villages sit among olive groves, have tiny wine bars, and look out across the hills to the Alps.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Liguria, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Pesto genoveseYoung basil, pine nuts, garlic, Parmigiano, Pecorino and Ligurian olive oil. Classic with trofie or trenette, potatoes and green beans.
Focaccia genoveseLow and dimpled, salty and slick with oil. Genoese dunk it in cappuccino for breakfast.
Focaccia di Recco col formaggio IGPTwo paper-thin sheets of dough with molten, tangy stracchino in between, baked until blistered. Eat it straight from the oven in Recco or Camogli.
FarinataA thin chickpea-flour pancake baked in huge copper pans in a wood oven, served hot with black pepper.
Acciughe di MonterossoAnchovies from the Cinque Terre, salted, marinated in lemon, fried or stuffed and baked. Fishermen still go out at night with lamps to catch them.
Cappon magroA towering festive salad of fish, seafood and vegetables over sea biscuits, dressed in a green herb sauce. It is famously elaborate to make.

Wine

SciacchetràA rare, amber sweet wine from the Cinque Terre, made from Bosco, Albarola and Vermentino grapes dried on racks.

Oil

Riviera Ligure DOP olive oilTaggiasca olives give a delicate, sweet oil with hints of almond. It's best poured raw over fish.

Sweets

Pandolce genoveseA dense, fragrant Christmas cake studded with candied fruit, raisins, pine nuts and fennel seeds.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

Pietro Romanengo fu Stefano

Genoa

Since 1780

A confectioner's shop with carved cabinets on Via Soziglia, still selling candied fruit, sugared almonds, rose syrup and violet pastilles, made by hand the way the Genoese aristocracy liked them.

Website

Fratelli Klainguti

Genoa

Since 1828

A Swiss-founded café on Piazza Soziglia. It closed in 2020 and was lovingly restored before reopening in 2025. Order the Falstaff brioche, which Verdi is said to have loved, with a cappuccino at the old counter.

Website

Caffè Pasticceria Balzola

Alassio

Since 1902

This family pastry café beside Alassio's Budello is the home of the baci di Alassio, two hazelnut-cocoa cookies joined with chocolate ganache. Take a box with you, or sit on the terrace with a coffee.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

An agriturismo above the Cinque Terre

Skip the crowded waterfront rooms and stay among the terraces near Volastra, Groppo or Campiglia. Farm stays there produce wine, lemons and honey, and the paths start at the gate.

A harbour flat in Camogli

Rent an apartment in one of the tall painted houses on Via Garibaldi. You'll fall asleep to waves on the pebbles, wake to the fishing boats coming in, and buy focaccia downstairs for breakfast.

An olive farm in the Ponente

In the valleys behind Imperia and Sanremo, family olive estates rent rooms among the Taggiasca trees. Many will show you around the mill in November, when the new oil is pressed.

Day trips

Out in the morning, back for dinner.

Day trips from every major city

Genoa to Camogli and San Fruttuoso

Spend the morning in Camogli's harbour, take the boat to the abbey cove for a swim and lunch, and catch a train back for dinner.

30 min by train, then boat

La Spezia to Lerici and Tellaro

Visit the Gulf of Poets, which Byron and Shelley loved. Lerici has a castle and a seafront promenade, and Tellaro a pink fishing hamlet on the rocks.

About 30 min by bus

Sanremo to Dolceacqua

Cross the hump-backed bridge that Monet painted, climb to the Doria castle, and taste Rossese di Dolceacqua in a cellar bar.

About 30 min by car or bus

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Dolceacqua

A dark-stone village in the Nervia valley, built in layers below the ruined Doria castle, with a single-arched medieval bridge that Monet painted in 1884. Taste the local Rossese red and the michetta, a sweet bun with a saucy backstory.

Tellaro

Squeezed onto a rocky point at the end of the Gulf of La Spezia, Tellaro is a knot of pink and ochre houses and steps leading down to the sea. Legend says an octopus once rang the church bell to warn of pirates.

Apricale

A Ponente hill village spiralling up to its castle. Its walls are painted with murals by visiting artists, and its piazza is one of the prettiest on the Riviera. Come for an evening meal with views over the olive groves.

Noli

Once a tiny independent maritime republic, Noli still has its medieval towers, arcaded lanes and the Romanesque church of San Paragorio. Fishermen sell their catch straight from the beach in the morning.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Sagra del Pesce

Camogli

Second Sunday of May

A giant frying pan, four metres across, is set up on the harbour and thousands of portions of fried fish are handed out to honour San Fortunato, the fishermen's patron saint.

Stella Maris

Camogli and Punta Chiappa

First Sunday of August

A procession of boats sails to the Madonna's altar at Punta Chiappa. At dusk, thousands of small candles are set afloat on the sea to drift back to shore.

Festa di San Giovanni Battista

Genoa

24 June

Genoa's patron saint is celebrated with a procession to the old port, the blessing of the sea, and bonfires and music in the historic centre the night before.

Festival della Canzone Italiana

Sanremo

February

Italy's most-watched song contest takes over the Teatro Ariston and the whole town for a week of crowds, celebrities and late-night parties.

Music, craft and custom

Liguria is a seafaring culture. Genoese, a lilting dialect full of Arabic and Spanish loanwords, still echoes in the markets and the songs of Fabrizio De André, whose album 'Crêuza de mä' is written entirely in it. Trallalero, polyphonic singing by all-male squads from the Genoese docks, is still performed. Crafts include Zoagli silk velvet, slate from the Fontanabuona valley, and the filigree silver of Campo Ligure. Genoa also gave the world blue jeans: 'bleu de Gênes' was the sailors' sturdy cotton.
