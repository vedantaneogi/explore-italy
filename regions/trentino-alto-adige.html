The North  /  Trentino-Alto Adige / Südtirol

Trentino-Alto Adige

Pale Dolomite spires, apple orchards and dumplings in three languages.

The Sassolungo and Sassopiatto peaks glowing orange at sunset above the Alpe di Siusi meadowsPhoto: Wolfgang Moroder, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

  1. Home
  2. Regions
  3. Trentino-Alto Adige

Two provinces share this mountain region. Trentino, to the south, is Italian-speaking, and Alto Adige/Südtirol, to the north, is mostly German-speaking and was Austrian until 1919. Between them they hold the most theatrical part of the Dolomites: pale limestone towers that glow pink at sunset, a phenomenon the Ladin people call enrosadira. Below the peaks are meadows dotted with hay barns, castles on every spur, and valleys lined with apple orchards and vineyards.

Life here mixes Alpine and Mediterranean habits. In Bolzano you might eat speck and Knödel at lunch and drink an espresso under the arcades afterwards. Merano has palm trees and a spa, and Lake Garda's northern tip is lined with olive groves and windsurfers. Infrastructure is superb, with good trains, buses, cable cars and hiking huts, which makes it one of the easiest places in Italy to explore properly without a car.

Capital
Trento
Best time to go
Come late June to September for hiking, open huts and summer lifts. September and October are golden, with Törggelen wine-and-chestnut feasts. Late November to early January is Christmas-market season, and the ski season runs December to April. Lifts and many huts close from mid-April to early June.
Getting there and around
Verona and Innsbruck airports are about 1h30 to 2h from Bolzano, and Bolzano itself has a few seasonal flights. The Brenner railway links Verona, Rovereto, Trento and Bolzano with fast trains from Milan, Rome, Munich and Innsbruck, and branch lines run to Merano and up the Val Venosta. Regional buses reach almost every valley, and many hotels include a guest transport pass.

Places to know

Where to spend your days in Trentino-Alto Adige, from the big names to the towns locals choose for a Sunday.

Val Gardena and the Seceda ridge

Ortisei is the base for the Ladin valley of woodcarvers. From there, ride the cable car to Seceda, where the grassy Odle ridge breaks off into a row of jagged spires. Walk down via the Pieralongia meadows and their huts, or cross to the Alpe di Siusi, Europe's largest high-altitude pasture.

Bolzano

The German-speaking capital of South Tyrol, with arcaded Laubengasse, an open-air fruit market on Piazza delle Erbe and Ötzi the Iceman in the archaeology museum. Ride the cable cars to the Renon plateau to see the earth pyramids, or walk the Oswald promenade above the vineyards at dusk.

Merano

A Habsburg-era spa town of Art Nouveau villas, palms and mountain views. Walk the Tappeiner promenade, soak in the Terme Merano pools, and lose an afternoon in the terraced Gardens of Trauttmansdorff Castle, where Empress Sisi wintered. In autumn the Waalwege, paths along old irrigation channels, smell of ripening apples.

Trento

Trento hosted the Council of Trent in the 1500s and kept the frescoed façades to prove it. Look at the Piazza Duomo and Neptune fountain, then see the medieval Months cycle in the Castello del Buonconsiglio. MUSE, Renzo Piano's science museum, is great with kids, and Trentodoc sparkling wine flows at every aperitivo.

Lago di Braies

A jade-green lake under the sheer wall of the Croda del Becco, with wooden rowing boats tied to a little boathouse. Walk the flat 3.5-km loop early in the morning. In summer, private-car access is restricted at peak hours, so come by bus or book a parking slot.

Riva del Garda

At Lake Garda's narrow northern tip, cliffs drop straight into the water and the afternoon Ora wind brings out windsurfers and sailors. Climb to the Bastione for the view, swim from the pebble beaches, and ride a bike along the cantilevered cliff path at Limone, just down the shore.

Things to do

Experiences that only make sense here.

Go hut-to-hut in the Dolomites

Walk a few days of the Alta Via 1 or the Puez-Odle circuit, sleeping in rifugi with bunk rooms, hot showers and dinners of polenta and goulash. Book huts months ahead for July and August.

Join Törggelen in autumn

From October to November, farm taverns around Bressanone and the Isarco valley serve new wine, roast chestnuts, speck, sauerkraut and Krapfen. Walk there along the Keschtnweg chestnut trail.

Meet Ötzi the Iceman

The South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology in Bolzano displays the 5,300-year-old glacier mummy in a chilled cell, along with his copper axe, grass cloak and bearskin cap.

Climb a via ferrata

Iron ladders and cables make Dolomite summits possible for fit hikers. Hire a guide in Corvara or Canazei for a first route, such as the Brigata Tridentina on the Sella group.

Ride the Renon railway

Take the cable car up from Bolzano, then the little narrow-gauge tram across the Renon plateau to Collalbo. From there, walk to the earth pyramids, clay pillars topped with boulders.

Cycle the Val Venosta

Take the train up to Malles, then roll downhill for about 60 km on the cycle path to Merano, through apple orchards and past castles. You can drop rented bikes at any station.

Visit the Christmas markets

Bolzano's on Piazza Walther is the biggest, Merano's runs along the river promenade, and Bressanone's fills the cathedral square. Drink Glühwein from the souvenir mug and eat Kiachl.

What to eat and drink

The flavours of Trentino-Alto Adige, as you would order them at the counter or the table.

Plates

Canederli / KnödelBread dumplings bound with egg and milk and flavoured with speck, cheese or spinach. Served in broth or with melted butter and Parmesan.
SchlutzkrapfenHalf-moon rye ravioli filled with spinach and quark, drowned in brown butter and chives. Every South Tyrolean grandmother has her own version.
StrangolapretiTrentino's 'priest-stranglers': spinach-and-bread gnocchi in sage butter. The name comes from a story about greedy clergy at the Council of Trent.

Cheese

Puzzone di Moena DOPA pungent washed-rind cheese from the Fassa and Fiemme valleys, much milder than it smells and wonderful melted.

Cured meats

Speck Alto Adige IGPHam cured with juniper, lightly smoked and air-dried for months. Slice it thick on a wooden board with rye crispbread and a glass of Vernatsch.

Wine

TrentodocBottle-fermented mountain sparkling wine from Trentino's Chardonnay and Pinot Nero: crisp, fresh and the region's favourite aperitivo.
Lagrein and GewürztraminerTwo South Tyrolean classics: the dark, violet-scented Lagrein red from Bolzano, and the aromatic Gewürztraminer white, which takes its name from the village of Tramin.

Coffee & spirits

Grappa trentinaTrentino distillers make clean, aromatic grappa from Teroldego, Nosiola and Müller-Thurgau pomace. It's served after a big dinner.

Sweets

ApfelstrudelThin pastry rolled around local apples, raisins, pine nuts and cinnamon. Eat it warm with vanilla sauce in a mountain hut.

Legacy tables

Historic restaurants, cafés and bakeries that have been feeding locals for generations.

More legacy tables across Italy

Osteria a le Due Spade

Trento

Since 1545

Opened to feed delegates to the Council of Trent, it is a tiny, refined dining room steps from the cathedral with an eighteenth-century pine Stube. Expect creative Trentino cooking, such as ricotta gnocchi and lake fish.

Website

Wirtshaus Vögele

Bolzano

Since 1840

First recorded in 1277 and run by the Kamaun family since 1840, this is Bolzano's 'living room': panelled Stuben, Knödel trios, goulash and Kaiserschmarrn, with locals at the Stammtisch.

Website

Batzen Häusl

Bolzano

Since 1404

An old Teutonic Order tavern, an artists' haunt for centuries, now home to a microbrewery. Drink house-brewed beer in the garden with hearty Tyrolean plates.

Stay like a local

Farm stays, village houses and family homes where you live alongside the people who belong here.

How local stays work

A Roter Hahn farm stay

South Tyrol's 'Red Rooster' label certifies working farms with a handful of rooms or apartments. Collect eggs, help with the hay, and eat the farm's own apple juice, speck and cheese at breakfast.

See options

Agritur Maso il Gioco, Balbido

A family farm stay in Balbido, the 'painted village' of the Giudicarie, a short walk from Rango, one of Italy's most beautiful villages. Lunches use the farm's own produce, and in December you can walk to Rango's Christmas market in its old courtyards.

See options

Sleep in a Dolomite rifugio

Huts such as Rifugio Genova (Schlüterhütte), beneath the Odle above Val di Funes, offer bunks, Knödel dinners and sunrise on the peaks. Book ahead for summer weekends, and bring a sleeping-bag liner.

Hidden gems

Lesser-known corners that reward the detour.

Hidden gems across Italy

Val di Funes

A quiet farming valley facing the Odle spires. Its little Baroque church of St. Johann in Ranui, alone in a meadow, has become one of the Dolomites' most loved images. Come early, walk the Adolf Munkel trail, and eat at a valley Gasthof.

Lago di Tovel

Hidden in the Brenta Dolomites in the Adamello-Brenta Nature Park, this lake was famous until the 1960s for turning blood-red in summer from algae. Today it is a calm blue-green, with an easy forest loop around its shore.

Glorenza

One of the smallest towns in the Alps, Glorenza (Glurns) still sits entirely within its sixteenth-century walls, with three gate towers and arcaded lanes. In autumn, walk the walls and eat in an old Gasthaus in the Val Venosta.

Castel Thun

This fairy-tale castle above the Val di Non's apple orchards has its rooms intact, including the pine-panelled Bishop's room. On a clear day you can see from the Brenta Dolomites to the Adige valley.

Festivals

When to time your visit.

The festival calendar

Christmas markets

Bolzano, Merano, Bressanone, Brunico, Vipiteno, Trento

Late November to 6 January

The markets, with wooden stalls, carved nativity figures, mulled wine and strudel, are among the best in Europe. Smaller valley markets, such as Rango's in Trentino, are more intimate.

Meraner Traubenfest

Merano

Mid-October

Merano's grape festival dates back to 1886. There are brass bands and Schützen in traditional dress, and a parade of floats decorated with grapes and apples through the spa town.

I Suoni delle Dolomiti

Mountain meadows and huts across Trentino

Late June to August

Concerts high in the mountains: audiences hike up to listen to world-class musicians playing in pastures and beside huts, sometimes at dawn.

Herz-Jesu-Feuer

Mountainsides across South Tyrol

Sunday after the feast of the Sacred Heart, June

At nightfall, hundreds of bonfires shaped like hearts, crosses and symbols blaze on the slopes, a tradition dating back to the wars against Napoleon.

Music, craft and custom

This is the only Italian region with three living languages: Italian, German and, in the Gardena, Badia and Fassa valleys, Ladin, an ancient Rhaeto-Romance language with its own schools and newspapers. Val Gardena's woodcarvers have shaped saints, toys and nativity figures for centuries. Village brass bands, the Musikkapellen, play at every feast, and the Schützen, traditional riflemen's companies, march in feathered hats. In early December, Krampus runs send horned, fur-clad devils through the streets, and Ladin valleys still hold their own carnival masks and harvest rituals.
