Experiences  /  Festivals & events

A year of feast days, races and masks

Bareback horse races, orange battles, flower carpets and candlelit processions: plan your trip around one of Italy's great festivals and you will never forget it.

Racehorses' hooves pounding the packed earth of Piazza del Campo during the Palio di SienaPhoto: Roberto Vicario, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons

In this guide

  1. The festival calendar, month by month
  2. Six festivals worth building a trip around
  3. Arts, film and food fairs

Italian festivals are not put on for visitors. Most are centuries-old rituals of rivalry and devotion, run by neighbourhoods, guilds and confraternities who rehearse all year. In Siena, people are baptised into their contrada; in Gubbio, the men who carry the Ceri train like athletes; in Catania, hundreds of thousands follow the relics of St Agatha through the night. Watching, you are a guest at someone else's party.

That makes them unforgettable, and a little chaotic. Hotels fill months ahead, streets close and timetables slip. Below is a month-by-month calendar of the big ones, the headline events worth building a trip around, and the arts and food fairs that fill the gaps. Many dates follow Easter or the saints' calendar, so always check the year's programme before you book.

The festival calendar, month by month

Fixed dates repeat every year; moveable ones are tied to Easter, Carnival or a weekend, so confirm locally.

Sant'Antonio Abate and the Mamuthones

Mamoiada, Sardinia

16-17 January

Bonfires burn across inland Sardinia, and in Mamoiada the Mamuthones make their first outing of the year: men in black sheepskins and dark wooden masks, shaking heavy cowbells in a slow, eerie march.

Sardinia guide

Festa di Sant'Agata

Catania, Sicily

3-5 February

One of the largest religious festivals in the world. Devotees in white robes haul the saint's silver carriage through the city for two days and nights, with fireworks, giant candles and torrone stalls.

Sicily guide

Carnevale di Venezia

Venice, Veneto

Late January to Shrove Tuesday

Masks, costumed balls and the Flight of the Angel from the Campanile in St Mark's Square. The best moments are early morning, when costumed Venetians pose in empty campi. In 2027 it ends on 9 February.

Veneto guideOfficial programme

Carnevale di Viareggio

Viareggio, Tuscany

February weekends

Giant papier-mache floats, some as tall as a building, satirise politicians and celebrities as they roll along the seafront promenade over several weekends.

Tuscany guide

Battle of the Oranges

Ivrea, Piedmont

Carnival Sunday to Shrove Tuesday

For three days, teams on foot pelt armoured carts with oranges in a mock revolt against a medieval tyrant. Spectators who wear the red Phrygian cap are off-limits; everyone else is fair game.

Piedmont guideStorico Carnevale di Ivrea

Processione dei Misteri

Trapani, Sicily

Good Friday

Twenty groups of carved wooden statues of the Passion are carried through the old town, swaying to brass bands, for around 24 hours from Good Friday afternoon.

Sicily guide

Scoppio del Carro

Florence, Tuscany

Easter Sunday morning

An ornate cart packed with fireworks is pulled by white oxen to the Duomo; at the Gloria, a dove-shaped rocket shoots down a wire from the altar and sets it off in a roar of smoke and bangs.

Tuscany guide

Corsa dei Ceri

Gubbio, Umbria

15 May

Three wooden towers topped by saints, each around 280 kg, are raced through the streets and up Monte Ingino at full tilt by teams of bearers in coloured shirts.

Umbria guideFesta dei Ceri

Infiorata di Noto

Noto, Sicily

Third weekend of May

Via Nicolaci is carpeted in pictures made from flower petals, framed by Baroque balconies. Spello in Umbria and Genzano near Rome hold their own spectacular infiorate for Corpus Domini in late spring.

Sicily guideInfiorata di Noto

La Biennale di Venezia

Venice, Veneto

May to November

The world's most famous contemporary art show alternates yearly with the Architecture Biennale, spreading through the Giardini, the Arsenale and palazzi across the city.

Veneto guideLa Biennale

Calcio Storico Fiorentino

Florence, Tuscany

June, final 24 June

Part rugby, part wrestling, in 16th-century costume on a sand pitch in Piazza Santa Croce. Four neighbourhoods fight it out, with the final on 24 June, the feast of St John.

Tuscany guide

Palio di Siena

Siena, Tuscany

2 July and 16 August

Ten horses, bareback riders and three laps of the Campo in about 90 seconds. The days before are full of trial races, blessings of the horses in church and long outdoor contrada dinners.

Tuscany guideIl Palio

Festino di Santa Rosalia

Palermo, Sicily

14-15 July

A towering triumphal cart carries Palermo's patron saint, who saved the city from plague in 1624, down the Cassaro to the sea, followed by fireworks over the Foro Italico.

Sicily guide

Festa del Redentore

Venice, Veneto

Third weekend of July

Venetians tie their boats together in the Bacino di San Marco, eat dinner on the water and watch a huge fireworks display. On Sunday a bridge of boats links the Zattere with Palladio's Redentore church.

Veneto guide

La Notte della Taranta

Melpignano, Puglia

Late August

The summer-long pizzica festival in Salento's villages ends with a free all-night concert for tens of thousands in Melpignano.

Puglia guideFestival site

Venice International Film Festival

Lido di Venezia, Veneto

Late August to early September

The oldest film festival in the world, on the Lido. Public tickets are sold for many screenings, and stars arrive by water taxi along the red carpet.

Veneto guideBiennale Cinema

Regata Storica

Venice, Veneto

First Sunday of September

A water parade of 16th-century-style boats and costumed gondoliers, followed by fierce rowing races down the Grand Canal.

Veneto guideRegata Storica

Macchina di Santa Rosa

Viterbo, Lazio

3 September, evening

A 30-metre illuminated tower is carried on the shoulders of about a hundred facchini through the dark medieval streets, with the lights turned off around them.

Lazio guideMacchina di Santa Rosa

Barcolana

Trieste, Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Second Sunday of October

Probably the most crowded sailing regatta on the planet, with often more than 1,800 boats jostling on one start line in the Gulf of Trieste, with a week of parties along the waterfront.

Friuli-Venezia Giulia guideBarcolana

Alba White Truffle Fair

Alba, Piedmont

October to early December

Weekends of truffle market, tastings and cooking shows, plus a donkey palio. The 2026 edition, the 96th, runs from 10 October to 6 December.

Piedmont guideFiera del Tartufo

Lucca Comics & Games

Lucca, Tuscany

Late October to 1 November

Europe's biggest comics and gaming festival takes over the walled city, with cosplayers queuing for pastries under Renaissance ramparts.

Tuscany guideLucca Comics & Games

Autumnia

Figline e Incisa Valdarno, Tuscany

Early November

A free farming, food and environment fair between Chianti and the Pratomagno: a market of local producers, a farmyard of piglets and calves, and guided visits to the Teatro Garibaldi and Palazzo Pretorio. The 2026 edition runs 6-8 November.

Tuscany guideAutumnia

Festa della Salute

Venice, Veneto

21 November

A temporary bridge of boats crosses the Grand Canal to the domed Salute church, built to give thanks for the end of the 1630 plague. Venetians light candles and eat castradina, a smoked mutton stew.

Veneto guide

Prima della Scala

Milan, Lombardy

7 December

La Scala's season opens on the feast of Sant'Ambrogio with a black-tie gala, while the Oh Bej! Oh Bej! street fair runs around the Castello Sforzesco.

Lombardy guide

Christmas markets of Alto Adige

Bolzano, Merano, Bressanone, Brunico, Vipiteno

Late November to early January

Wooden huts, mulled wine, Zelten fruit bread and handmade decorations in the Alpine towns of South Tyrol, with snowy peaks behind.

Trentino-Alto Adige guideSouth Tyrol Christmas markets

Six festivals worth building a trip around

Palio di Siena

Siena, 2 July and 16 August

2 July and 16 August

Not a show but the climax of a year of passion for Siena's seventeen contrade. Stand in the centre of the Campo for free (arrive hours early, with water) or pay for a balcony seat. Don't miss the historical parade before the race.

Tuscany guideIl Palio

Carnevale di Venezia

Venice, before Lent

Ends Shrove Tuesday

Rent a costume or simply buy a handmade mask from a workshop, not a stall. The first weekend has the Festa Veneziana on the water; the final days bring the crowds. Midweek and early mornings are magical.

Veneto guideCarnival programme

Battle of the Oranges

Ivrea, Carnival

Carnival Sunday to Tuesday

The messiest festival in Italy: tonnes of oranges flying through the air in the town's squares. Watch from behind the nets or buy a red cap to stay out of the fight. Wear old clothes either way.

Piedmont guideCarnevale di Ivrea

Corsa dei Ceri

Gubbio, 15 May

15 May

From dawn the town is a sea of yellow, blue and black shirts. The Ceri are raised in Piazza Grande at midday and paraded all afternoon, then raced at full tilt through the streets and up the mountain to the basilica of St Ubaldo in the early evening.

Umbria guideFesta dei Ceri

Regata Storica

Venice, first Sunday of September

First Sunday of September

The Grand Canal at its most glorious: gilded ceremonial boats, rowers in Renaissance costume and the gondolino races cheered from every palazzo window. Book a canal-side table or join the crowds on the Rialto.

Veneto guideRegata Storica

Christmas in South Tyrol

Late November to early January

Late Nov to early Jan

Bolzano's market fills Piazza Walther with wooden chalets; Merano's runs along the river promenade; Bressanone's sits beneath the cathedral. Ride the cable car to Renon for snowy views and an early dinner of dumplings.

Trentino-Alto Adige guideMarkets and dates

Arts, film and food fairs

Big cultural events that bring a city to life for weeks, not days.

Biennale Arte

Venice

Even years, May-November

The 2026 edition, the 61st, runs from 9 May to 22 November. Architecture takes over in odd-numbered years, with dance, theatre and music festivals in between.

Veneto guideBiennale Arte 2026

Venice Film Festival

Lido di Venezia

Late Aug-early Sept

The 2026 festival ran 2-12 September. Tickets for public screenings go on sale in August.

Veneto guideBiennale Cinema

Festival dei Due Mondi

Spoleto, Umbria

Late June-July

Founded in 1958, this festival of opera, theatre, dance and music fills the hill town's theatres and squares from late June into July.

Umbria guideSpoleto Festival

Umbria Jazz

Perugia, Umbria

July

Ten days of jazz in July with free stages in the medieval squares, plus a winter edition in Orvieto at New Year.

Umbria guideUmbria Jazz

Sagre

Everywhere, all summer and autumn

June-November

Village food festivals devoted to one dish or ingredient, from chestnuts to wild boar to fried fish, with long tables, local wine and a dance band. Look for posters on the roadside.

Good to know

  • Book accommodation as soon as you have dates: Siena in early July, Venice at Carnival and Gubbio on 15 May fill up months ahead.
  • Many moveable feasts depend on Easter; in 2027 Easter Sunday falls on 28 March, with Carnival ending on 9 February.
  • At crowded events, agree on a meeting point, keep bags small and drink plenty of water; you may not be able to leave the Campo during the Palio.
  • Patron saints' days often mean shops and offices close in that town; check before planning museum visits.
  • Respect the ritual: festivals like the Palio and Sant'Agata are deeply serious to locals, so don't push into processions for photos.
