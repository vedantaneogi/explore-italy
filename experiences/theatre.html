Experiences  /  Theatre & opera houses

Velvet boxes, Greek stone and Sicilian knights

Gilded opera houses, two-thousand-year-old theatres still selling tickets, and puppet knights in Palermo. Here is where to take your seat.

The red and gold tiers of boxes rising to the painted ceiling of Teatro di San Carlo in NaplesPhoto: Sony photographer, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

In this guide

  1. The great opera houses
  2. Ancient stages, still in use

Italy built the modern theatre. The horseshoe of boxes rising to a painted ceiling, the proscenium arch, the idea of a stage set with streets vanishing into perspective: all were worked out here between Palladio's Vicenza and the Bourbon court in Naples. Almost every town of any size has its own little teatro, often restored and busy again; in July 2026 ten of them, in central Italy, joined the World Heritage list.

Older still are the Greek and Roman theatres of the south, where plays and concerts go on each summer with the sea or Etna behind the stage. And for something nobody else does, there is commedia dell'arte with its masked stock characters, and the Opera dei Pupi, Sicily's armoured marionettes. Book the big houses online; for the small ones, just turn up at the box office.

The great opera houses

All of these offer guided or self-guided visits by day, which is the best way to see the auditorium if you cannot get a ticket for a performance.

Teatro alla Scala

Milan, Lombardy

Opened 1778

Opened in 1778, La Scala is opera's most famous stage and its toughest audience. The season opens every 7 December, the feast of Milan's patron saint Ambrose, with the city's grandest night out. The museum lets you look into the red-and-gold auditorium from a box when there is no rehearsal, and gallery seats can be surprisingly affordable.

Lombardy guideSeason and tickets

Teatro La Fenice

Venice, Veneto

Opened 1792

The Phoenix lived up to its name: opened in 1792, burned in 1836, rebuilt, burned again in 1996 and reopened in 2003 in every gilded detail. Verdi premiered Rigoletto and La Traviata here. Take the audio tour by day, or come for the New Year's Day concert broadcast across Italy.

Veneto guideBook La Fenice

Teatro di San Carlo

Naples, Campania

Opened 1737

Built for the Bourbon king Charles in 1737, four decades before La Scala, San Carlo is the oldest opera house in Europe still in use. Tier upon tier of boxes, a royal box crowned in gold and blue and red velvet everywhere. Guided tours run most days.

Campania guideSan Carlo tickets and tours

Teatro Massimo

Palermo, Sicily

Opened 1897

Italy's largest opera house, opened in 1897, with a staircase guarded by bronze lions. Film fans know its steps from the final scene of The Godfather Part III. The guided tour includes the Royal Box and the frescoed Pompeian Room, with its strange, echoing acoustics.

Sicily guideTickets and tours

Teatro Regio di Parma

Parma, Emilia-Romagna

Opened 1829

Built for Duchess Marie Louise in 1829, the Regio is the heart of Verdi country, and the regulars in the top gallery, the loggione, are famous for booing any tenor who cracks a high note. Come in autumn for the Festival Verdi.

Emilia-Romagna guideTeatro Regio

Teatro Olimpico

Vicenza, Veneto

Opened 1585

Palladio's last design, completed by Scamozzi and opened in 1585 with Oedipus Rex. It is the oldest surviving indoor theatre with its original stage set: seven streets of Thebes, built in wood and stucco, that seem to run away for hundreds of metres. Plays and concerts still take place here.

Veneto guideTeatro Olimpico

Ancient stages, still in use

Summer evenings, ancient stone and live performance. Bring a cushion and a layer for when the sun goes down.

Greek theatre of Syracuse

Sicily

INDA season: May-July

Aeschylus may have staged his plays here in the 5th century BC. Since 1914 the INDA foundation has put on Greek tragedies and comedies every season, from May into early July, cut into the limestone hill with the sea glinting beyond.

Sicily guideINDA tickets

Ancient theatre of Taormina

Sicily

Summer concerts and film festival

Greek in origin and rebuilt by the Romans, it frames the Ionian coast and the cone of Etna in one view. Each summer it hosts concerts, opera, ballet and the Taormina Film Festival: an unforgettable place to watch the sun set before a show.

Sicily guide

Arena di Verona

Veneto

Opera season: June-Sept

A Roman amphitheatre from the 1st century AD, staging grand opera since 1913 and rock concerts too. Aida with real-scale sets under the stars is the classic; as dusk falls, the audience lights small candles before the overture.

Veneto guideBook the Arena

Baths of Caracalla

Rome, Lazio

Summer season

Rome's opera company moves outdoors every summer, staging opera and ballet in front of the towering brick ruins of the emperor's baths. Sit with a picnic beforehand in the gardens as the swifts wheel overhead.

Lazio guideCaracalla summer season

Segesta's hilltop theatre

Sicily

Summer festival

High on Monte Barbaro, with a view over hills to the sea, Segesta's Greek theatre hosts a summer festival of drama and music. Get there early to walk up past the temple.

Sicily guide

Masks and marionettes

Italy's popular theatre traditions are still performed, and still very funny.

Commedia dell'arte

Born in the Veneto in the 1500s

Since 1545

The first known contract for a professional acting company was signed in Padua in 1545. Masked stock characters, Arlecchino the hungry servant, Pantalone the miser, the pompous Dottore, improvised their way across Europe. See it at Milan's Piccolo Teatro, whose Arlecchino has toured since 1947, or visit Goldoni's house in Venice.

Veneto guidePiccolo Teatro di Milano

Opera dei Pupi

Sicily

UNESCO-listed 2008

Armoured marionettes re-enact the battles of Charlemagne's paladins, Orlando and Rinaldo, against the Saracens, with clanging swords and stamping feet. Palermo's pupi are light and nimble; Catania's are taller and much heavier. UNESCO-listed in 2008, and still performed by families like the Cuticchio in Palermo.

Sicily guideFigli d'Arte Cuticchio

Pulcinella and the guarattelle

Naples, Campania

Street shows in the old centre

The white-clad, black-masked Pulcinella is Naples' anarchic everyman, half fool, half philosopher. Glove-puppet shows called guarattelle still pop up in the city's squares, with the puppeteer speaking through a reed in his mouth to give Pulcinella his squawk.

Campania guide

Museo delle Marionette

Palermo, Sicily

Palermo

The Antonio Pasqualino International Puppet Museum holds thousands of puppets from Sicily and around the world, with painted scenery, battle-scarred Sicilian knights and a small theatre where shows are staged. A good rainy-afternoon stop near the Marina.

Sicily guidePuppet museum

Little theatres with big stories

Some of Italy's best nights out are in tiny, perfect theatres in small towns.

The condominio theatres of central Italy

World Heritage since 2026

Inscribed July 2026

Ten 18th- and 19th-century theatres across Emilia-Romagna, Marche and Umbria, paid for by groups of families who each owned a box. The ten include the Pergolesi in Jesi, the Ventidio Basso in Ascoli Piceno, the Lauro Rossi in Macerata, Spoleto's Teatro Nuovo and the tiny Teatro Mariani in Sant'Agata Feltria.

Marche guide

Teatro della Concordia

Monte Castello di Vibio, Umbria

99 seats

Built in 1808 by nine local families, with just 99 seats, it calls itself the smallest theatre in the world. The painted ceiling and miniature boxes are pure delight.

Umbria guide

Teatro Bibiena

Mantua, Lombardy

Opened 1769

A bell-shaped theatre of tiered wooden boxes opened in 1769. A few weeks later, on 16 January 1770, a 13-year-old Mozart gave a concert here.

Lombardy guide

Teatro Farnese

Parma, Emilia-Romagna

Built 1618

A vast wooden theatre built in 1618 inside the Pilotta palace, one of the first with a movable stage. Badly bombed in 1944 and rebuilt; it is now part of the Pilotta museum visit.

Emilia-Romagna guideComplesso della Pilotta

Ca' Goldoni

Venice, Veneto

Venice

The Gothic birthplace of Carlo Goldoni, the playwright who swapped commedia's masks for real Venetian characters. A small museum with an 18th-century puppet theatre.

Veneto guideCa' Goldoni

Good to know

  • Most opera houses sell discounted last-minute or gallery seats on the day of the performance; ask at the box office.
  • Book big houses through the official sites linked here; resale sites often charge double.
  • In ancient theatres the stone gets hard after an hour: bring or rent a cushion, and a jacket for after dark.
  • Day tours of the opera houses are often cheaper and easier than a performance, and you may catch a rehearsal.
