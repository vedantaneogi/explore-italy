Experiences  /  Scenic Italy

Cliffs, lakes, peaks and smoking volcanoes

Thousands of kilometres of coast, lakes in the shadow of the Alps, the Dolomites at sunset and three live volcanoes: Italy's landscapes are as good as its art.

The three sheer peaks of the Tre Cime di Lavaredo above a green alpine meadowPhoto: Daniele Bonaldo, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

It is easy to come to Italy for the cities and leave without seeing how wild and varied the country is. Within a day's travel you can swim off pink granite in Sardinia, walk a glacier in the Alps, smell the sulphur on Etna's flanks and watch wolves' tracks in the Abruzzo snow. More than a third of the country is mountainous, the coast runs for some 7,500 kilometres, and national and regional parks protect around a tenth of the land.

The best of it rewards a little effort: a dawn start for the Dolomites, a ferry rather than the coast road, the slow train instead of the motorway. Here are the coasts, lakes, mountains, volcanoes and countryside that we would plan a trip around, with links to the national parks and the scenic railways that cross them.

Coasts

Go in May, June or September for warm water without the August crush.

Amalfi Coast

Campania

Best: May-June, Sept

Lemon terraces and pastel towns clinging to cliffs above a deep blue sea. Skip the traffic by using the ferries between Salerno, Amalfi and Positano, and walk the Path of the Gods from Bomerano to Nocelle for the view the gods presumably enjoyed.

Campania guide

Cinque Terre

Liguria

National park since 1999

Five fishing villages linked by cliff paths, a coastal train and vineyards planted on terraces so steep they need little monorails. Walk the coastal Blue Path early in the day, swim off the rocks at Manarola and end with focaccia and a glass of Sciacchetrà.

Liguria guideCinque Terre National Park

Costa Smeralda and La Maddalena

North-east Sardinia

Best: June, September

Water in every shade of emerald and turquoise around wind-carved granite. Beyond the glamour of Porto Cervo, take a boat day trip through the islands of the Maddalena archipelago national park, swimming off empty coves.

Sardinia guideLa Maddalena park

Salento

Puglia

Best: June, September

The heel of Italy has two seas: Adriatic cliffs and sea caves around Otranto, Torre Sant'Andrea and the Grotta della Poesia, and long Caribbean-pale sand on the Ionian side. Base yourself in baroque Lecce or a masseria among the olive trees.

Puglia guide

Tropea and Capo Vaticano

Calabria

Stromboli on the horizon

A clifftop town above white sand, with the church of Santa Maria dell'Isola on its own rock just offshore. On clear evenings you can see Stromboli smoking on the horizon. Eat the sweet red onions of Tropea, raw in salad or in jam.

Calabria guide

Polignano a Mare

Puglia

40 min from Bari

A whitewashed old town built right on the edge of limestone cliffs, with a tiny pebble cove between them. Swim at Lama Monachile in the early morning before the day-trippers arrive.

Puglia guide

Lakes

The great lakes at the foot of the Alps have soft light, grand villas and ferries that make the car unnecessary.

Lake Como

Lombardy

Ferries link every town

A deep, narrow lake ringed by mountains, villas and gardens. Base yourself in Bellagio or Varenna, use the ferries, and visit Villa del Balbianello on its wooded promontory. The Greenway path links lakeside villages on foot.

Lombardy guideVilla del Balbianello

Lake Garda

Lombardy, Veneto and Trentino

Italy's largest lake

Italy's largest lake is two lakes in one: Mediterranean in the south, with olive groves and Sirmione's castle and Roman villa; fjord-like in the north, where the afternoon wind makes Riva and Torbole a windsurfing paradise. Ride the cable car from Malcesine up Monte Baldo.

Veneto guide

Lake Maggiore

Piedmont and Lombardy

Gardens open spring to autumn

Belle Epoque hotels in Stresa and the Borromean Islands floating offshore: Isola Bella's terraced Baroque garden with white peacocks, Isola Madre's botanical rarities and the fishermen's island in between, where you can eat lake fish by the water.

Piedmont guide

Lake Orta

Piedmont

1h30 from Milan

Small, quiet and enchanting. Orta San Giulio's piazza looks out to the island of San Giulio, where a path called the Way of Silence circles a Benedictine convent. Above town, the chapels of the Sacro Monte are on the World Heritage list.

Piedmont guide

Mountains and volcanoes

From the highest Alps to Europe's most active volcano.

The Dolomites

Trentino-Alto Adige and Veneto

Best: late June-September

Walk the loop around the Tre Cime di Lavaredo, ride up from Ortisei to the knife-edge meadow of Seceda, or sit by the emerald water of Lago di Braies at dawn. In summer the huts serve dumplings and strudel; in winter the Sella Ronda links four valleys on skis.

Trentino-Alto Adige guideDolomites UNESCO

Gran Paradiso

Valle d'Aosta and Piedmont

National park since 1922

Italy's first national park, founded in 1922 to save the Alpine ibex, which you will now see grazing calmly by the trail. Walk up Valnontey from Cogne to glacier views; the Gran Paradiso itself is the only 4,000-metre peak entirely in Italy.

Valle d'Aosta guideGran Paradiso National Park

Gran Sasso and Campo Imperatore

Abruzzo

Highest Apennine peak

The highest peak of the Apennines, Corno Grande at 2,912 metres, rises above Campo Imperatore, a vast grassy plateau with wild horses called Italy's 'little Tibet'. Drive on to the castle of Rocca Calascio for sunset.

Abruzzo guideGran Sasso park

Mount Etna

Sicily

UNESCO-listed 2013

Europe's highest active volcano, over 3,300 metres and always changing. Take the cable car from Rifugio Sapienza and a 4x4 higher still, walk among old craters and lava flows, then taste the mineral wines made on its slopes. Summit areas require an authorised guide.

Sicily guideEtna Park

Vesuvius

Campania

1,281 metres

The volcano that buried Pompeii is quiet for now. A gravel path climbs from the car park to the rim of the crater, with the Bay of Naples spread out below. Tickets for the crater trail are sold online only, and the trail closes in bad weather.

Campania guideVesuvius National Park

Stromboli

Aeolian Islands, Sicily

Erupting continuously

A perfect cone in the sea that has been erupting in small bursts for thousands of years. Watch the fireworks from a boat below the Sciara del Fuoco after dark, or join a licensed guide for a trek when access is open; rules change with the volcano's mood.

Sicily guide

Countryside

Farmed landscapes that are as beautiful as any wilderness.

Val d'Orcia

Tuscany

Best: April-June, October

Green velvet in spring, gold in summer, with cypress avenues leading to lonely farmhouses. Soak in the hot springs of Bagno Vignoni and taste Brunello in Montalcino. The famous zigzag cypress road is near Monticchiello; go at first light for mist in the valleys.

Tuscany guide

Langhe

Piedmont

Best: October

Vine-striped hills crowned with castles and villages: Barolo, La Morra, Barbaresco, Serralunga. Come in autumn for the harvest colours and white truffles, and in spring when the hazelnut groves are green.

Piedmont guide

Castelluccio and the Piano Grande

Umbria

Flowering: June-early July

A vast upland plain in the Monti Sibillini where lentil fields burst into a patchwork of red poppies, blue cornflowers and yellow mustard each summer. The village, damaged in the 2016 earthquake, is slowly coming back to life.

Umbria guideMonti Sibillini park

National parks and scenic railways

Italy has around two dozen national parks, plus hundreds of regional reserves. These are some of the best, followed by the train rides that show off the scenery best.

Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise National Park

Abruzzo

Beech forests home to wolves, chamois and the rare Marsican brown bear. Base in Pescasseroli or Civitella Alfedena and walk with a local guide at dawn.

Abruzzo guidePark website

Stelvio National Park

Lombardy and Trentino-Alto Adige

Glaciers, larch forests and the Stelvio Pass, whose 48 hairpin bends on the Prato side are a cyclist's pilgrimage.

Lombardy guidePark website

Gran Paradiso National Park

Valle d'Aosta and Piedmont

Ibex, chamois and marmots, and well-marked paths between alpine huts.

Valle d'Aosta guidePark website

Gargano National Park

Puglia

The spur of the boot: the ancient beech woods of the Foresta Umbra, white cliffs, sea stacks and the Tremiti islands offshore.

Puglia guidePark website

Pollino National Park

Basilicata and Calabria

Italy's largest national park, with rare Bosnian pines clinging to the ridges, canyoning in the Raganello gorges and rafting on the Lao river.

Basilicata guidePark guide

Cinque Terre and Vesuvius national parks

Liguria and Campania

Two small, much-visited parks where entry tickets fund path repairs and conservation.

Cinque Terre park

Bernina Express

Tirano to St Moritz and Chur

About 2h15 to St Moritz

Red Swiss trains climb from the vineyards of Tirano in Lombardy over the Bernina Pass and its glaciers, crossing 196 bridges and 55 tunnels including the spiral viaduct at Brusio. The line is a World Heritage site; reserve panoramic seats ahead.

Lombardy guideBook the Bernina Express

Vigezzina-Centovalli railway

Domodossola to Locarno

About 2 hours

A narrow-gauge line through the Val Vigezzo, the 'valley of painters', and the Swiss Centovalli, over gorges and viaducts. Gorgeous in October, when the chestnut woods turn gold.

Piedmont guideVigezzina-Centovalli

Circumetnea railway

Around Mount Etna, Sicily

A slow single-track line circling Etna through lava fields, pistachio groves in Bronte and hill towns, from Catania to Riposto.

Sicily guideCircumetnea

Good to know

  • Dolomite cable cars and huts mostly open from mid-June to mid-September; check before booking an early or late trip.
  • On popular coasts, use ferries and trains rather than a car: parking in Positano or the Cinque Terre is scarce and expensive.
  • Volcano access changes with activity and weather; check the park sites the day before you go.
  • Some of the most famous beaches and trails now need a booking or entry fee in summer, so check ahead.
  • Carry water and a layer: Apennine and Alpine weather changes fast, even in July.
