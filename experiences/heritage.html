The world's richest heritage list, room by room

Sixty-two World Heritage sites, twenty living traditions and some of the planet's greatest museums - here is how to see the best of them without the queues.

The Baptistery, Cathedral and Leaning Tower on the lawns of Pisa's Piazza del DuomoPhoto: kallerna, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons

In this guide

  1. The ancient world
  2. Medieval and Renaissance cities

No country has more places on UNESCO's World Heritage List than Italy. The count reached 62 in July 2026, when the committee meeting in Busan added ten 18th- and 19th-century theatres in Marche, Umbria and Emilia-Romagna, from Jesi's Pergolesi to Spoleto's Teatro Nuovo, built and run by townspeople who pooled their money for a box each. It is a fitting addition: so much of Italy's heritage was paid for by ordinary towns that cared about looking good.

The list is only the start. Italy also has twenty traditions on UNESCO's intangible heritage list, from Neapolitan pizza-making to opera singing and, since December 2025, Italian home cooking itself. Below we sort the sites into ancient worlds, great cities and landscapes, then list the living heritage you can see, hear and taste, and the big museums worth booking before you fly.

The ancient world

Greek temples, Etruscan tombs, Roman roads and island towers older than Rome itself. Go early, carry water and give each site a full morning.

Historic centre of Rome

Lazio, inscribed 1980

Inscribed 1980, extended 1990

The Forum, the Palatine and the Colosseum sit in one walkable valley, but the listing covers far more: the Pantheon's open oculus, Trajan's Market, Baroque piazzas and the Vatican's properties inside the city. Walk the Forum at opening, before the heat bounces off the marble.

Lazio guideBook Colosseum & Forum

Pompeii, Herculaneum and Oplontis

Campania, buried by Vesuvius in AD 79

Inscribed 1997

Pompeii is a whole Roman town of bakeries, bars and frescoed bedrooms; Herculaneum is smaller and better preserved, with carbonised wooden beams and upper floors. Oplontis, at Torre Annunziata, is one sumptuous villa. New houses keep opening at Pompeii, so even repeat visitors find something fresh.

Campania guidePompeii official site

Valley of the Temples

Agrigento, Sicily

Inscribed 1997

A ridge of honey-coloured Doric temples above almond groves and the sea, the finest outside Greece. The Temple of Concordia, from the 5th century BC, stands almost whole. Come for the late opening on summer evenings, when the stone glows and the crowds thin.

Sicily guidePark official site

Su Nuraxi di Barumini

Sardinia's Bronze Age fortress

Inscribed 1997

Sardinia has thousands of nuraghi, the cone-shaped stone towers of the Nuragic people, and Barumini is the grandest: a central tower and bastions wrapped in a honeycomb village, built from around 1600 BC. Guided visits take you inside the dark, cool passages.

Sardinia guideVisit Barumini

Etruscan necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia

Lazio, north of Rome

Inscribed 2004

Cerveteri's Banditaccia is a city of the dead, with grassy mound tombs laid out along streets. At Tarquinia you step down into painted chambers where Etruscans dance, dive, feast and wrestle in colours that still feel alive after 2,500 years.

Lazio guide

Via Appia, Regina Viarum

Rome to Brindisi, listed 2024

Inscribed 2024

The Queen of Roads became Italy's 60th World Heritage site in 2024. Rent a bike on a Sunday, when the stretch outside Rome is quieter, and ride the original basalt paving past tombs, catacombs and umbrella pines; the listing follows the road all the way to the Adriatic.

Lazio guideAppia Antica park

Domus de Janas

Sardinia, listed 2025

Inscribed 2025

Prehistoric tombs cut into rock across Sardinia, some carved with bull horns and false doors to mirror the houses of the living. Their name means 'houses of the fairies'. Twenty-six sites make up the 2025 listing, from Sant'Andrea Priu to Montessu and the Elephant Rock, many on quiet hillsides you may have to yourself.

Sardinia guide

Villa Romana del Casale

Piazza Armerina, Sicily

Inscribed 1997

A late Roman country villa with some 3,500 square metres of floor mosaics: hunting scenes, sea monsters, exotic animals being loaded onto ships and the famous girls in 'bikinis' doing exercises. Walkways let you look down on the floors room by room.

Sicily guideVilla official site

Medieval and Renaissance cities

Whole historic centres are on the list, so the heritage here is the walk to dinner as much as the famous rooms.

Florence

Tuscany, cradle of the Renaissance

Inscribed 1982

Brunelleschi's dome, Giotto's bell tower, Michelangelo's David and the Uffizi's Botticellis, all within a 20-minute walk. Climb to San Miniato al Monte at sunset for the view across the river, then drift back down through the Oltrarno's workshops.

Tuscany guideUffizi Galleries

Venice and its lagoon

Veneto

Inscribed 1987

The city and the lagoon are listed together, which is the clue: take the vaporetto out to Torcello's Byzantine mosaics and Burano's painted houses as well as the Doge's Palace. Day-trippers pay an access fee on peak days, so check before you go.

Veneto guideDoge's Palace

Siena

Tuscany, Gothic city of the Palio

Inscribed 1995

The shell-shaped Piazza del Campo tilts toward the Palazzo Pubblico, where Lorenzetti's frescoes of good and bad government still make their point. Climb the Torre del Mangia, then lose yourself in the contrade, each with its own fountain and flag.

Tuscany guide

Ravenna's early Christian monuments

Emilia-Romagna

Inscribed 1996

Eight buildings from the 5th and 6th centuries hold the most dazzling mosaics in Europe. In San Vitale, Emperor Justinian and Empress Theodora glitter in gold; in the tiny Mausoleum of Galla Placidia a blue sky full of stars arches over your head.

Emilia-Romagna guideBook the mosaics

Assisi

Umbria, the town of St Francis

Inscribed 2000

The double basilica of St Francis, with Giotto's and Cimabue's frescoes, rises on a buttressed platform above the Umbrian plain. Stay the night: once the coach groups leave, the pink stone lanes go quiet and the bells carry across the valley.

Umbria guide

The Sassi of Matera

Basilicata

Inscribed 1993

Two ravines of cave houses and rock-cut churches, inhabited for thousands of years, emptied in the 1950s and now reborn as hotels, restaurants and museums. Walk the rim of the gorge opposite at dusk to watch the lights come on.

Basilicata guide

Padua's fresco cycles

Veneto, listed 2021

Inscribed 2021

Giotto's Scrovegni Chapel is the star: a blue-vaulted box of scenes so human they changed Western painting. The listing adds seven more 14th-century fresco cycles around the city. Chapel visits are short and timed, after a few minutes in a climate-control room.

Veneto guideBook the Scrovegni Chapel

The porticoes of Bologna

Emilia-Romagna, listed 2021

Inscribed 2021

Around 62 kilometres of covered arcades keep Bologna dry in winter and shaded in summer. The longest runs nearly four kilometres and 666 arches up to the sanctuary of San Luca: a pilgrimage, a workout and the best view of the city.

Emilia-Romagna guide

Landscapes and wild places

Some of Italy's listings are not buildings at all, but mountains, islands and hills shaped by centuries of farming.

The Dolomites

Trentino-Alto Adige, Veneto and Friuli

Inscribed 2009

Nine separate mountain groups of pale, jagged limestone that turn pink at sunset, a glow the Ladin people call enrosadira. Hut-to-hut walking here is some of the best in Europe, with strudel and speck waiting at every rifugio.

Trentino-Alto Adige guideDolomites UNESCO Foundation

Aeolian Islands

Sicily

Inscribed 2000

Seven volcanic islands off Sicily's north coast, listed for what they teach about how volcanoes behave. Stromboli still throws lava into the night sky; Vulcano has warm mud and fumaroles; Salina grows capers and Malvasia wine between two extinct cones.

Sicily guide

Val d'Orcia

Tuscany

Inscribed 2004

The rolling, cypress-dotted hills that appear on half the postcards of Tuscany were designed: Renaissance landowners laid out farms, roads and villages to look exactly like this. Base yourself in Pienza or San Quirico and drive the back roads at golden hour.

Tuscany guide

Portovenere, Cinque Terre and the islands

Liguria

Inscribed 1997

Terraced vineyards held up by dry-stone walls, five fishing villages stacked above the sea, and Portovenere's striped church on its rocky point. Walk between villages in spring or autumn, and take the boat back as the sun drops.

Liguria guide

Prosecco hills of Conegliano and Valdobbiadene

Veneto, listed 2019

Inscribed 2019

Steep 'hogback' ridges striped with vines that are still picked by hand. Drive the Strada del Prosecco, stop at a family cantina for a glass, and look for the self-service osterie senza oste, honesty-box huts where you pour your own.

Veneto guide

Langhe-Roero and Monferrato

Piedmont, listed 2014

Inscribed 2014

The vineyard landscape that gives us Barolo, Barbaresco and Moscato: hilltop castles, wine villages and long rows of Nebbiolo that blaze yellow and red in October, just as truffle season begins in Alba.

Piedmont guide

Living heritage

UNESCO's intangible list honours skills and rituals rather than places. Italy has twenty entries, several shared with other countries. These are the ones you can most easily see, hear or taste.

Italian cooking

Inscribed December 2025

Italy-wide

The first time a whole national cuisine has been recognised as a practice: recipes handed down, seasonal shopping, wasting nothing and sitting down together. You join in every time you linger over Sunday lunch.

The art of the Neapolitan pizzaiuolo

Inscribed 2017

Naples

The dough-spinning, wood-fire skills of Naples' pizza makers. Watch them at work in the old centre, and eat your Margherita folded 'a portafoglio' on the street.

Campania guide

Opera singing in Italy

Inscribed 2023

Italy-wide

The technique of projecting the voice without amplification, passed from teacher to student since the 1600s. Hear it at its purest in a small historic theatre rather than a giant arena.

Violin-making in Cremona

Inscribed 2012

Cremona

Stradivari and Guarneri worked here, and around 150 workshops still build instruments by hand. At the Museo del Violino you can hear instruments from the collection, Stradivaris included, played in short auditorium recitals.

Lombardy guideMuseo del Violino

Canto a tenore

Inscribed 2008

Sardinia

Four-man shepherds' polyphony from inland Sardinia, with a deep throat-sung bass that sounds like a lowing animal. Listen for it in bars and at festas around Orgosolo, Bitti and Oliena.

Sardinia guide

Opera dei Pupi

Inscribed 2008

Palermo and Catania

Sicilian marionette theatre with armoured knights battling Saracens. Palermo still has small family companies performing in tiny theatres near the Massimo.

Sicily guide

Big shoulder-borne processional structures

Inscribed 2013

Nola, Viterbo, Palmi, Sassari

Towers carried through the streets by hundreds of bearers: the Gigli of Nola, the Macchina di Santa Rosa in Viterbo, the Varia of Palmi and the Candelieri of Sassari.

Truffle hunting and extraction

Inscribed 2021

Piedmont, Umbria, Marche

The knowledge shared between a trifolao and their dog in the autumn woods. Several farms in Piedmont, Umbria and Le Marche take visitors on a dawn hunt.

Head-trained bush vines of Pantelleria

Inscribed 2014

Pantelleria

Zibibbo vines grown low in hollows to escape the wind on this African-facing island, then dried into the amber passito di Pantelleria.

Sicily guide

The Mediterranean diet

Inscribed 2013, shared

Cilento

Olive oil, legumes, vegetables and conviviality, with the Cilento village of Pollica as Italy's reference community.

Campania guide

Celestinian Forgiveness

Inscribed 2019

L'Aquila

L'Aquila's Perdonanza, a jubilee founded by Pope Celestine V in 1294, celebrated every 28-29 August with a historical procession and the opening of the Holy Door of Santa Maria di Collemaggio.

Abruzzo guide

Transhumance and dry-stone walling

Shared inscriptions

Apennines and Alps

Two rural skills that shaped Italy's hills: the seasonal moving of flocks along ancient drove roads, still seen in Molise and Abruzzo, and the craft of building walls without mortar.

Great museums and how to book them

For the big names, booking a timed slot on the official site is not optional in high season. These links go straight to the source, with no reseller mark-up.

Uffizi Galleries

Florence

Botticelli's Birth of Venus, Leonardo, Caravaggio and a corridor of Roman statues. Book a timed slot and come at opening or in the last two hours.

Tuscany guideBook Uffizi tickets

Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel

Vatican City

Seven kilometres of galleries ending under Michelangelo's ceiling. Closed most Sundays; the free last Sunday of the month is the busiest day of all.

Lazio guideBook Vatican tickets

Colosseum, Roman Forum and Palatine

Rome

One combined ticket with a timed Colosseum entry. Book the version with the arena floor or underground if you want to see where the gladiators waited.

Lazio guideOfficial ticketing

Pompeii archaeological park

Campania

Tickets carry the visitor's name and there is a daily cap of 20,000, so book ahead in summer. Only small bags are allowed in.

Campania guideTickets and opening hours

Leonardo's Last Supper

Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan

Fifteen minutes in small groups. Tickets are released three months at a time and the good slots go within hours; closed Mondays.

Lombardy guideBooking information

Galleria dell'Accademia

Florence

Michelangelo's David and his unfinished Prisoners, which seem to be pulling themselves out of the marble. Quick to see if you book the first slot of the day.

Tuscany guideAccademia official site

Galleria Borghese

Rome

Bernini's marble sculptures and Caravaggio's paintings in a cardinal's villa. Entry is by reservation only, in fixed two-hour slots, so plan this one first.

National Archaeological Museum of Naples (MANN)

Naples

Where the best frescoes, mosaics and bronzes from Pompeii and Herculaneum ended up, including the Farnese marbles and the Secret Cabinet. Go before or after the sites, not on the same day.

Campania guideMANN official site

Museo Egizio

Turin

The oldest museum in the world devoted entirely to ancient Egypt, with colossal statues, papyri and the intact grave goods of the architect Kha. Allow three hours.

Piedmont guideMuseo Egizio

Pinacoteca di Brera

Milan

Mantegna's foreshortened Dead Christ, Raphael, Piero della Francesca and Hayez's The Kiss, in a handsome old palazzo in the arty Brera quarter.

Lombardy guideBrera official site

Doge's Palace

Venice

Tintoretto's vast Paradise, the Bridge of Sighs and the prisons. Book the Secret Itineraries tour for the hidden offices and Casanova's cell.

Veneto guideDoge's Palace tickets

Good to know

  • State museums and archaeological sites are free on the first Sunday of each month: wonderful for locals, very crowded for visitors.
  • Book on the official sites linked here; resellers charge more for exactly the same timed entry.
  • Many smaller listed sites close one day a week, usually Monday, so check before driving out.
  • Carry a scarf or light layer to cover shoulders and knees in churches, including the Vatican and Assisi.
  • In August, visit the big archaeological sites at opening or in the late afternoon: there is little shade at Pompeii or Agrigento.
